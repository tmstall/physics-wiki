## 1. Punchy Title & One-Sentence Hook

**Spacetime as a Box of Indivisible Cells**

Chamseddine and Mukhanov throw out the idea that space is infinitely divisible, rebuild geometry from a finite kit of Planck-sized, structureless cells connected only by local rotations — and prove that ordinary curved-spacetime geometry falls back out exactly, term for term, the moment you let the cells shrink to zero.

## 2. Big-Picture Context

General relativity assumes spacetime is a smooth, infinitely-divisible manifold — you can always ask "what's at the point exactly halfway between these two points?" and get a well-defined answer. That assumption is what quantum gravity keeps breaking. Naive perturbative attempts to quantize the gravitational field predict that quantum fluctuations of the metric become of order 1 — not small corrections, but complete scrambling — at distances near the Planck length (~10⁻³⁵ m). At that point, "smooth manifold" stops being a meaningful description of anything, the same way asking for sub-pixel detail on a display that has no more pixels to give isn't really a question about the image anymore. This has motivated a decades-long family of attempts to build gravity on a fundamentally discrete foundation instead of trying to patch a continuum theory after the fact.

Prior discretization attempts each carry specific technical baggage the authors call out directly. Lattice gauge theory (the Wilson-loop machinery behind lattice QCD) puts group-valued variables on the links of a fixed Cartesian grid — but it's unclear how to even set that up in curved or angular coordinates, let alone a genuinely curved spacetime, since the grid itself assumes a flat background to be regular on. Regge calculus (simplicial spacetime, building geometry from flat triangulated chunks glued along their faces) has the opposite problem: it *presupposes* a notion of distance between neighboring vertices before you've built any geometry — which the authors argue directly contradicts the goal of starting from truly structureless, universal building blocks — and there's no guaranteed unique way for a Regge triangulation to converge to a specific continuous manifold as you refine it.

This paper's move is to build the discreteness in from the ground floor rather than discretizing GR's continuum formulas after the fact. Cells are labeled purely by integer tuples — no coordinates, no assumed distances. Each cell only knows how to hand off local rotation information to its immediate neighbors. Metric, torsion, and curvature are then *derived* from that local data, not assumed. The central technical payoff, and the one this analysis spends most of its time on, is a proof that letting the elementary cell volume shrink to zero exactly reproduces the ordinary machinery of differentiable-manifold geometry — the metric, the Riemann tensor, and the Einstein–Hilbert action all fall out unchanged.

**Paper Type & Stakes:** this is a formal, mathematical-physics construction paper — no experiment, no new particle, no new observable claimed. It proposes (and partially proves) a specific discretization scheme for spacetime geometry engineered to dodge known technical failures of earlier discretization schemes, with the *eventual* payoff — a natural UV cutoff, a language for quantized black holes, an emergent expanding universe — explicitly promised for "future publications," not delivered here.

**Prior Belief Check:** this aligns with, rather than contradicts or overturns, an established (if not mainstream-dominant) research program: recasting gravity as a gauge theory of local frames and pursuing spacetime discreteness as a route toward quantum gravity. Recasting gravity via vielbeins and a spin connection long predates this paper (Kibble's 1961 gauge-theoretic formulation of gravity is cited directly as an ancestor). Nothing here is surprising or field-shaking to a quantum-gravity specialist — it's an incremental, confirmatory-in-spirit methodological contribution: a new discretization mechanism explicitly targeted at a known, named technical obstacle (the "Leibniz rule problem," below), not a new physical claim about the universe.

**Replication & Convergence Note:** single-group result — two authors, no outside confirmation. The only known follow-up as of this analysis is a 2022 *European Physical Journal C* paper (Chamseddine, Malaeb & Najem) that shares two of its three authors with this one and numerically tests the 2D continuum-limit claim on a discretized two-sphere. That's a same-lineage extension, not independent replication. Independent confirmation would look like a group with no author overlap either (a) reproducing the torsion-free/curvature construction in 3D or 4D and checking it against a known exact solution (e.g. Schwarzschild), or (b) running this scheme head-to-head against Regge calculus or causal dynamical triangulation on an identical benchmark geometry, to see whether the claimed fix for the Leibniz-rule problem actually changes numerical behavior in practice, not just the formal bookkeeping.

## 3. Necessary Background Crash-Course

**Why physicists suspect space isn't infinitely divisible.** As above: perturbative quantum gravity predicts O(1) metric fluctuations at the Planck scale, meaning the "smooth manifold" picture likely stops applying there.

> **Analogy:** think of ordinary GR's continuum as an addressing scheme that assumes infinitely fine-grained memory — any real-numbered location is a legitimate, independently meaningful address. Quantum gravity's Planck-scale breakdown is like discovering the hardware underneath doesn't actually support that: below a cache line's granularity, "the value at byte 3 of this line, considered on its own" isn't a separately meaningful quantity to the caching system — only whole-line fetches exist. The paper's postulate is that physical space works the same way below the Planck volume: points inside one elementary cell are not separately, physically addressable.
>
> **Breaks when:** you ask what's *underneath* the cache line. In real hardware there's plenty of finer structure below a cache line — individual bytes, transistors, physical silicon. The whole point of this paper's postulate is the opposite: there is nothing finer beneath the elementary cell. The discreteness is proposed as fundamental, not an engineering convenience sitting on top of a continuous substrate.

**Elementary cells and displacement operators.** A cell isn't a point in a pre-existing space — it's just an integer label **n** = (n¹, ..., n^d). The only structure handed to a cell at the start is a rule for stepping to each of its 2d immediate neighbors: a displacement operator **E**_β that bumps the β-th integer coordinate up by one (and its inverse, which steps back down). Nothing about distance, angle, or curvature is assumed yet — that all gets built up from what's called the *soldering form*, next.

**Vielbeins as unit converters.** The vielbein turns an abstract "one integer step in direction α" into an actual local physical displacement with a length and orientation — the *soldering form* ē is the conversion factor.

> **Analogy:** like a stride value in a strided memory-access pattern — the thing that converts "index step of 1" into "so many actual bytes forward." The difference here is that the stride can be a different value in every cell, and it can rotate as well as rescale, not just change magnitude.
>
> **Breaks when:** a memory stride is a single global constant chosen once for a whole array; the vielbein here is cell-local and dynamical — it's exactly the thing whose cell-to-cell variation *is* the geometry (curved space = a stride/orientation that changes from cell to cell in a way that can't be undone by any single global relabeling).

**Spin connection, parallel transport, and torsion.** Moving a direction-vector from one cell to its neighbor means re-expressing it in the neighbor's own local frame — the *spin connection* Ω_β is the local rotation matrix that does the re-expressing. "Torsion-free" is the requirement that stepping +1 in direction α then +1 in direction β lands you (physically) in the same place as stepping β then α — order shouldn't matter.

> **Analogy:** picture a distributed system where every node stores only a *local* relative transform to its immediate neighbors — no global coordinate, no global clock — the way a pose-graph SLAM system or a set of vector clocks only tracks local relative updates. Curvature, in this framework, is exactly a **loop-closure residual**: walk the loop α→β→(–α)→(–β) around one elementary plaquette by composing the four local rotations, and check whether you land back on the identity transform. If you don't, that mismatch — not an error to be fixed, but the physical content itself — is curvature.
>
> **Breaks when:** in SLAM or distributed-clock reconciliation, a nonzero loop-closure residual signals a *measurement fault* you want to drive to zero. Here it's the reverse: zero residual everywhere means flat, gravity-free space. A "perfectly clean" instrument in this analogy would report exactly the boring case; real gravitating spacetime is expected to have that residual almost everywhere.

**Central analogy for this paper:** Quantized memory cells; curvature as loop-closure residue.

## 4. Core Technical Explanation

**Setting up the local frame.** Each cell defines a *tangent operator*

$$\mathbf{e}_{\alpha}(n) \equiv \tfrac{1}{2}\left(\mathbf{E}_{\alpha}(n) - \mathbf{E}_{\alpha}^{-1}(n)\right)$$

**Symbol definitions:** $\mathbf{e}_{\alpha}$ : the discrete stand-in for a coordinate basis vector/partial derivative in direction α · $\mathbf{E}_{\alpha}$, $\mathbf{E}_{\alpha}^{-1}$ : forward and backward integer-step operators in direction α.

**What this actually means:** this is a symmetric (centered) finite difference — step forward, step backward, average — built purely from integer-label shifts, with no assumed metric yet. It's the discrete-gravity analog of replacing a derivative with a centered finite-difference stencil in numerical computing, except the "grid" here has no coordinates to speak of, only adjacency.

The vielbein converts this into an orthonormal local frame, $\mathbf{v}_a(n) = \bar{e}_a^{\alpha}(n)\,\mathbf{e}_{\alpha}(n)$, and the metric is simply the dot product of two tangent operators: $g_{\alpha\beta}(n) \equiv \mathbf{e}_{\alpha}(n)\bullet\mathbf{e}_{\beta}(n) = e_{\alpha}^{a}(n)e_{\beta}^{b}(n)\delta_{ab}$ — distance is a *derived* quantity, read off from the soldering forms, never an input.

**Parallel transport and the torsion-free master equation.** The spin connection is written as a group element, $\varOmega_{\beta}(n) = e^{\omega_{\beta}^{cd}(n) J_{cd}}$ (an exponentiated rotation generator — the discrete stand-in for "infinitesimal rotation"), and it fixes how a tangent operator gets re-expressed after being carried from a neighboring cell back to n:

$$\mathbf{e}_{\alpha}^{\text{p.t.}}(n+1_{\beta}\rightarrow n) = \mathbf{e}_{\alpha}(n) + \Gamma_{\alpha\beta}^{\gamma}(n)\,\mathbf{e}_{\gamma}(n)$$

**Symbol definitions:** $\Gamma_{\alpha\beta}^{\gamma}$ : the discrete affine connection, the cell-based stand-in for GR's Christoffel symbols · $\mathbf{e}_{\alpha}^{\text{p.t.}}$ : the tangent operator after being parallel-transported from the neighboring cell.

**What this actually means:** this says "carrying a direction one hop over and correcting for the local rotation is the same operation as adding a correction term built from the connection" — precisely the discrete version of what a Christoffel symbol does in continuum GR when you differentiate a vector field on a curved manifold.

Requiring **no torsion** — that the connection be symmetric in its lower indices, $\Gamma_{\alpha\beta}^{\gamma}=\Gamma_{\beta\alpha}^{\gamma}$ — produces the paper's master equation:

$$(\varOmega_{\beta}(n))_{a}^{\ b}\,e_{b\alpha}(n+1_{\beta}) - e_{a\alpha}(n) \;=\; (\varOmega_{\alpha}(n))_{a}^{\ b}\,e_{b\beta}(n+1_{\alpha}) - e_{a\beta}(n)$$

**Symbol definitions:** $\varOmega_{\alpha}, \varOmega_{\beta}$ : the spin connections in the two directions · $e_{a\alpha}$ : the soldering-form components (vielbeins) at the labeled cells.

**What this actually means:** this single condition is solvable *entirely* in terms of the soldering forms sitting in cell n and its two forward neighbors — meaning the spin connection isn't an independent, freely-specifiable field the way it can be in continuum Einstein–Cartan gravity with torsion. It's fully determined by the local geometry data already assigned to neighboring cells. This is also the mechanism the authors use to sidestep what they call the **"Leibniz rule problem"**: ordinary finite differences don't obey the product rule the way continuum derivatives do (∂(fg) ≠ (∂f)g + f(∂g) exactly, on a grid), which is exactly why naively discretizing GR's continuum formulas term-by-term has historically produced awkward, inconsistent results. This paper never discretizes a continuum formula in the first place — it builds the whole structure from the group-valued spin connection, where no product rule was ever needed to begin with.

**Curvature from a loop.** Curvature is defined by composing the four spin connections around one elementary plaquette in the α–β plane:

$$\mathcal{R}_{\alpha\beta}(n) = \varOmega_{\alpha}(n+1_{\beta})\,\varOmega_{\beta}(n)\,\varOmega_{\alpha}^{-1}(n)\,\varOmega_{\beta}^{-1}(n+1_{\alpha})$$

**Symbol definitions:** $\mathcal{R}_{\alpha\beta}(n)$ : the discrete curvature two-form at cell n, in the α–β plane.

**What this actually means:** walk the loop — forward in β, forward in α, back in β, back in α — composing the local rotation at each leg. If the four rotations exactly cancel, $\mathcal{R}_{\alpha\beta}=\mathbb{1}$ (identity) and the space is locally flat at that plaquette. Any leftover rotation *is* curvature — structurally identical to a Wilson-loop plaquette variable in lattice gauge theory, and mathematically identical to a loop-closure check in a pose-graph or distributed-consistency system (the central analogy above). The paper shows this reduces to the ordinary Riemann tensor $R_{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta}$ in the continuum limit.

**The continuum limit.** As the elementary volume shrinks (ε→0): the tangent operators $\mathbf{e}_\alpha$ become ordinary partial derivatives $\partial/\partial x^\alpha$; the affine connection $\Gamma_{\alpha\beta}^\gamma$ becomes the Christoffel symbols; the plaquette curvature becomes the Riemann tensor; and — the headline mathematical result — the discrete action functional converges to the ordinary Einstein–Hilbert action. Nothing about standard differential geometry is lost; it's recovered exactly, not approximately, in this limit.

**Worked numerical example — reading a curvature number off a plaquette.** The paper's own explicit two-dimensional (SO(2)) worked example, from a discretized flat Euclidean plane with three neighboring cells and a diagonal soldering form, reduces the master torsion-free equation to two coupled scalar equations for the two local spin-connection angles, and gives the resulting scalar curvature as

$$R^{12}_{\ \ 12}(n) = 2\sin\!\left[\tfrac{1}{2}\Big(\omega_2(n_1{+}1,n_2) - \omega_1(n_1,n_2{+}1) + \omega_1(n_1,n_2) - \omega_2(n_1,n_2)\Big)\right], \qquad R(n) = 2\,R^{12}_{\ \ 12}(n)\,e^{-2}(n)$$

**Symbol definitions:** $\omega_1, \omega_2$ : the two local spin-connection angles (in radians) at the labeled cells · $e(n)$ : the (here, isotropic) vielbein/scale factor at cell n.

**What this actually means:** the argument of the sine is exactly a signed sum of four neighboring spin-connection angles around a loop — a **deficit angle**, in the same spirit as the deficit angles used in Regge calculus to encode curvature at a vertex, except built here from gauge rotations rather than from cone angles in a triangulated mesh.

To make the mechanics concrete with a toy (analyst-constructed, not the paper's own reported figures) numeric pass: suppose the four local spin-connection angles around one plaquette happen to be $\omega_1(n_1,n_2)=0.10$, $\omega_2(n_1,n_2)=0.04$, $\omega_1(n_1,n_2{+}1)=0.07$, and $\omega_2(n_1{+}1,n_2)=0.09$ radians. The loop sum is $(0.09-0.07+0.10-0.04)/2 = 0.04$ radians, so $R^{12}_{\ \ 12} = 2\sin(0.04)\approx 0.080$. Taking a unit vielbein $e(n)=1$ gives $R(n)\approx 0.160$ — a small but nonzero curvature, exactly the size and sign you'd expect from a small residual imbalance in the four neighboring rotation angles, and it would vanish (flat space) if all four angles matched pairwise.

### Assumption Audit

> **Watch:** you might assume this paper discretizes GR the way lattice QCD discretizes a field *on top of* an already-given curved background — a numerical-methods trick layered onto standard geometry. **The paper actually does the opposite:** there is no background manifold underneath at all. The integer labels **n** are not coordinates on a pre-existing space; metric, distance, and curvature are entirely *reconstructed* from the cell-to-cell soldering-form and spin-connection data, a posteriori.

> **Watch:** you might assume the "cells are indistinguishable below this volume" postulate is a calculational regulator introduced for convenience — like a lattice spacing in QCD that you formally take to zero at the end and never really believe is physical. **The paper actually presents it as a physical claim** about nature, motivated by the expectation of O(1) metric fluctuations at sub-Planckian scales in perturbative quantum gravity — though it's worth being clear this motivation is not itself derived from a deeper theory here; it's an assumed starting postulate, not a proven consequence of one.

> **Watch:** you might assume the torsion-free condition is an optional simplifying choice, the way continuum GR could equally well be studied with torsion (Einstein–Cartan gravity) as without it. **The paper actually uses torsion-freeness as the load-bearing equation that fully *determines* the spin connection** from neighboring soldering forms — there's no independent spin-connection degree of freedom left over to set by hand, which is different in character from continuum Einstein–Cartan setups where torsion is often a genuinely free field.

> **Watch:** you might assume the continuum-limit proof means this is already a working alternative to full GR — including dynamics, matter, and quantization. **The paper only establishes kinematics:** the metric, connection, Riemann tensor, and the Einstein–Hilbert action functional are recovered as ε→0, in Euclidean signature. It does not derive or solve any discrete Einstein-equation analog, does not couple matter beyond a passing degrees-of-freedom remark, does not address Lorentzian/Minkowski signature, and does not quantize anything — all explicitly named by the authors as future work.

## 5. What's Genuinely New or Clever

**1. Sidestepping the Leibniz-rule problem by never invoking it.** Decades of discretization attempts have stumbled on the fact that finite differences don't obey the continuum product rule, which makes discretizing GR's derivative-heavy formulas term-by-term unreliable. This paper doesn't discretize continuum formulas at all — it builds the theory natively from group-valued spin connections and a torsion-free condition, so there's no product rule to fail in the first place. That's a genuine reframing of the problem, not a patched workaround.

**2. Exact, manifest local rotational gauge invariance at every step.** Unlike Regge calculus, where diffeomorphism invariance only re-emerges approximately as you refine the triangulation, this construction is exactly invariant under local rotations from the start, and the freedom to *choose* how cells are carved up is explicitly likened by the authors to a discrete stand-in for diffeomorphism invariance, reappearing cleanly in the continuum limit.

**Predictive Content Check.**

- **Falsifiable handle:** essentially none, in the ordinary "new observable" sense — and that should be stated plainly rather than smoothed over. This paper doesn't predict a new effect, particle, or measurable deviation from known physics; the closest thing to a checkable claim is the purely mathematical assertion that the ε→0 limit exactly reproduces the Riemann tensor and Einstein–Hilbert action, which the authors derive analytically here and which the 2022 follow-up paper checks numerically (successfully, on a 2-sphere test case). This is a reconstruction/relabeling of known continuum GR in discrete language, not a prediction of new phenomena.
- **Formalism load:** fires, and is judged load-bearing. The entire contribution of the paper *is* its specific algebraic machinery — the displacement operators, the torsion-free master equation, the plaquette curvature definition. Strip out that formalism and there is no separate physical story left standing; it is not decorative dressing on a simpler underlying claim.

## 6. Limitations & Open Questions

**Euclidean signature only.** The entire construction is carried out in d-dimensional Euclidean space; Lorentzian/Minkowski spacetime — needed for actual gravitational dynamics, causality, and time evolution — is not addressed here. **(A) Consensus** — this is the authors' own explicitly stated scope, not a criticism from outside. **(paper, stated scope)**

**No quantization yet.** Despite motivating language about "quanta of geometry," this is a classical (pre-quantum) discrete geometric structure; the cell degrees of freedom are not quantized into an actual quantum theory here. **(A) Consensus** — the authors themselves defer quantization, the noncommutative-geometry connection, and quantized-black-hole applications to "future publications." **(paper, conclusion)**

**No dynamics or matter coupling solved.** The paper establishes kinematics — how curvature and torsion are defined and how they reduce correctly to GR in the continuum limit — but does not derive or solve a discrete analog of the Einstein field equations, and only briefly remarks on degrees-of-freedom counting per cell (one scalar, two for a massless vector) rather than working through matter coupling. **(C) Speculative** — inferred from what was and wasn't present across two independent retrieval passes of the paper's text rather than from an explicit "we do not address dynamics" statement by the authors, so treat this specific gap as likely-but-not-certain. **(analyst inference)**

**The founding postulate is assumed, not derived.** Treating points within an elementary volume as physically indistinguishable is motivated by the expectation of O(1) metric fluctuations at sub-Planckian scales in perturbative quantum gravity, but that's a fairly specific read of what happens at the Planck scale — other programs (asymptotic safety's UV fixed point, LQG's dynamically-derived discreteness from quantizing geometry rather than postulating it) diagnose the same short-distance breakdown differently. **(B) Contested** — reasonable quantum-gravity researchers disagree about which short-distance diagnosis is correct; this is a live foundational disagreement in the field, not a settled point either side of. **(broader literature)**

**No independent testing yet.** As of this analysis, the only follow-up found is the 2022 EPJC paper sharing two of three authors with the original — no outside group appears to have adopted, extended, or critiqued the formalism, and there's no head-to-head comparison against other discrete-gravity approaches (Regge calculus, causal dynamical triangulations, spin foams) on a shared benchmark. **(A) Consensus that this gap exists** — this is a straightforward citation-search finding, not a matter of physics interpretation. **(analyst inference, from citation search conducted this session)**

## 7. Detailed Summary & Explanation

Chamseddine and Mukhanov start from a physical postulate: below some elementary volume — plausibly Planck-scale — points are not physically distinguishable by any experiment, because perturbative quantum gravity predicts the metric itself becomes wildly (O(1)) uncertain there. Rather than trying to patch continuum GR to accommodate this, they rebuild geometry from scratch out of cells that carry no internal structure at all. A cell is just an integer label; the only data attached to it is a set of displacement operators connecting it to its 2d nearest neighbors and a spin-connection element governing local rotations. From this bare-bones starting point, they define a tangent operator (a centered finite difference built from the displacement operators), a vielbein/soldering form (which converts abstract integer hops into real local lengths and orientations), and a metric (the dot product of two tangent operators) — none of these are inputs; all are derived.

The technical core is a single master equation: requiring the connection to be torsion-free (order-independence of displacement) turns out to fully determine the spin connection purely from the soldering forms in a cell and its immediate neighbors, with no leftover freedom to set by hand. This is also how the paper dodges a long-standing technical obstacle — the "Leibniz rule problem," where ordinary finite differences don't obey the continuum product rule, making naive term-by-term discretization of GR's formulas historically unreliable. Curvature is then defined the way lattice gauge theory defines it: compose the four spin connections around one elementary plaquette and see what rotation, if any, is left over after the loop closes. The paper's central mathematical payoff is proving that as the elementary cell volume shrinks to zero, every one of these discrete objects — the metric, the affine connection, the Riemann curvature tensor, and the Einstein–Hilbert action itself — converges exactly onto its familiar continuum counterpart. An explicit two-dimensional worked example (three neighboring cells in flat Euclidean space, SO(2) rotations) shows the machinery grinding through to a closed-form curvature expression built from a signed loop-sum of local rotation angles, structurally reminiscent of a Regge-calculus deficit angle but derived here from gauge rotations rather than triangulated cone geometry.

## 8. Three Crystallized Takeaways

1. **Gravity gets rebuilt from a kit of indivisible Planck-sized cells with no notion of "inside" them — and ordinary curved spacetime pops right back out, term for term, the instant you let the cells shrink to zero.** Nothing about known general relativity is lost in this limit; it's recovered exactly, not approximately.

2. **The trick is refusing to discretize Einstein's equations directly.** A decades-old problem with discretizing GR is that finite differences don't obey the same product rule as continuum derivatives. This paper sidesteps that entirely by building the whole theory out of local rotation operators between neighboring cells, with the requirement of "no torsion" doing all the work of pinning those rotations down.

3. **This is a proof-of-concept in mathematical physics, not a new prediction about the universe.** It only covers Euclidean (not real, time-including) space, doesn't quantize anything, and hasn't yet been tested or extended by anyone outside the original two-to-three-person team. The promised payoffs — a natural UV cutoff, quantized black holes, an emergent expanding universe — are explicitly left for future papers.

## 9. Shorter Summary

*(350-word ceiling)*

General relativity assumes space can be divided infinitely finely. Quantum gravity calculations suggest that assumption breaks down near the Planck length, where quantum fluctuations in the shape of space itself become enormous. This paper takes that breakdown seriously and rebuilds geometry from scratch, assuming points closer together than some elementary volume simply aren't physically distinguishable.

Instead of laying a grid over an already-existing curved space (the way some prior approaches did), the authors start with nothing but a collection of featureless cells, each one only aware of its immediate neighbors through a local rotation rule. Distance, angle, and curvature are never assumed — they're built up afterward from that local rotation data. The key equation in the paper says that requiring consistent, order-independent movement between cells (no "torsion") is enough, all by itself, to completely pin down those local rotations from neighboring cells' data, with nothing left to set by hand. That also lets the authors dodge a known headache in earlier discretization attempts: ordinary step-by-step differences don't obey the same math rules as smooth derivatives, which had made naive discretizations of Einstein's equations unreliable. Curvature, in this picture, is simply the leftover rotation you find after walking a small square loop of four cells and composing the local rotations along the way — mathematically the same idea used to detect inconsistencies in distributed systems that only track local relative updates.

The main proof in the paper is that as these cells shrink toward zero size, this entire machinery reconstructs ordinary curved-space geometry exactly — the metric, the curvature tensor, and even Einstein's own gravitational action formula all reappear unchanged. A worked two-dimensional example shows the method producing an explicit curvature number from local rotation angles.

What this paper does *not* do yet: it only covers space without time (no real spacetime dynamics), it doesn't quantize anything despite gesturing at "quanta of geometry," and it hasn't been picked up or tested by any research group outside the original authors. Think of it as a carefully engineered piece of mathematical scaffolding — solid and internally consistent — built in the hope that quantized black holes, a natural cutoff for infinities, and an emergent picture of the expanding universe can eventually be hung on it. That hanging hasn't happened yet.
