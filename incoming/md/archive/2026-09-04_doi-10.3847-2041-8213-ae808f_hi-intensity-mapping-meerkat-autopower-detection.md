## 1 — Punchy Title & One-Sentence Hook

**Hearing the Hydrogen Without a Map.**

For the first time with an SKA-precursor interferometer, the collective 21 cm hum of billions of unresolved galaxies was pulled out of foregrounds ~10⁵ times brighter — using MeerKAT *alone*, with no optical galaxy survey to lean on — by exploiting the one thing smooth foregrounds cannot fake: structure across frequency.

---

## 2 — Big-Picture Context

Neutral hydrogen (HI) emits a faint spectral line at a rest wavelength of 21 cm (~1420 MHz). Every hydrogen cloud is a tiny beacon at that fixed frequency, redshifted by cosmic expansion, so observed frequency encodes distance and a radio map across frequencies is a 3-D map of the matter. Any single galaxy's line is absurdly faint; **intensity mapping** measures the aggregate brightness of many unresolved sources in low-resolution voxels, trading per-object detail for enormous survey volume.

The obstacle is contamination: Galactic synchrotron and radio galaxies (the "foregrounds") outshine the HI signal by four to five orders of magnitude. Because radio systematics don't correlate with an optical catalog, every prior detection was made in **cross-correlation** — multiply the radio map against a galaxy survey so only the shared cosmological signal survives. That is a crutch: it confirms the signal but can't stand alone and is limited to where an overlapping optical survey exists.

This paper detects the HI power spectrum in **auto-correlation** (HI against itself, no galaxy survey) at z ≈ 0.32 and 0.44, using MeerKAT in *interferometer* mode (not single-dish). Interferometry reaches smaller, quasi-linear scales (~1 Mpc) that single-dish IM can't. The method is **foreground avoidance**: rather than model-subtract foregrounds (risky — you can subtract signal too), refuse to look at the Fourier region the foregrounds occupy.

**Paper Type & Stakes.** An observational detection plus methods demonstration — an experimental first, not theory. If auto-power 21 cm cosmology works with a precursor like MeerKAT, it validates the core survey mode for the SKA and opens precision cosmology on quasi-linear scales without paired galaxy surveys.

**Prior Belief Check.** The community *expected* this to be possible — forecasts (Paul et al. 2021) predicted ~100 hr of MeerKAT would suffice, and ~96 hr is what was used. So the result confirms an expectation rather than overturning one. The honest read is that confidence *fell* under scrutiny: over ~3.5 years of review the headline significances dropped (conservative framework at 3.2σ/3.5σ vs v1's 8.0σ/11.5σ) and "first" left the title/abstract. Two drivers: residual instrumental systematics that a more conservative reanalysis cut harder, and a genuinely **contested primacy landscape** — GMRT auto-power gave only upper limits (Chakraborty, Pal, Elahi 2021–2024); MeerKLASS reported a single-dish auto-power result; and in Nov 2025 CHIME claimed "the first detection of the cosmological 21 cm signal in auto-correlation at z ~ 1" at 12.5σ. **Verdict: an important, real experimental first-of-kind (interferometric auto-power at Mpc scales), but incremental rather than paradigm-shifting, and hedged for good reasons.**

**Replication & Convergence Note.** Single group, single field (DEEP2), single instrument; no independent replication at these scales/redshifts. Confidence is raised by convergence from adjacent efforts (CHIME z ~ 1, MeerKLASS single-dish, GMRT limits). Strongest future confirmation: an independent auto-power detection at z ≈ 0.3–0.44 on ~Mpc scales showing the amplitude and its integration-time scaling behave as a thermal-noise-limited cosmological signal. The dramatic v1→v2 significance revision is itself a within-paper robustness stress-test.

## 3 — Necessary Background Crash-Course

**The 21 cm line.** A hyperfine transition — the electron and proton spins flipping from aligned to anti-aligned — emits a 21 cm photon; the same spin-state energy splitting from chemistry, but extraordinarily small. *Analogy:* a fixed-pitch tuning fork every atom rings at; redshift lowers the pitch with distance, so pitch is a distance ruler. *Breaks when:* per-atom emission is so weak only the summed chorus is detectable — the reason intensity mapping exists.

**Intensity mapping vs. galaxy surveys.** A galaxy survey resolves and catalogs individual objects (row-by-row scan). Intensity mapping computes an *aggregate over unresolved rows* — a SUM/histogram over a billion-row table without materializing rows. *Breaks when:* unlike a clean DB aggregate, the "column" is buried under contamination 10⁵× larger than the signal.

**Interferometry and visibilities.** An interferometer never images directly. Each antenna pair (a *baseline*) measures the *correlation* of the sky's field between two points — one Fourier mode of the sky. All baseline measurements form the **visibility** function; baselines populate a plane of spatial-frequency samples (the "uv-plane"). *Analogy:* instead of the bitmap, store pairwise correlations — a compressed, transformed representation. *Breaks when:* sampling is sparse and beam-convolved; you never capture the full transform, so imaging needs deconvolution (which this pipeline avoids). [See deep-dive appendix for the full build of this.]

**The power spectrum.** The fundamental summary statistic: how much variance lives at each spatial scale (wavenumber *k*; large *k* = small scales). *Analogy:* a profile bucketing the field's total "wiggliness" by feature size. *Breaks when:* it captures only two-point (Gaussian) information; non-Gaussian structure at nonlinear scales is invisible to it.

**Foreground avoidance and the wedge.** Foregrounds are *spectrally smooth* → concentrate at low line-of-sight wavenumber k∥ ≈ 0. HI has frequency structure → spreads to high k∥. Instrument chromaticity smears foregrounds up along a diagonal boundary — the **wedge** — that widens with k⊥. Stay above the wedge and you're in a clean window. Concretely they keep only k∥ > 0.3 k⊥, deliberately ~10× stricter than MeerKAT's real horizon slope (~0.02). *CS analogy:* a conservative keyspace partition — refuse to query strictly inside the contaminated region, accepting loss of some clean modes to guarantee no poisoned ones (Bloom-filter-style conservative exclusion). *Breaks when:* chromaticity leaks foregrounds up into the window, and a band of instrumental systematics sits right on the boundary (a known MeerKAT artifact near zero-length baselines), so avoidance alone wasn't enough — extra flagging was needed.

**The delay spectrum.** Fourier-transform the visibilities *along frequency*; the conjugate variable "delay" (τ) maps to k∥. Work in the transform domain directly — no imaging/deconvolution.

**The halo model (for §4).** Clustering = a **2-halo term** (between halos, large scales), a **1-halo term** (within one halo, small scales), and **shot noise** (a flat floor from discreteness). *Analogy:* shot noise is the quantization floor from a finite number of discrete records, dominated (mass²-weighting) by the rare heavy hitters.

> **Central analogy for this paper:** cross-check independent copies — shared signal survives, random noise cancels.

The whole reason auto-power is hard is *noise bias*: correlate a dataset with itself and you add its noise variance on top of signal. The escape is to split the data into independent halves and correlate the halves — true sky is shared and squares up; independent noise multiplies to zero. Same intuition as running a computation on two disjoint shards and cross-validating.

## 4 — Core Technical Explanation

**Pipeline, end to end.** ~96 hr of MeerKAT L-band data on one 2 deg² field (DEEP2, unusually free of bright radio sources), calibrated from scratch (RFI flagging, full-pol calibration, self-cal) to gain error ~10⁻⁵ (foreground leakage scales with calibration error, so this precision is load-bearing). Then, entirely in the visibility domain:

1. **Grid** into coarse uv-cells (Δu = 60λ, large so the primary beam doesn't smear power between cells).
2. **Split** three ways: even vs. odd scans, two time-blocks, two ~46 MHz sub-bands (986 MHz → z ≈ 0.44; 1077.5 MHz → z ≈ 0.32; narrow bands keep Δz ~ 0.06).
3. **Delay-transform** (FFT along frequency), first applying a Blackman-Harris window to stop smooth foregrounds leaking to high k∥.
4. **Cross-correlate** the independent splits — the central trick — to remove noise bias.
5. **Foreground avoidance** (keep k∥ > 0.3 k⊥) and **flag residual systematics**.
6. **Inverse-variance-average** to a 1-D P(k); compute detection significance.

**The measurement estimator (load-bearing equation):**

$$P_D(k_\perp,\,k_\parallel)=\frac{x^{2}y}{\Omega_{ps}\,B}\left(\frac{\lambda^{2}}{2k_B}\right)^{2}\mathrm{Re}\{\tilde{V}_1\tilde{V}_2^{*}\}$$

- $P_D$ : delay power spectrum — variance at mode $(k_\perp,k_\parallel)$ (mK²·Mpc³)
- $x$ : comoving distance to the redshift (Mpc); $y$ : comoving depth of the bandwidth (Mpc)
- $\Omega_{ps}$ : power-squared primary-beam area (sr); $B$ : sub-band bandwidth (Hz)
- $\lambda$ : wavelength at band center (m); $k_B$ : Boltzmann constant
- $\tilde{V}_1,\tilde{V}_2$ : delay-transformed visibilities from two **independent splits**; $\mathrm{Re}\{\tilde{V}_1\tilde{V}_2^{*}\}$ : real part of their cross-product

*What it means:* the visibility is already a correlation; this takes the correlation of two independent measurements of the same sky and keeps the real part. Systems terms: compute the same aggregate twice from disjoint inputs and multiply — the shared true value squares up, independent errors multiply to ~zero. The prefactor is unit conversion to a cosmological temperature-variance-per-volume.

**Why cross-correlation kills noise (worked micro-example).** Let true signal variance ⟨S²⟩ = 4 and thermal-noise variance ⟨N²⟩ = 25 (noise ≫ signal).
- Auto-power of one split: ⟨(S+N)²⟩ = ⟨S²⟩ + ⟨N²⟩ = 4 + 25 = **29** — 25 is pure noise bias (87% overstatement).
- Cross-power of two independent splits: ⟨(S+N₁)(S+N₂)⟩ = ⟨S²⟩ + 0 + 0 + 0 = **4**.

Independent, zero-mean N₁, N₂ make every noise cross-term vanish. Identical to cross-checking two disjoint shards: the correct answer survives, transient faults cancel. This is why they never autocorrelate a single cube — that keeps the +25.

**The foreground cut, quantified:**

$$k_\parallel=\frac{x\,H_0\,E(z)\,\sin\theta_0}{c\,(1+z)}\;k_\perp$$

- $k_\parallel,k_\perp$ : line-of-sight and transverse wavenumbers (Mpc⁻¹); $x$ : comoving distance
- $H_0$ : Hubble constant; $E(z)$ : dimensionless expansion rate ($H(z)=H_0E(z)$); $c$ : speed of light; $\theta_0$ : angular extent over which foregrounds enter
- Coefficient collapses to a slope; here $k_\parallel \approx 0.3\,k_\perp$

*What it means:* smooth foregrounds want k∥ ≈ 0, but chromatic leakage drags them up this slope. Physical worst case (sin θ₀ = 1) ≈ 0.26; MeerKAT's real value ≈ 0.02; they adopt 0.3, deliberately over-cautious. A mode at k⊥ = 1 Mpc⁻¹ is trusted only if k∥ > 0.3 Mpc⁻¹.

**Pinning the noise with Stokes V:**

$$\sigma_N=\frac{2k_B T_{sys}}{A_e\sqrt{\delta\nu\,\delta t}}$$

- $\sigma_N$ : noise per visibility; $T_{sys}$ : system temperature; $A_e$ : effective area; $\delta\nu = 209$ kHz; $\delta t = 8$ s
- $A_e/T_{sys}$ ("natural sensitivity") ≈ 6.5 m²/K measured vs 6.22 expected

*What it means:* noise per sample falls as $1/\sqrt{\delta\nu\,\delta t}$ (ordinary $1/\sqrt{N}$ averaging). Measured ≈ spec is a sanity check calibration didn't inflate sensitivity. Stokes V (circular polarization, barely emitted by real sources) is a near-pure noise-only probe; they simulate 10,000 noise realizations and flag any k-pixel deviating >5σ — the cut that removes the boundary stripe.

**Headline number.** Top-hat window at R = 1.0 Mpc gives rms HI brightness-temperature fluctuation **σ_HI = 0.44 ± 0.04 mK (z ≈ 0.32)** and **0.63 ± 0.03 mK (z ≈ 0.44)** — *v1 values; likely revised in the published version.* Small-scale amplitude (~0.1 mK²·Mpc³) is consistent with shot noise (~1 mK²·Mpc³) heavily attenuated by Fingers-of-God (random intra-halo velocities smear the signal along the line of sight — a Doppler blur on the k∥ axis only).

**Model fitting.** A 5-parameter halo model (HI-mass–halo-mass amplitude and slope, profile shape, velocity dispersion, shot noise) via MCMC. Honest result: **most parameters don't converge.** 1-D binning discards the k∥ dependence that separates velocity dispersion from shot noise, so those stay degenerate. Only an external ΩHI prior (HI stacking / DLAs) yields partial constraints, and only at z ≈ 0.44 (higher S/N) does a real σᵥ ≈ 146 km/s emerge. Tying shot noise to an HI mass function removes an unphysical bias — a consistency demonstration, not a measurement.

### Assumption Audit

- **Watch:** "auto-correlation" reads as *one dataset correlated with itself*. They **never** do that — they cross-correlate *independent splits*. "Auto-power" means *same tracer* (HI × HI, no galaxy survey), **not** same data. Miss this and the noise-bias story inverts.
- **Watch:** a higher σ reads as a better result. Here the *lower* conservative significance (3.2σ/3.5σ, baseline-flagging) is the more **robust** claim; the 5.9σ/9.18σ comes from a less conservative flag, and v1's 8.0σ/11.5σ was revised *down*. More σ can mean *less* cautious systematics handling.
- **Watch:** "detection at N-σ" reads as "the HI signal is confirmed at N-σ." It means the binned power in the clean window differs from zero at N-σ. Its *identity* as HI rests on three legs — null test, foreground avoidance, amplitude consistency — not on σ.
- **Watch:** the halo-model section reads as *delivering* astrophysics. It mostly delivers *divergent posteriors* — proof-of-concept for future data, heavily prior-dependent.

## 5 — What's Genuinely New or Clever

1. **A galaxy-survey-independent (auto-power) HI detection at quasi-linear ~Mpc scales with an SKA precursor.** Cross-correlation detections existed; single-dish IM reaches only tens-of-Mpc scales. Interferometric auto-power at ~1 Mpc at these redshifts is the genuine first-of-kind, removing the galaxy-survey crutch and reaching the nonlinear regime single-dish can't.
2. **A quantified, honest systematics handle.** The Stokes-V-calibrated thermal-noise simulation for 5σ k-space flagging, plus the two-framework approach (conservative baseline-flagging vs power-spectrum-flagging). The enabling move — cross-correlating independent splits to null noise bias — is *borrowed* from the EoR delay-spectrum tradition (Parsons, Liu, Pober); the novelty is porting it to post-EoR IM auto-power and making it stick.

*New to field vs new to reader:* the delay-spectrum + foreground-avoidance toolkit is well-established in EoR 21 cm work — for a systems reader, this is *applying a known algorithm to a new regime*, not inventing it. The "first" framing is contested: v1 said "first direct detection," the published version dropped it, and CHIME (2025), MeerKLASS, and GMRT limits crowd the claim. Defensible narrow version: *first interferometric HI auto-power detection at Mpc scales at z ≈ 0.3–0.44.*

**Predictive Content Check.**
- *Falsifiable handle (real):* the measured P(k) amplitude and σ_HI (~0.44 / 0.63 mK at 1 Mpc) are numbers deeper MeerKAT/MIGHTEE data on the same field — or an independent instrument — can confirm or contradict. Sharpest tests: (a) signal should scale with integration time as thermal-noise-limited; a plateau or wrong growth means systematics; (b) the **null test** (two disjoint frequency bands → consistent with zero) is a built-in falsification they *pass*; (c) amplitude should match independent ΩHI·b_HI. A materially different amplitude on future DEEP2 data challenges the detection.
- *Formalism load (fire this half):* the *measurement* machinery is load-bearing (estimator, wedge cut, noise sim). But the **§4 halo-model/HIMF apparatus is closer to decorative-for-now** — elaborate, produces largely unconstrained posteriors, removable without changing any *measured* result. It demonstrates feasibility for future data rather than extracting present-day astrophysics (the paper says so). Don't mistake the modeling scaffold for delivered constraints.

## 7 — Detailed Summary & Explanation

Using ~96 hr of MeerKAT interferometer data on the exceptionally clean DEEP2 field, the authors measured the HI power spectrum in auto-correlation (no galaxy survey) at z ≈ 0.32 and 0.44 on scales down to ~1 Mpc. Working with visibilities (no imaging), they calibrated to ~10⁻⁵ gain error, split the data into independent chunks, delay-transformed, and cross-correlated the chunks so thermal noise cancels while the shared cosmological signal survives. They discarded the foreground wedge (k∥ > 0.3 k⊥), used Stokes-V-calibrated noise simulations to flag a residual stripe, and averaged to a 1-D spectrum. The result is a detection at each redshift, cross-checked by a null test showing no leftover correlated power. They constrained sub-mK rms HI fluctuations at 1 Mpc and attempted — with limited success — to extract HI astrophysics.

Three interpretive choices drive the framing. First, foregrounding the v1→published discrepancy, because it *is* the story of the paper's maturation: confidence fell and "first" was dropped — the honest-calibration signal the Prior Belief and Replication checks exist to surface. Second, hammering that "auto" means *same tracer, not same data*, without which the noise-bias logic is unintelligible. Third, labeling the modeling demonstrative rather than constraining — the paper delivers a detection and a method, not astrophysical measurements.

> **Where I'm least confident:** I have the *full* v1 text but only the *published abstract* for v2, so the distinction between "baseline-flagging" (3.2σ/3.5σ) and "power-spectrum-flagging" (5.9σ/9.18σ), and precisely which numbers (σ_HI, Table 1, posteriors) were revised, is **inferred, not read**. Best reading: "baseline-flagging" aggressively excises the whole short-baseline (u ≈ 0) region — more conservative, lower σ — while "power-spectrum-flagging" is the refined descendant of v1's per-k-pixel 5σ thermal-noise cut. Treat that mapping as a hypothesis to verify against the published methods. (Structurally all nine sections kept; no material adaptation.)

## 8 — Three Crystallized Takeaways

1. **They heard hydrogen without a map.** For the first time with an SKA precursor, the collective 21 cm glow of billions of unresolved galaxies was detected using MeerKAT alone — no galaxy survey — the hard part being that radio foregrounds outweigh the signal ~100,000-to-1, and in auto-correlation nothing external cancels them.
2. **The trick is cross-checking independent copies.** Split the data in half, correlate the halves: real sky is shared and squares up, random noise differs and cancels — then dodge the foregrounds entirely by only looking at the Fourier modes their spectral smoothness can't reach.
3. **The honesty is in the version history.** Over 3.5 years of review the headline confidence fell (8σ → 3.2σ conservative) and "first" quietly left the title — a real milestone, but a more sober one than the 2023 preprint, whose cosmological payoff is still a promise for deeper data.

## 9 — Shorter Summary

Neutral hydrogen across the universe emits a faint radio line at 21 cm; mapping its collective brightness (rather than cataloging individual galaxies) is a powerful, cheap way to chart cosmic structure in 3-D. The obstacle: unwanted "foregrounds" from our galaxy and radio galaxies are up to 100,000× brighter. Until now, every detection leaned on a crutch — cross-correlating the radio data against an optical galaxy survey so the shared signal survives and the mismatched junk averages out.

This paper removes the crutch. Using ~96 hours of MeerKAT data on an unusually clean patch of sky, the team detected the hydrogen signal *on its own* — "auto-correlation," hydrogen against hydrogen with no galaxy survey — at redshifts 0.32 and 0.44, on smaller scales than single-dish efforts reach. Two ideas make it work. To beat the random noise, they split the data into independent halves and multiply them: the true signal is common and reinforces, the noise differs and cancels. To beat the foregrounds, they exploit that foregrounds are smooth across frequency while the signal is not, and refuse to analyze the region where foregrounds live.

The result is a solid detection with a passing null test. But the picture is measured, not triumphant: over a long review the reported confidence dropped (a conservative analysis lands ~3σ vs much higher 2023-preprint claims), "first" was removed, and competing claims (CHIME, single-dish MeerKAT) crowd the label. Attempts to extract detailed HI astrophysics mostly didn't converge. The real significance is as a proof of concept: the core survey method planned for the Square Kilometre Array works with today's precursor instruments.

## Appendix — Deep-Dive Discussion Log (2026-09-04)

Deep dive stayed entirely on the **measurement geometry of radio interferometry**, driven by the reader working to *visualize* how a set of antenna correlations becomes a 3-D power spectrum. It ran as an escalating rebuild — the first pass overshot and had to be reconstructed twice from more physical first principles — and is worth logging both for its content and for the calibration lesson. Three analyst-authored SVG concept diagrams were rendered in-chat during the dive (in addition to the k-space wedge diagram rendered during the analysis).

**Thread 1 — Visibilities as Fourier samples of the sky.** Corrected a loose phrase in §3 ("one Fourier mode"): a single baseline at one instant/frequency samples *one point of the sky's continuous 2-D Fourier transform* (van Cittert–Zernike), slightly beam-blurred. Mechanism established: a plane wave from direction **ŝ** reaches the two antennas with a relative geometric phase 2π(**b·ŝ**)/λ that *depends on direction*; the correlator multiplies and time-averages, summing each sky direction's brightness weighted by that phase → the 2-D FT of the beam-weighted sky evaluated at spatial frequency (u,v) = **b**/λ. Key identities: u = b/λ is literally cycles-per-radian (a spatial frequency), so baseline length *selects* which Fourier coefficient is read (short → coarse, long → fine; angular scale ≈ 1/u). CS framing that landed: each baseline is a *random-access read of one coefficient of the sky's transform*; imaging is the expensive reconstruction/inversion from sparse, non-uniform, beam-convolved reads — which this pipeline skips. Worked anchor: Δu = 60λ at 1077.5 MHz → λ ≈ 0.278 m → 60λ ≈ 16.7 m → 1/u ≈ 0.95° → k⊥ = 2πu/x ≈ 0.29 Mpc⁻¹, matching the paper's ΔK⊥ ~ 0.3 Mpc⁻¹ (same object in angular vs comoving units; map = divide on-sky spatial frequency by comoving distance). Beam-blur (multiplication in sky space = convolution in the uv-plane) is exactly why they grid at 60λ. Flat-sky/small-field caveat (the w-term) noted; frequency-fanning of a fixed physical baseline (u = b/λ drifts across the band) noted — foreshadowing the wedge. *This pass was pitched too high — the reader did not follow it, which triggered the rebuild.*

**Thread 2 — Rebuild from the ground up (why a correlator reports one spatial frequency).** Reframed around motivation (fake a kilometers-wide dish from many small ones) and the two-antenna time delay (stereo-hearing analogy for delay → direction; *breaks when:* ears localize one source, a baseline reports one spatial-frequency component summed over the whole sky). Correlation = multiply + time-average = a **dot product** = a similarity score. First stripe/fringe framing introduced. Fringe spacing displayed as $\Delta\theta \approx \lambda/b$ with worked number (16.7 m → 0.95°); short baseline = coarse comb (large-scale-sensitive), long = fine comb; MeerKAT's dense 1 km core = a deliberate pile of short baselines. *The reader remained stuck specifically on where the stripes come from and what "line up with the stripes" means* — the assertion "picture stripes" had been left underived.

**Thread 3 — Deriving the fringe mechanism (the pass that landed).** Built the stripes from scratch in two mechanical, checkable steps. (1) Multiply-and-average of two phase-shifted copies of the same wave → output ∝ cos(phase difference); demonstrated by sampling a wave as [+1, 0, −1, 0] and multiplying against in-phase (avg +½), quarter-lag (avg 0), and half-lag/inverted (avg −½) copies. (2) The phase difference is set by source position: $\varphi = 2\pi\, b\sin\theta / \lambda$. Combined: output vs sky angle = cos of a phase that grows with angle → an oscillation +½, 0, −½, 0 across the sky = **the stripe comb**. "Lining up with the stripes" = the real sky (many sources) projected onto the comb: a dot product Σ brightness(θ)·cos(comb at θ) — sky variation matching the comb spacing reinforces (big number), mismatched/smooth cancels (~0) — which *is* one spatial-frequency measurement / one Fourier coefficient. *Break-when:* the primary beam windows the comb (soft spotlight), so one baseline isolates a narrow band around a spatial frequency, not a perfectly pure one — the small blur behind coarse gridding. SVG rendered: fringe response (correlator output) vs source position across the sky (a cosine), bright/dark fringes marked. **This unlocked the visualization.**

**Thread 4 — The third dimension ("3-D").** Frequency is the depth ruler: every H atom emits at the same rest frequency, redshift grows with distance, so *observed frequency encodes distance* and a spectrum is a 1-D map of hydrogen stacked by distance along the line of sight → the k∥ axis. Line-of-sight structure = ripples in the spectrum; decompose with the same comb *along frequency* (the delay transform) → k∥ (few cycles per band = large scale/low k∥; many = fine/high k∥). The payoff, and the whole reason the third axis is worth having: foregrounds are **smooth in frequency** → collapse to low k∥; clumpy HI **ripples** → spreads to high k∥ — so on the new axis they *separate*, even though in the flat 2-D sky-plane the foreground is 10⁵× brighter at every k⊥ and inseparable. Cube assembly: per baseline record a whole spectrum; baseline → k⊥, FT of that baseline's spectrum → k∥; fill a 3-D grid; collapse the two statistically-equivalent transverse axes to a single magnitude → the cylindrical (k⊥, k∥) plane of the wedge diagram. The wedge re-derived with understanding: u = b/λ drifts across the band (chromaticity), stamping a faint spurious slow ripple on even a smooth foreground, growing with baseline → leaks foregrounds upward in k∥ by an amount growing with k⊥ → a diagonal boundary; the detection is the leftover correlated power safely above it. SVG rendered: smooth foreground spectrum vs rippling HI spectrum (drawn at unequal true scales). 

**Calibration / pedagogy learnings (for the framework).** (i) This reader needed the interferometry mechanism built *from physical first principles* (path delay → phase → multiply-average = cos Δφ → oscillation-vs-angle = fringes → projection = Fourier coefficient), with worked micro-numbers at each step; a formalism-first pass (van Cittert–Zernike / FT integral) overshot and required two rebuilds.
