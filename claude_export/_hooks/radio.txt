Big-Picture Context **Paper Type & Stakes:** This is an observational discovery-characterization paper — a multiwavelength case study of a single object that coins a new class term ("radio changing-look AGN") and works through a systematic elimination of outburst scenarios. What's at stake is our understanding of how radio jets switch on around supermassive black holes, particularly the low-mass, fast-growing kind that dominated the early Universe. Active galactic nuclei have historically been filed into tidy bins: radio-loud or radio-quiet, type 1 or type 2, blazar or Seyfert. Over the last decade, the "changing-look" phenomenon has been dismantling that filing system. Optical changing-look AGN swap their spectroscopic type as broad emission lines appear or vanish; X-ray changing-look AGN swing between absorbed and unabsorbed states. What had *not* been seen is an AGN switching between radio-quiet and radio-loud and *staying* there — the classification everyone assumed was the most stable of all, because it's tied to whether the black hole launches a relativistic jet. The object here, SDSS J1105+1452, is a narrow-line Seyfert 1 (NLS1) at redshift 0.12 — a class hosting small black holes (here roughly 3–4 million solar masses) accreting near their Eddington limit. NLS1s matter beyond their own zip code: they're the local, observable analogs of the rapidly growing black holes JWST is now finding in the early Universe.
