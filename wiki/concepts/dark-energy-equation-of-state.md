# Dark Energy Equation of State

**One-line summary:** The ratio \(w = P/(\rho c^2)\) that tells gravity how dark energy pushes; \(\Lambda\) means \(w=-1\) forever, while \(w_0 w_a\) models allow a present value and a time slope.

## Physical picture

Matter clumps (\(w=0\)); radiation dilutes fast (\(w=1/3\)); a cosmological constant has \(w=-1\) with fixed density. Dynamical dark energy can sit at different \(w\) and can evolve. DESI’s preferred picture: more \(\Lambda\)-like in the past, *weaker* today (\(w_a < 0\))—not phantom runaway toward a Big Rip.

A competing story replaces (or reshapes) the fluid entirely: **modified gravity** with a finite range. Ghost-free massive gravity ([[massive-gravity-drgt]]) gives the graviton a tiny mass so gravity weakens on the largest scales — acceleration without a separate DE battery — but it must still pass local GR tests via Vainshtein screening and GW multimessenger bounds. Data-driven DE analyses ([[desi-evolving-dark-energy]], [[universe-gas-pedal-leaky]]) and modified-gravity alternatives answer different questions; both sit under the same “why does expansion run away?” umbrella.

## Key results

- DESI DR2 BAO + CMB + SN: ~3–4σ preference for evolving DE over \(\Lambda\)CDM depending on SN sample ([[desi-evolving-dark-energy]]).
- SN Ia standardization and lensing time delays remain independent cross-checks ([[type-ia-supernovae]], [[time-delay-cosmography]], [[hubble-tension]]).
- Massive-gravity / finite-range gravity is a theory-side alternative, not a drop-in $w_0$–$w_a$ fit ([[massive-gravity-drgt]]).
