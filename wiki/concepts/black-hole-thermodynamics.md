# Black Hole Thermodynamics

**One-line summary:** Horizon area as entropy and surface gravity as temperature—Bekenstein–Hawking thermodynamics—plus modern attempts to use entropy extremization to predict merger remnants, and a dynamical first law that does not wait for equilibrium.

## Physical picture

Bigger horizon → more entropy. Mergers increase total area (second law). [[entropy-maximization-bh-mergers]] asks whether the remnant spin is the one that *would have* maximized Kerr entropy along the inspiral path—a thermostat heuristic that tracks NR surprisingly well.

The classical **third law** (you cannot drive surface gravity $\kappa$ to zero in finite time) was the shakiest pillar. [[black-hole-third-law-violation]] constructs vacuum 5D counterexamples that reach extremality ($\kappa=0$, $T_H=0$) in finite time—including from pure gravitational waves—so the analogy with Nernst’s third law does **not** hold as a general theorem of GR.

**Far from equilibrium (2026-09-05):** [[bh-thermo-far-from-equilibrium]] rebuilds the first law for finite processes on a **marginally trapped surface** (quasi-local), not the teleological event horizon — the entropy surface you can locate *now*, with a budget that recovers BCH at equilibrium.
