# Chemistry and Biotech Methods

**One-line summary:** This wiki’s chemistry / biotech shelf is a methods-and-mechanism map — synthetic cells and droplets, single-atom and bond-level catalysis, water under confinement and in enzymes, readout/barcoding tools, ultrafast reaction control, plus thin astrochem and quantum-biology neighbors — not a substitute for condensed-matter or ultrafast *physics* hubs.

## Why pull these together

The index graduated **Chemistry & biotech** as a first-class section with ≥15 papers and no prior synthesis. They answer one engineer’s question:

> What **handle** does each paper give you — a new reaction environment, a cheaper bond break, a water rule, a barcode, or a clock on a molecule — and what does it *not* claim?

Stack them and five threads appear. Stay separate from [[condensed-matter-topology-fractionalization]] and [[ultrafast-optics-and-solid-state-emitters]] unless a paper earns dual listing (e.g. ultrafast chemical shifts sits here as *chemistry readout*, not as solid-state emitter physics).

## Thread notes (short)

**A — Soft compartments.** Coacervate “velcro” cells, microdroplet ring rewrites, and midair millisecond scaffold factories all move synthesis out of flasks into programmable soft matter. Limits are usually scale-up, leakage, and how “cell-like” the analogy is allowed to get.

**B — Catalysis & bonding.** Single-Ru sites, catalytic bond-breaking discounts, and ~one-bond inductive range are local electronic-structure engineering. Do not collapse them into solid-state topology papers without a shared Hamiltonian claim.

**C — Water.** Nanoconfinement and RNA Pol II both ask whether bulk water rules survive inside a machine. Different length scales; same humility about continuum solvent models.

**D — Tools.** Cas13a kinetic barcodes, evolutionary resistance “tax,” and GPU MS are enabling tech — cite for method, not for a unified biotech theory.

**E — Ultrafast chemistry.** Chemical-shift movies and two-laser / bias-point control sit next to [[ultrafast-optics-and-solid-state-emitters]] without merging: here the *molecule* is the device under test.

**Neighbors.** Interstellar sulfur ice is astrochem; cryptochrome ascorbate null is quantum-biology adjacent to measurement / spin chemistry — keep them labeled as neighbors, not core threads.
