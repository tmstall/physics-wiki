# Nuclear and Dense-Matter Precision Observables

**One-line summary:** This wiki’s nuclear, heavy-ion, and dense-QCD pages form a comparative map of **precision observables** — net-proton cumulants, charged/neutral kaon ratios, $A=3$ EMC slopes, SRC shell memory, UPCs, and color-SC theory — that constrain different corners of nuclear structure and the QCD phase diagram without a single shared “smoking gun.”

## Why pull these together

Nuclear structure, the EMC effect, critical-point searches, isospin breaking, and cold dense matter look like separate communities. In this wiki they answer one systems question:

> What does each **precision observable** actually constrain — and what baselines must you subtract before you claim new physics?

No single measurement maps the whole $T$–$\mu_B$ plane or the whole nuclear many-body problem. Stack them and four laboratories appear: (1) **hot fireballs** at RHIC/SPS scanning $\mu_B$, (2) **cold nuclei** at Jefferson Lab reading medium-modified partons and SRCs, (3) **hard / diffractive QCD** at RHIC and the ISR as historical and modern parton probes, (4) **theory corners** of cold dense QCD (color SC, mesic nuclei) that experiments only touch indirectly. The tensions live where “isospin” or “medium modification” mean different things in DIS versus heavy-ion yields.

This synthesis is **nuclear / dense-matter precision**. Multi-messenger HEA and reionization live on [[high-energy-astrophysics-multimessenger]]. Condensed-matter fractionalization lives on [[condensed-matter-topology-fractionalization]]. Do not collapse those programs into this page.

## Thread A — Hot dense matter: fluctuations and isospin

**Papers:** [[rhic-net-proton-fluctuations]], [[na61-isospin-kaon-asymmetry]], [[color-superconductivity-qcd]]

### A1 — Net-proton cumulants as critical-point sensors

[[rhic-net-proton-fluctuations]] (STAR BES-II, arXiv:2504.00817): high-precision $C_n$ and factorial $\kappa_n$ for (net-)protons across $\sqrt{s_{NN}}=7.7$–$27$ GeV. Central $C_4/C_2$ stays **below Poisson** at all energies. The structure of interest is a **minimum in significance of deviation** (~2–5σ) from non-critical models and peripheral data near **19.6 GeV** — not a BES-I-style peak above Poisson.

**Intuition:** Near a critical point the system is hypersensitive; higher moments swing. Net protons proxy baryon number while the energy scan walks $\mu_B$. BES-II is the high-statistics remake: boring baselines (UrQMD, HRG CE, hydro EV, peripheral events) leave a notch near 20 GeV that critical dynamics are invited to explain.

**Limits:** Baseline choice moves the σ; volume/efficiency/centrality systematics dominate; FXT gap 3–7.7 GeV open; no CP dynamical model fully matches the suite.

### A2 — Kaon isospin excess at the SPS

[[na61-isospin-kaon-asymmetry]]: $R_K=1.184\pm0.061$ in Ar+Sc at 11.9 GeV — charged kaons exceed neutrals by ~18%, far above few-percent HRG / mass-splitting / neutron-excess expectations (~4.7σ with world data in the analysis narrative).

**Intuition:** Isospin says $u$ and $d$ are near twins. Kaon charge states should lockstep. The fireball plays favorites more than textbooks budgeted.

**Limits:** Neutral vs charged reconstruction systematics; Ar+Sc not perfectly isospin-symmetric; origin unidentified. (Extract filename “nuclear magnetization” is a **mismatch** — body is NA61 only.)

### A3 — Theory corner: cold dense pairing

[[color-superconductivity-qcd]]: Cooper’s theorem + attractive color-$\bar{3}$ exchange makes quark pairing at high density essentially inevitable; NS cores are the cold laboratory (2SC, CFL, …). Lattice sign problem blocks a first-principles cold-dense map.

**A-thread tension:** RHIC cumulants probe the **hot, moderate-$\mu_B$** critical region. NA61 $R_K$ probes **isospin in strangeness production** at SPS energies. Color SC is the **cold, high-$\mu_B$** corner. Same phase-diagram cartoon; three different thermometers.

## Thread B — Cold nuclei: EMC, SRCs, and medium structure

**Papers:** [[emc-effect-marathon-a3]], [[nucleus-shell-src-memory]]

### B1 — $A=3$ EMC without large isovector component

[[emc-effect-marathon-a3]] (MARATHON, arXiv:2410.12099): first tritium EMC; $^3$H and $^3$He mirror ratios for $0.20<x<0.83$. Isoscalar slopes for $0.3<x<0.7$ are comparable ($^3$He $-0.085\pm0.037$, $^3$H $-0.10\pm0.04$). Data agree with Kulagin–Petti **isoscalar** off-shell convolution; they **do not** require a sizable isovector EMC of the TEMS type.

**Intuition:** Bound nucleons redraw their PDF traffic maps. Mirror $A=3$ is the cleanest isospin swap — but after isoscalarity corrections the two EMC curves look alike. Medium modification is real; large *p-vs-n different* EMC dressing is not demanded.

### B2 — Shell architecture reaches the hard core

[[nucleus-shell-src-memory]]: Hall C $(e,e'p)$ on $^{40}$Ca, $^{48}$Ca, $^{54}$Fe shows SRC rates track **which orbital is filling**, not only $A$ and $N/Z$. Scale separation between mean-field shells and universal hard-core contacts is incomplete.

**Intuition:** Shell model = register file; SRCs = bumper-to-bumper crashes. The crash rate remembers the orbital geometry that fed it.

**B-thread bridge:** EMC and SRC communities share the “medium modification / high-momentum nucleons” story. MARATHON constrains **parton-level** ratios; CaFe constrains **nucleon-pair** hard rates. A dual-cause EMC–SRC model must survive both — the wiki holds both anchors but not a single joint fit page.

**B vs A isospin note:** NA61 sees **large unexpected isospin breaking** in kaon yields. MARATHON sees **no large isovector EMC** in $A=3$ DIS. Different probes, different energies, different “isospin” questions — do not paste one conclusion onto the other.

## Thread C — Hard scattering and nuclear gluons

**Papers:** [[star-jpsi-spin-interference]], [[high-pt-physics-cern-isr]], [[levinthal-high-pt-isr]] · concepts [[high-pt-scaling]], [[parton-jets]]

[[star-jpsi-spin-interference]]: ultra-peripheral Au+Au J/ψ photoproduction shows spin-dependent interference — two nuclear photon paths as a polarized double slit; angular terms flip sign at the diffractive minimum.

[[high-pt-physics-cern-isr]]: historical ISR high-$p_T$ spectra and correlations open the parton-scattering era (flattening spectra, jet-like companions).

**Intuition:** Hard probes ask “what is pointlike inside the nucleus or proton?” UPC spin interference adds helicity structure to nuclear gluon tomography. ISR is the museum piece that still defines the language of jets ([[parton-jets]], [[high-pt-scaling]]).

**Limits:** UPC systematics and model backgrounds; ISR pre-modern pQCD.

## Thread D — Finite-density QCD probes beyond the beam scan

**Papers:** [[eta-prime-mesic-nucleus]], [[color-superconductivity-qcd]], [[b-meson-fcnc-anomaly]] (flavor, thin overlap)

[[eta-prime-mesic-nucleus]]: semi-exclusive search for η′ bound in a carbon remnant tightens the optical potential without a discovery claim — a density probe of U(1)$_A$ / topological mass.

**Intuition:** Trap a fat topological meson in a nucleus and ask whether the vacuum softens. Coincidence protons kill the quasi-free noise that buried earlier inclusive tries.

**D vs A:** η′-mesic and color SC speak to **hadronic / quark matter at finite density** without walking the RHIC energy ladder. They are theory- and rare-probe anchors, not BES-II replacements.

## Open questions the wiki is positioned to answer

1. **Two isospins?** Can a single QCD isospin-breaking framework accommodate a large NA61 $R_K$ excess and MARATHON’s lack of large isovector EMC, or must the wiki keep “fireball isospin” and “DIS isospin” strictly separate?
2. **EMC–SRC dual cause:** Do orbital-dependent SRC rates ([[nucleus-shell-src-memory]]) quantitatively predict or bound $A=3$ EMC slopes ([[emc-effect-marathon-a3]]) under existing off-shell models?
3. **Critical region baselines:** Which non-critical baseline family (UrQMD, HRG CE, hydro EV, peripheral data) should be the wiki’s default reference when citing the ~2–5σ notch at 19.6 GeV — and how does that choice change the claim strength?
4. **Hot vs cold dense:** What observational bridge (if any) connects BES-II fluctuation structure to color-SC / NS-core EOS handles without overclaiming continuity across the sign-problem wall?
5. **What to ingest next:** Neutron-star radius/tidal precision papers, dilepton continuum fluctuation results, or a nuclear PDF global-analysis page — which gap most strengthens this map?
