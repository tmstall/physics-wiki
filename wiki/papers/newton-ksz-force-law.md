# Newton Was Right Across 230 Mpc (kSZ Force-Law Test)

**One-line summary:** ACT DR6 + BOSS pairwise kinematic SZ velocities measure the gravitational force-law exponent at 30–230 Mpc as \(n = 2.1 \pm 0.3\) — consistent with inverse-square gravity and ~3.3σ from large-scale MOND (\(n \approx 1\)).

## Key claims and results

- **Paper:** Gallardo et al. / ACT collaboration, *Phys. Rev. Lett.* (2026). DOI: 10.1103/rk8v-rcm3.
- Method: mean pairwise halo momentum from kSZ Doppler imprints on the CMB; fit acceleration \(\propto 1/r^n\).
- ~343k galaxies (primary slice \(z \sim 0.44\)–0.66); separations ~30–230 Mpc.
- ΛCDM (\(n=2\)): good fit; MOND large-scale limit (\(n=1\)): poor shape fit (\(\chi^2\) worse).
- Model-light: uses observed \(\xi(r)\) + expanding isotropic universe; floats optical depth \(\bar\tau\).

## Physical intuition

Positions of galaxies tell you *where* structure ended up after a messy history. Pairwise *speeds* tell you how hard gravity is pulling **now**. kSZ is the Big Bang’s radar gun on hot cluster gas. On these scales the slope of that radar readout is the force-law exponent.

## Limitations and assumptions

- MOND external-field effect not modeled (argued negligible at these scales).
- Redshift evolution of \(\xi(r)\) approximated; screening below ~10 Mpc unconstrained.
- Single frequency (150 GHz); tSZ contamination concerns.
- \(\bar\tau\) degeneracy partially shapes constraints.
