# HRV Biofeedback Methods — A Systematic Review of Protocol Heterogeneity

**One-line summary:** Appl Psychophysiol Biofeedback DOI 10.1007/s10484-023-09582-6. “HRV biofeedback” pools three protocols (personal resonance, live device pacing, fixed ~6/min). ~1 in 5 studies checked actual breathing rate; when checked, people often started nearer 8.5 than 6. Building inspection / checklist, not an efficacy verdict.

## Key claims and results

- Lalanza, Lorente, Bullich, García, Losilla & Capdevila (*Appl. Psychophysiol. Biofeedback* 48:275–297, 2023) review methods and reporting in 143 HRV-biofeedback studies from 2000 to April 2021. They do not compare outcomes.
- Three protocols share the name: personal resonance measured first, then trained (37); a device sets the pace from live data (48); everyone breathes at a fixed rate, usually ~6/min, often in one session (51). Nine more are too vague to classify.
- About two-thirds omit enough detail (breath timing, rate control, or posture) to block replication. Only 19 of 88 studies that recorded breathing (~22%) checked the rate people actually hit. About 19% reported the inhale:exhale ratio. Only six rechecked resonance every session.
- Where someone did check, trainees often began nearer 8.5 breaths/min than 6, and reached ~6.3 only by the end of training.
- The paper closes with an eight-point reporting checklist aimed at how the breathing dose was delivered, not at how heart-rate variability was computed.

## Physical intuition

The label sounds like one treatment. It is three. The physiology cares about the exact breathing rate, because that rate is the drive frequency of a delayed blood-pressure reflex (see [[hrv-resonance-baroreflex]]). This review is a building inspection: it checks whether the blueprints name the dose. It does not test whether the building stands. An efficacy meta-analysis that pools the three protocols mixes experiments that may never have delivered the same stimulus.

## Limitations and assumptions

- No verdict on which protocol works. How “maximal” resonance is defined, and how heart-rate variability is analyzed, were left uncoded, so two “personal resonance” studies can still differ.
- Screening counts do not reconcile (262 records, 19 exclusions, 20 additions, yet 282 full texts). Coder agreement is reported for study selection, not for each extracted variable.
- The search closed in April 2021, before consumer wearables multiplied. The “live device” bin hides proprietary algorithms.
- The checklist is proposed, not shown to improve replication. Outcome-related bias domains were dropped on purpose.
- Whether a person’s resonance drifts across sessions is contested in the studies the review cites.
