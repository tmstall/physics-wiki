# Boronate Velcro Synthetic Cells

**One-line summary:** One reversible boronic acid–catechol chemistry simultaneously builds a coacervate interior, armors its surface, and tunes cytomimetic behavior — collapsing multi-step synthetic-cell fabrication into a single pot.

## Key claims and results

- Target object: **membranized coacervate microdroplets (MCMs)** — crowded liquid “cytoplasm” + stabilizing shell.
- Method: mix polymeric boronic acid with cationic and anionic catechols → dynamic zwitterionic polyboronates → spontaneous liquid–liquid phase separation **and** interfacial crosslinking in one step.
- Same bond does three jobs: (1) nucleate interior coacervate, (2) self-write surface armor, (3) remain post-assembly tunable via pH / ratios (dynamic covalent chemistry).
- Practical payoff: combinatorial screening of synthetic-cell architectures becomes feasible (analysis frames as JACS 2026, Fernandez-Megia group lineage).

## Physical intuition

Naked coacervates are great cytoplasm analogs with no package — they fuse, dissolve, and lack surface identity. Prior recipes formed the droplet first, then coated it later. Here the chemistry is Velcro: boronate esters form and break under mild conditions, so the system self-corrects into a dense droplet whose interface welds itself as it forms. One continuous pour instead of three sequential concrete pours.

## Limitations and assumptions

- Boronate esters prefer ~neutral/basic pH; acidic environments break the network (feature for triggered release, bug for stability).
- Catechol oxidation in air is a real materials risk.
- Interior viscoelasticity and compatibility with enzymes / cargo encapsulation need more characterization.
- Firmly protocell-model territory — not in vivo ready.
