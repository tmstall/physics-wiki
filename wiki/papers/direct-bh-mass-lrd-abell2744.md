# Direct Dynamical Black-Hole Mass for a $z\sim7$ Little Red Dot

**One-line summary:** Strong lensing + JWST spectroastrometry/MOKA3D kinematics yield the first direct (non-virial) BH mass in the reionization era for LRD Abell2744-QSO1 ($z=7.04$); $\log(M/M_\odot)\approx7.7$ (~$5\times10^7\,M_\odot$), consistent with virial within a few, with extreme $M_{\rm BH}/M_*$ (*Nature* 2026).

## Key claims and results

- **Paper:** *Nature* **653**, 1017–1021 (2026). DOI [10.1038/s41586-026-10579-4](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-026-10579-4); arXiv companion 2508.21748.
- Target: triply imaged LRD behind Abell 2744; ~3.5× magnification is load-bearing for resolving the sphere of influence.
- Spectroastrometry: narrow Hα ±50 km/s centroids → $r_{\rm spec}\sim12.5$ pc → inclination-uncorrected lower mass $\log M\approx6.90$.
- MOKA3D point-mass fit (inclination ~52°): $\log M=7.7\pm0.3$; NSC/Plummer alternatives disfavored (would need unrealistically dense cores / too much stellar mass).
- Broad+narrow lines and variability establish AGN; dynamics establish compact point mass.
- One object — does not settle the whole LRD mass debate; BH-to-stellar ratio is the sharper surprise.

## Physical intuition

High-$z$ BH masses usually come from a “RPM → speed” conversion (virial). This is the first speedometer reading on a dawn-of-time LRD: lensing stretches the image just enough that gas motion near the hole can be timed. The hole is heavy for its stars — more “black hole first, galaxy later” than the reverse.

## Limitations and assumptions

- Unique lensing geometry; method not trivially repeatable on every LRD.
- Single-object case study; authors frame as first of many needed.
- Point-mass dynamics ≠ automatic “BH” label without the AGN spectral evidence.
- Distinguish spectroastrometric lower limit from headline MOKA3D mass.
