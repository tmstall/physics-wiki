# Dynamic Mirror Breaking in CISS and Life’s Homochirality

**One-line summary:** Paltiel et al. argue that chiral molecules do not merely flip spin-filter *sign* between enantiomers—the *magnitude* of CISS spin polarization is itself asymmetric under transport, providing a dynamical engine that can drive global enantiomeric excess.

## Key claims and results

- **Paper:** Yossi Paltiel et al., *Dynamic Breaking of Mirror Symmetry in Spin-Dependent Electron Transport through Chiral Media Causes Enantiomeric Excesses*, *Science Advances* (2026). DOI: 10.1126/sciadv.aec9325; related arXiv discussion 2601.20475.
- Chiral-Induced Spin Selectivity (CISS): helical molecules spin-filter electrons; standard assumption was equal efficiency, opposite sign for L vs D.
- Claim: non-equilibrium transport + spin-orbit interference makes polarization **efficiency** enantiomer-dependent (not pure mirror).
- Molecular vibrations / decoherence at room temperature amplify rather than erase the asymmetry (dynamical SOC modulation).
- Prebiotic implication: if chemistry couples to spin-polarized electrons (magnetite, UV photoelectrons, …), one hand systematically wins → path to biosphere-scale homochirality.
- Engages unitarity/TRS no-go for single-channel two-terminal spin filters by leaning on multi-channel, inelastic, dynamical SOC.

## Physical intuition

Mirror images of screws look opposite, but if you force a directed current through the threads with spin-orbit “friction,” the two screws are not perfect time-reversed twins. Cross-terms in the SOC Hamiltonian interfere differently—not just with a minus sign—so one hand is a better spin filter. Room-temperature jiggle of the molecule is not pure noise: it modulates that filter asymmetrically. Life’s one-handedness becomes a quantum-transport ratchet, not only a frozen chemical accident.

## Limitations and assumptions

- Bridging from measured CISS asymmetry to full prebiotic ee amplification still requires chemical kinetics and environmental assumptions.
- Single-channel TRS theorems still constrain idealized models; experiment lives in multi-channel, open systems.
- Quantitative ee growth rates over geological time not closed in one paper.
- Field still debates microscopic CISS theory; this is a major proposed mechanism, not settled dogma.
