# LVK O1–O4a Silence, Read as Eight Early-Universe Limits

**One-line summary:** One null cross-correlation spectrum, now including O4a, is refit eight ways and still finds no cosmological gravitational-wave background; the exclusions bite only the loud corners, and several headline numbers depend on which loop model or prior you pick. LIGO–Virgo–KAGRA, *Phys. Rev. D* (2026).

## Key claims and results

- **Paper:** LIGO Scientific Collaboration, Virgo, and KAGRA. DOI [10.1103/dlfv-z6m2](https://doi.org/10.1103/dlfv-z6m2). Constraint paper: no new detector method and no detection. O4a is May 2023–January 2024, Hanford and Livingston only.
- Shared input: the isotropic search spectrum. Flat-spectrum 95% limit about $2.8 \times 10^{-9}$ in $\Omega_{\rm GW}$, about half the O1–O3 number, with most of the sensitivity in 20–58 Hz. Every fit includes an astrophysical merger foreground $\Omega_{\rm ref}(f/25\,\mathrm{Hz})^{2/3}$ plus one cosmological template. All Bayes factors are negative.
- Phase transitions: only strong, slow transitions around $10^8$ GeV and above are touched. Typical motivated models stay open. The generic broken-power-law cap is an amplitude parameter, not the height of the peak.
- Cosmic strings: LIGO leads, near a tension of $10^{-15}$, only for loop models rich in small loops. For the Nambu–Goto-like “model A,” LIGO reaches about $10^{-8}$ and pulsar timing is stronger. For model A with many kinks, O4a slightly *weakens* the O1–O3 limit. If field-theory strings decay to particles instead of waves, these limits mostly vanish.
- Domain walls: annihilation between about $10^7$ and $10^9$ GeV is excluded only when the network nearly dominates. An analyst recomputation puts the benchmark spectrum about five times below the plotted peak; the paper does not state which efficiency the figure used.
- Stiff (kination) eras and axion inflation: real but prior-shaped. In the free energy-scale axion column, the reported Hubble-rate bound is about twice the CMB cap the paper itself quotes, so that column averages in models the CMB already excludes. Use the fixed-scale columns.
- Scalar-induced waves: ripple amplitude capped near a percent for a narrow bump that maps to about 155 Hz. That scale makes ~$10^{12}$ g holes, long evaporated, so this is early-universe ripple physics, not today’s dark matter. At least one table entry at the edge of the band equals the prior.
- Primordial-black-hole binaries: dark-matter fraction below about 1% for 1–100 solar masses, and about 0.4% near 30–100 solar masses, in the early-binary channel. Microlensing is stronger below about 3 solar masses. This is a 4D stellar-mass merger bound. It does not speak to the asteroid-mass ceiling in [[primordial-black-holes-are-5d]].
- Handedness: no constraint. Amplitude limits sit near $2.5 \times 10^{-9}$, slightly tighter for right-handed waves because of the Hanford–Livingston geometry.

## Physical intuition

A stochastic background looks like shared noise. You only see it when two detectors rattle together. Frequency maps to the temperature of the epoch that made the wave, so a 10–1000 Hz band is a $10^7$–$10^{10}$ GeV thermometer. Silence is information only inside a template: the same null, read through a string network or a phase transition, closes different corners. Eight limits from one spectrum are one measurement, not eight confirmations.

## Limitations and assumptions

- Templates are contested (sound-shell spectra, loop distributions, particle decay of strings). The paper names the fights; the limits inherit them.
- Marginalized numbers move when priors move. The merger foreground is a pure two-thirds power law.
- O4a adds one baseline. Correlated magnetic noise is assumed small at this sensitivity.
- The merger hum is already within a factor of a few of population predictions. It will probably be detected before any cosmological piece, and separating the two is the next problem.
