# Entropy Maximization in Black Hole Mergers

**One-line summary:** Mapping a binary’s instantaneous mass and angular momentum to a single Kerr entropy and maximizing that entropy along the inspiral predicts remnant spin to within a few percent of numerical relativity—without a full NR simulation.

## Key claims and results

- **Paper:** *Phys. Rev. Lett.* **137**, 021406 (7 Jul 2026). DOI: 10.1103/hvp6-ydbq.
- Procedure: at each inspiral stage, pretend the system is one Kerr hole with current \(M,J\); compute horizon-area entropy; follow until entropy peaks.
- Peak lands near the true NR remnant spin (equal-mass nonspinning case and extensions).
- Heuristic lock: orbital frequency matching remnant horizon-spin frequency.
- Selected spin sits near a Kerr thermodynamic special point (phase-transition-like).
- Radiated energy / final mass still systematically off (~3–4% class).

## Physical intuition

Two bricks in contact maximize total entropy when temperatures equalize. Two black holes merging may pick their leftover spin the same way: keep shedding waves until the “would-be single hole” entropy stops rising. You get a thermostat estimate of the remnant without running a week of supercomputer GR.

## Limitations and assumptions

- No rigorous binary entropy in GR—Kerr map is a stand-in.
- Ad hoc extremum, not a derived variational principle from Einstein equations.
- Weak-field / PN scaffolding extrapolated into strong field—success may be coincidence.
- Mass loss underpredicted relative to NR.
