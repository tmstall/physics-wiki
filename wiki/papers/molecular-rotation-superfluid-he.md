# Optical Centrifuge of Molecules in Superfluid Helium Nanodroplets

**One-line summary:** An optical centrifuge spins single molecules inside $^4$He nanodroplets (~0.4 K) at dial-a-frequency rates — a tunable atomic-scale stirrer for superfluid response beyond ground-state IR rotors.

## Key claims and results

- First successful optical-centrifuge drive of molecules embedded in superfluid helium nanodroplets (~3000 atoms).
- Prior IR spectroscopy only accessed lowest $J$ at 0.4 K; different molecules mixed probe chemistry with probe frequency.
- Centrifuge sets rotational frequency independently, mapping molecule–superfluid coupling vs frequency (including near the roton band ~180 GHz).
- Separates rotational decoherence from energy relaxation better than pure femtosecond kick protocols.
- Renormalized moments of inertia (He dressing) remain central observables.

## Physical intuition

Drop one molecule into a few-thousand-atom superfluid droplet and it becomes a nanoprobe of the quantum liquid. IR only hears the ground-state rotor hum. An optical centrifuge is a rotating polarization “motor” that grabs the molecule’s polarizable axis and spins it up to a chosen frequency — a synchronous motor inside a frictionless (but frequency-dependent) bath.

## Limitations and assumptions

- Droplet-size and species dependence still limit universality claims.
- Analysis-based ingest; verify achieved $J$ distributions and coupling curves on the primary paper.
- Microscopic theory of roton-resonant dissipation remains model-dependent.
