# Phosphine-Photoredox Markovnikov Hydroamination of Terminal Alkenes

**One-line summary:** Fan et al. (*Nature* 2026) use a triarylphosphine + photoredox pair to generate a P$^{\rm IV}$ radical cation that activates unactivated terminal alkenes for Markovnikov hydroamination with N–H azoles — main-group catalysis succeeding where metals often poison on azoles.

## Key claims and results

- **Paper:** Flora Fan et al., *Nature* (2026), DOI 10.1038/s41586-026-10263-7 (Doyle group, UCLA per analysis).
- Reaction: intermolecular Markovnikov hydroamination of unactivated terminal alkenes with N–H azoles (imidazoles, triazoles, tetrazoles, benzimidazoles, …).
- Catalysis: P$^{\rm III}$/P$^{\rm IV}$ phosphine redox shuttle driven by visible-light photoredox (Ir/Ru-class mediator under blue LED).
- Mechanistic core: excited photocatalyst oxidizes PR$_3$ → PR$_3^{\bullet+}$; the radical cation electrophilically polarizes the alkene (P adds toward the terminal carbon); azole N attacks the more substituted carbon; H transfer closes the product and regenerates P$^{\rm III}$.
- Scope (analysis): yields $\sim50$–$90\%$, regioselectivity $\gtrsim20:1$ Markovnikov; functional-group tolerance for esters, halides, ketones in robustness screens.
- Support: radical probes, isotope labeling (H from azole N–H), DFT of the PR$_3^{\bullet+}$–alkene transition-state polarization.
- Contrast: prior phosphine photoredox often targets anti-Markovnikov modes; here P$^{\rm IV}$ activation enables metal-like pi-acid polarization without metal–azole poisoning.

## Physical intuition

Transition metals glue alkenes by pi-coordination, then insert N. Azoles often poison that glue. Phosphorus steals a page from photoredox: light makes a sticky P$^{\rm IV}$ radical cation that tags the double bond, polarizes the internal carbon, and lets the azole bite Markovnikov-style — then the electron is paid back and the phosphine walks away. Main-group element as temporary metal understudy.

## Limitations and assumptions

- Terminal alkenes and acidic N–H azoles only in the reported scope; aliphatic amines and internal alkenes largely out.
- Photoredox scale-up (light penetration, mixing) and Ir cost remain practical issues.
- Catalyst loadings not ultra-low; organic-dye replacements not yet standard.
- Asymmetric / chiral-phosphine versions open.
- Analysis-based ingest; verify yields, loadings, and elementary steps against primary *Nature* paper.
