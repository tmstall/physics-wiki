# Inclination Is Destiny (SMBH Pairing Angle Filter)

**One-line summary:** For post-merger disk galaxies, the orbital tilt of a secondary supermassive black hole relative to the host disk is a hard filter: \(\lesssim 20^\circ\) favors merger within a few Gyr; \(\gtrsim 45^\circ\) locks the pair into everlasting high-inclination stall.

## Key claims and results

- **Work:** Ghobadi et al. (per analysis)—3D semi-analytic dynamical-friction study across many host configurations and ~12 orbital tilts.
- Kiloparsec-scale pairing is driven by dynamical friction against stars/gas; inclination modulates how often the secondary samples the dense disk.
- **\(\lesssim 20^\circ\):** disk drag damps tilt → orbit settles into the plane → pairing on Gyr timescales.
- **\(\gtrsim 45^\circ\):** dynamical phase transition—disk torques **amplify** tilt rather than damp it → cosmological stall.
- Intermediate 20–45° band is sensitive to BH mass, disk spin, and gas content; heavier BHs and faster disks help.
- Observational filter: dual AGN and PTA GW progenitors preferentially sample low-inclination merger channels; many inclined pairs may still be frozen mid-inspiral.

## Physical intuition

Imagine sliding a heavy puck across a spinning vinyl record: if the path is nearly coplanar, the rough surface brakes and flattens the orbit; if you drop in almost edge-on, the record’s pull can throw the orbit into a steeper wobble. Same drag physics decides whether two galactic black holes ever meet.

## Limitations and assumptions

- Semi-analytic fixed potential—not live N-body/SPH; post-merger disks are clumpy and evolving.
- Stopping criterion ~10 pc is *pairing*, not final merger; last-parsec physics not solved here.
- No loss-cone depletion or circumbinary-disk stage.
- Disk-host morphology only; pure ellipticals lack a well-defined inclination parameter.
- Threshold angles will shift with bulge/disk ratio, density profile, and gas fraction.
