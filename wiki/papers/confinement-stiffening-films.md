# Confinement Stiffening: Inverse-Cube Ballistic Toughness

**One-line summary:** LIPIT ballistic tests show specific penetration energy of ultrathin films (graphene, GO, polymers) *rises* as thickness falls, with a shared inverse-cube thickness scaling explained by nonaffine elasticity under confinement (Zaccone & Sirk, PNAS / arXiv framing).

## Key claims and results

- Classical plate bending ($t^3$ stiffness) predicts thinner → weaker; data show thinner → higher specific penetration energy into the tens-of-nm regime.
- Same qualitative trend across chemically unrelated materials (covalent 2D crystal, disordered GO, glassy polymers).
- Mechanism: disordered-solid elasticity — nonaffine relaxations soften thick samples; confinement suppresses those soft modes as $t$ drops.
- Unified exponent / scaling collapses multi-material LIPIT datasets (analysis framing).
- Companion extract title “Why Thinner Is Tougher” is the same paper narrative — **not** a second wiki page.

## Physical intuition

A thick disordered film has room for atoms to shuffle sideways and dodge a hit (nonaffine soft modes). Squeeze the film into a few-dozen-nanometer slab and those escape routes freeze out — the network is forced to respond more affinely and stiffer per mass. Thinner is tougher because confinement kills the soft degrees of freedom, not because the material gains magic bonds.

## Limitations and assumptions

- Ballistic (high-rate) regime; quasi-static toughness may differ.
- Analysis-based ingest; verify exact exponent and materials list on the primary PNAS paper.
- Defects, substrate, and multi-layer stacking can modulate the ideal scaling.
