# Quantum Metallurgy: Melting Electron Crystals in 2D TaS₂

**One-line summary:** In-situ electron diffraction shows the incommensurate CDW in 1T-TaS₂ melts continuously through a hexatic route — wavelength expands, amplitude collapses — establishing defect-mediated “quantum metallurgy” as generic for 2D incommensurate charge crystals.

## Key claims and results

- **Paper:** Hovden, Shen, Sung et al., *Matter* (7 May 2026) — “Quantum metallurgy: Electron crystals deform and melt.”
- Temperature ramp 408–571 K on monolayer 1T-TaS₂; three signatures: azimuthal peak broaden ~3.5× (hexatic), \(q\) contracts ~14%, integrated intensity halves.
- Grand-canonical Monte Carlo + nonlinear elasticity: dislocations force wavelength expansion and local amplitude collapse inside a rigid atomic cage.
- Meta-analysis: same continuous melting pattern across ~28 incommensurate CDW systems.

## Physical intuition

Electrons form a traffic-density crystal inside a fixed atomic parking lot. When dislocations open space, cars cannot expand the lot walls — so peaks must flatten (amplitude drop) as spacing grows. Melting is continuous: first hexatic (angles ok, positions messy), then isotropic liquid — 2D KTHNY, not 3D first-order jump.

## Limitations and assumptions

- Incommensurate CDWs only; diffraction averages, not real-space single dislocations.
- Substrate-supported 2D; equilibrium thermal only — device drive of defects open.
- Phenomenological Monte Carlo, not full band/phonon Hamiltonian.
