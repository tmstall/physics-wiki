# Rapid Orbital Decay in eRASSU J060839 (Ultracompact DD Binary)

**One-line summary:** Two WDs with P≈6.2 min; orbital decay matches GR GW radiation; 3.5 yr X-ray phase clock across telescopes; spectral T variation maps extended accretion impact structure. ApJL DOI 10.3847/2041-8213/ae8cf7.

## Key claims and results

- Ultracompact double-degenerate binary eRASSU J060839.5–704014: $P = 374.15013(2)$ s (~6.2 min), $\dot{P} = -4.7(1)\times10^{-11}$ s/s — largest orbital decay yet in this class.
- Phase-connected timing over 3.5 years stitches archival XMM-Newton with NICER and Einstein Probe across a ~750-day gap; NICER+EP-only fit extrapolates cleanly to the XMM epoch.
- Mass-transfer angular-momentum loss is ~5 orders of magnitude smaller than GW loss; decay is attributed to gravitational radiation → chirp-mass upper limit $M_c\sim0.43\,M_\odot$.
- Phase-averaged spectra are supersoft absorbed blackbodies ($kT\sim126$–144 eV); no strong lines.
- Phase-resolved spectroscopy: temperature, normalization, and flux rise and fall together across the bright half — extended impact zone with a real temperature gradient, not a fixed-T hotspot (contrast with HM Cnc).

## Physical intuition

At these separations there is no room for an accretion disk: gas free-falls and slams the accretor (direct-impact accretion). The soft X-ray pulse is a clock. Track its phase for years; curvature means the orbital frequency is rising — the two dead stars spiral in as they radiate quadrupole GW power. Watching how the spectrum’s temperature breathes across the orbit maps the splash zone without spatially resolving the system.

## Limitations and assumptions

- Distance is unconstrained (LMC foreground only); luminosity, accretion rate, and DI vs unipolar-inductor preference all depend on it.
- Chirp mass is an upper limit under the assumption that GW emission is the sole angular-momentum sink.
- ~374 s period is identified as orbital by analogy to HM Cnc / V407 Vul — no independent radial-velocity orbit yet.
- Early EP epochs with unexplained phase offset were excluded; raw timing fit needed a phenomenological jitter term ($\chi^2_\nu\sim7.9\to\sim1$).
