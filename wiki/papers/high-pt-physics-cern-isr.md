# High-\(p_T\) Physics at the CERN ISR

**One-line summary:** Mid-1970s ISR measurements of inclusive high transverse momentum production and correlations — spectrum flattening to \(\sim p_T^{-5.5}\), jet-like charged companions, and symmetric pair triggers that open parton-scattering kinematics.

## Key claims and results

- Experiment: Levinthal et al., CERN ISR, \(\sqrt{s} = 31.5\), 45, 62.4 GeV; lead-glass EM calorimeters + magnetic spectrometer.
- Inclusive \(\pi^0\)-dominated spectra to \(\sim 15\) GeV/\(c\): power-law softens from \(\sim p_T^{-8}\) at low \(p_T\) toward \(\sim p_T^{-5.5}\) at high \(p_T\).
- Associated charged particles show collimation / momentum balance consistent with jet-like fragmentation.
- Symmetric back-to-back high-\(p_T\) pair trigger → approximate pair rest-frame mass and angular distributions of the hard scatter, less dependent on fragmentation models.
- Early experimental support for hard scattering of pointlike constituents inside protons — groundwork for the QCD jet era.

## Physical intuition

Soft hadron traffic mostly sprays along the beams. High \(p_T\) is a sideways kick that soft processes rarely produce — unless two hard “trucks” (partons) collide inside the protons. Flattening spectra and jet-like companions were the first hardware-level profile showing those rare hard events in \(pp\) collisions.

## Limitations and assumptions

- Pre-modern pQCD (no full running coupling / gluon-jet machinery).
- Large systematics from energy scale, resolution, coil corrections on a steep spectrum.
- Jet “ID” via correlations, not modern algorithms.
- \(\sqrt{s} \lesssim 62\) GeV only.
