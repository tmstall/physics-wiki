# Category 79 Quasar Wind (J2318)

**One-line summary:** SDSS quasar J2318 at $z\approx2.68$ hosts the fastest UV-detected broad absorption-line wind on record — C IV / Si IV troughs at ~0.26–0.3$c$ that strengthen over rest-frame years — pushing kinetic feedback at or above the conventional galaxy-impact threshold under conservative assumptions.

## Key claims and results

- Object found in archival SDSS spectra (undergraduate pattern recognition); redshift $z=2.6781\pm0.0004$ confirmed with Gemini near-IR H$\alpha$ (critical because it is a weak-lined quasar).
- UV BAL troughs identified as C IV and Si IV at blueshifts corresponding to ~77,000 to $\gtrsim$90,000 km/s (relativistic Doppler conversion). Blue edge may be truncated by spectral coverage.
- Black-hole mass $\sim1.65\times10^9\,M_\odot$ (virial); Eddington ratio $\sim0.45$ — high, but not alone enough to explain the extreme UV velocity.
- C IV trough strengthens over ~2.2 rest-frame years across epochs; multi-decade photometry shows ~0.5 mag g-band variability.
- Conservative kinetic luminosity lower bound $L_{\rm kin}/L_{\rm bol}\gtrsim0.75\%$ (mass-loss lower limit ~0.8 $M_\odot$/yr) sits at the usual ~0.5–1% feedback threshold; analogy to PDS 456 suggests true power could be far larger.
- Puzzle: ions that produce UV lines should normally be stripped at the radiation field needed to drive 0.3$c$ — weak-lined / soft continuum may be why the UV wind is visible at all.

## Physical intuition

A quasar is a black hole’s accretion disk as a radiation firehose. Some of that light drives gas outward; when the gas sits in our line of sight, it carves blueshifted notches in the UV spectrum — a radar gun for outflow speed. X-ray ultrafast outflows already hit ~0.3$c$, but UV troughs add ion chemistry. J2318 is the UV record-holder: carbon and silicon still partially clothed while racing at a sizable fraction of light speed, and the notches deepen while we watch. Weak emission lines, usually a nuisance, clean the continuum under the troughs and may signal a softer ionizing spectrum that lets those ions survive.

## Limitations and assumptions

- Analysis-based ingest; verify velocities, virial mass, and kinetic-luminosity assumptions against the primary paper.
- Covering fraction, launch radius, and true mass-loss rate remain model-dependent; quoted kinetic ratio is a lower limit under conservative geometry.
- Weak-lined redshift solution depends on NIR confirmation quality.
- Single object; comparison to PDS 456 is analogy, not a matched multiwavelength campaign on J2318.
