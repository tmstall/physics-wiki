# Supernova $^{60}$Fe in Antarctic Ice Maps the Local Interstellar Cloud

**One-line summary:** Koll et al. (*PRL* 2026) measure time-variable $^{60}$Fe influx in 40–80 kyr Antarctic ice lower than modern snow, dating Solar System entry into the Local Interstellar Cloud and showing that cloud still carries supernova ash with internal structure.

## Key claims and results

- **Paper:** Dominik Koll et al., *Physical Review Letters* **136**, 19 (2026), DOI 10.1103/nxjq-jwgp.
- Isotope: $^{60}$Fe (half-life ~2.62 Myr) — only known natural production is core-collapse supernova nucleosynthesis.
- Sample: ~295–300 kg EPICA Dronning Maud Land ice, 40–80 kyr before present; AMS at ANU HIAF after chemical purification.
- $^{10}$Be/$^{26}$Al monitors validate chemical recovery.
- $^{60}$Fe flux in 40–80 kyr ice **lower** than recent surface snow and younger sediment archives.
- Interpretation: Sun entered the LIC tens of kyr ago; modern $^{60}$Fe is dominated by cloud material still holding ancient SN ejecta, not a fresh local SN.
- Time variation maps LIC internal density/enrichment structure along the Solar trajectory (~20 km s$^{-1}$).

## Physical intuition

The Solar System is driving through a fog bank (the Local Interstellar Cloud) that was seeded long ago by supernova smoke. $^{60}$Fe is a still-smoldering fuse from that explosion. Antarctic ice is a strip chart: each layer is a year of windshield collection. Older ice shows less $^{60}$Fe than today → we entered a richer patch of the cloud tens of thousands of years ago, and the tape is still rolling.

## Limitations and assumptions

- One core, one site; dating uncertainties blur exact entry time.
- Atom counts are tiny; AMS systematics and deposition efficiency matter.
- Which supernova and how well mixed remains open.
- Pre-entry baseline needs older ice (Beyond EPICA).
- Analysis-based ingest; verify fluxes and ages against primary PRL.
