# LZ High-Energy Nuclear Recoils under Dark-Matter EFT

**One-line summary:** LZ reanalyzes the same 220-day, 2.84 t·yr xenon exposure with ROI extended to ~270 keV NR and 293 NREFT/inelastic models; one ~248 keV event of interest yields ≤3.4σ local / **2.6σ global** — anomaly to watch, not a discovery (LZ preprint draft `260901`, no DOI/arXiv yet).

## Key claims and results

- Same dataset as prior low-energy EFT result; window from ~80 keV → ~270 keV NR; model catalog ≫ SI/SD only.
- Motivation: momentum/velocity-dependent and inelastic operators can peak at high recoil energy; low-energy-only searches are blind to them.
- Supporting work: NEST charge-yield extension stitching D-D and AmBe calibrations; high-energy ER backgrounds.
- One science-sample event (16 Jun 2023): $248\pm23\,{\rm (stat)}\pm23\,{\rm (sys)}$ keV; NR-like but sparsely calibrated region.
- Artifact hunts (glitches, calibration proximity, double scatters, pulse shape) inconclusive.
- Blind salting did not fully cover the new high-energy window — acknowledged bias-mitigation gap.
- Continued running under same conditions is the near-term falsifier.

## Physical intuition

Standard WIMP searches stare at soft kicks. Some more general interaction “opcodes” prefer harder kicks — or, for inelastic DM, forbid soft ones until a mass-splitting threshold is paid. LZ opened the high-energy drawer on data it already had and found one odd event: interesting, far from 5σ.

## Limitations and assumptions

- Unreviewed collaboration preprint — adversarial posture; treat as provisional.
- Single event; global 2.6σ after look-elsewhere over 293 models.
- High-energy NR/ER discrimination and salting coverage are soft spots.
- Degeneracy reduction 616→293 models can move the global significance slightly.
