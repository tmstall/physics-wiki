# Extraordinary Momentum and Spin in Evanescent Waves

**One-line summary:** Bliokh, Bekshaev & Nori (*Nature Communications*) show that evanescent electromagnetic waves carry **transverse** Belinfante spin momentum and helicity-dependent transverse force components that vanish in bulk plane waves — a classical optics window onto field-theory spin–orbit structure.

## Key claims and results

- **Paper:** Konstantin Y. Bliokh, Aleksandr Y. Bekshaev, Franco Nori, *Nature Communications* (evanescent-wave momentum and spin program).
- Propagating plane waves: orbital momentum along $\mathbf{k}$ (polarization-independent); spin along $\mathbf{k}$ (helicity-dependent); Belinfante spin momentum density forms closed loops that **cancel** net contribution.
- Evanescent waves (total internal reflection / near-field decay $e^{-\kappa x}$): inhomogeneity prevents loop cancellation → **transverse spin** (even for linear polarization) and **transverse momentum** components that flip with helicity — roles reversed vs bulk.
- Canonical (orbital) longitudinal momentum can exceed the usual $\propto\omega$ scale associated with phase velocity along the interface.
- Probe particles: Rayleigh dipoles feel gradient forces + orbital momentum and torques from spin; Mie particles ($ka\sim1$) couple to Belinfante $p_S$ and show helicity-dependent **transverse force** measurable in TIR geometries.
- Clarifies Fedorov–Imbert transverse beam shifts as interference effects rather than single-mode “superluminal” momentum lore alone.

## Physical intuition

In a uniform laser beam the hidden spin-momentum eddies cancel like closed current loops. Pin the wave to an interface and force it to die sideways (evanescent decay): those eddies no longer cancel, so a sideways spin and a polarization-dependent sideways push appear. Near-field optics becomes a tabletop lab for Belinfante’s old QFT bookkeeping of field spin vs orbital momentum.

## Limitations and assumptions

- Idealized monochromatic interface modes; real beams mix propagating + evanescent continua.
- Force/torque predictions sensitive to particle size, absorption, and multipoles — Mie numerics required beyond Rayleigh.
- Does not by itself rewrite far-field optical angular momentum applications; the novelty is near-field exposure of $p_S$.
