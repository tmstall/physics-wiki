# Second-Order Cosmological GW Strain: Newton Gauge Wins

**One-line summary:** Computing what freely falling observers actually measure via light-pulse timing at second order shows the physical GW strain is the transverse-traceless metric in **Newton gauge**—settling a long gauge debate for induced cosmological gravitational waves.

## Key claims and results

- **Paper:** *Phys. Rev.* (2026). DOI: 10.1103/pwbs-xwrh (per analysis).
- Problem: at second order, TT metric pieces are **gauge-dependent**—different coordinates disagree on the induced GW signal.
- Method: gauge-invariant observable = light-travel timing between geodesic observers (ideal PTA / free-fall interferometer idealization).
- Result: that observable equals the TT components computed in Newton gauge.
- The notorious “frozen mode” is physical **GW memory**, not a coordinate artifact.
- Puts scalar-induced stochastic GW calculations that use Newton gauge on firmer observational footing.

## Physical intuition

A detector does not read a coordinate table—it times light between free-falling clocks. If every honest stopwatch agrees, that number is the strain. This paper runs that stopwatch at second order and finds it matches Newton-gauge TT waves. The “frozen” leftover offset is memory: a permanent stretch, like a step that stays after the ripple passes.

## Limitations and assumptions

- Freely falling observers only—not LIGO suspensions yet.
- Scalar×scalar second-order terms emphasized; scalar–tensor / tensor–tensor incomplete.
- Idealized cosmology; detector response functions for real PTAs still need folding in.
