# Intentional Phase Separation in HfNbTaTiZr HEA

**One-line summary:** A nominally uniform refractory high-entropy alloy is driven to self-sort into a coherent nanoscale mosaic of three crystal structures, roughly doubling strength — HEA as metastable *precursor*, not finished uniform product.

## Key claims and results

- Alloy: equiatomic HfNbTaTiZr (BCC refractory HEA lineage).
- Processing coaxes coherent multi-phase nanoscale mosaic rather than single random solid solution.
- Strength gain ~2× (analysis framing) via engineered heterogeneity (Hall–Petch / interface blocking).
- Design principle: use high configurational entropy to *store* a uniform precursor, then intentionally “fall apart” into hierarchical defects.

## Physical intuition

HEA folklore optimizes uniformity. Classical metallurgy optimizes obstacles to dislocations. This work does both in sequence: mix thoroughly first, then let the alloy phase-separate into a 3D nano-mosaic you could not mill into bulk any other way. Entropy buys the blank canvas; controlled decomposition paints the strengthening pattern.

## Limitations and assumptions

- Analysis-based ingest; verify strength numbers and phase IDs on the primary paper.
- Thermal stability of the mosaic under service temperatures open.
- Scalability of the heat/mech path to large components open.
