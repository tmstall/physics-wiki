# Radio Changing-Look AGN (SDSS J1105+1452)

**One-line summary:** Narrow-line Seyfert 1 SDSS J1105+1452 jumped from ~1.4 mJy to ~39 mJy at 1.4 GHz (radio-loud) between 1999 and 2017 and held that level for years with almost no multiwavelength twin signature — the first long-duration “radio changing-look” AGN.

## Key claims and results

- Object: NLS1 at $z\approx0.12$; BH mass ~3–$4\times10^6\,M_\odot$, near-Eddington accretor (local analog of fast-growing early BHs).
- Radio flux rose more than ~20× and stayed elevated ≥8 years; classification flips quiet→loud and *stays* loud.
- Multiwavelength campaign finds little optical/X-ray “outburst twin,” so not a standard flaring CL-AGN copy.
- Coins / solidifies the class term **radio changing-look AGN** by elimination of common jet-outburst scenarios.
- Jet-launching physics around low-mass, high-accretion NLS1s is the open theoretical stake.

## Physical intuition

Optical and X-ray changing-look AGN already dismantle neat type bins. Radio loudness was supposed to be the stable one — either the hole launches a relativistic jet or it does not. J1105 shows a quiet core that flipped its radio switch on and left it locked, with almost no companion fireworks. Think of a CPU that suddenly enables a high-speed serial link and keeps the PHY powered for years while the rest of the chip’s voltage rails barely twitch.

## Limitations and assumptions

- Single-object case study; class needs a statistical sample.
- Sparse historical radio sampling can miss intermediate states.
- Jet power, opening angle, and beaming still model-dependent.
- Analysis-based ingest; verify light curves against the primary paper.
