# Phantom-Halo Surface Density in Verlinde Gravity

**One-line summary:** SSRN 7180683 (preprint). Verlinde+Yoon quadrature reproduces the observed halo surface-density scale (MOND already did, roughly). Buried result: the same relation predicts a cusp, not a core — magnitude matches, shape does not. Single-author modification, limited prior tests.

## Key claims and results

- Short letter by Yoon. ScienceDirect abstract and SSRN 7180683; the PDF body was not retrieved (lite analysis). It applies the author’s 2024 replacement of Verlinde’s straight sum $g=g_B+g_D$ by the quadrature $g=\sqrt{g_B^2+g_D^2}$.
- Target: the Donato et al. (2009) halo surface-density scale. Cored (Burkert) fits give about $141\,M_\odot/{\rm pc}^2$ ($10^{2.15\pm 0.2}$), roughly constant across 14 magnitudes in luminosity.
- In the window where local acceleration is well above Milgrom’s scale, the quadrature phantom halo gives $r\cdot\rho_{\rm ph}=10^{2.24\pm 0.09}\,M_\odot/{\rm pc}^2\approx 174\,M_\odot/{\rm pc}^2$ — about 23% high, inside Donato’s scatter. MOND already lands in the same ballpark from $a_M/G$.
- Buried result, caught in revision: the spherical mass–density identity forces the phantom density to rise as $1/r$ near the center. A cusp, not the Burkert core the number is compared against. Magnitude matches; shape does not.
- Prior test of the same quadrature law: one wide-binary ratio (predicted 1.29 versus observed 1.37). No outside group has reproduced either step.

## Physical intuition

Donato’s product of core density and core radius is a mass per area that barely cares which galaxy you picked. Cold dark matter has no obvious reason for that conspiracy. MOND has one acceleration scale, so a surface density falls out. Verlinde’s “phantom” halo is built to look MOND-like at low acceleration; Yoon combines the baryonic pull and the phantom pull in root-sum-square, like two uncorrelated noise sources, instead of adding them. Hitting the number is mostly expected. Differentiating the enclosed phantom mass is the real prediction: the inner density cusps. You can match the area-density scale and still draw the wrong radial profile.

## Limitations and assumptions

- Lite basis: abstract, section openings, and a popular-science note. The derivation, and the 2024 argument for quadrature rather than some other fix, were not in hand.
- The “constant” surface density is contested. The paper’s own introduction cites work tying it to luminosity, halo mass, morphology, color, or gas, and work questioning a universal acceleration scale.
- The calculation holds near, but not extremely close to, the center, and only where the local acceleration is well above about $10^{-10}\,{\rm m\,s^{-2}}$. The innermost cusp and the deep low-acceleration regime are outside the claim.
- The magnitude match is close to relabeling a known coincidence. The falsifiable piece is the cusp, against inner-profile measurements in dwarfs and low-surface-brightness galaxies.
- Two stacked single-author steps, previously checked on one dataset. One point in a modified-gravity program, not a vetted result.
