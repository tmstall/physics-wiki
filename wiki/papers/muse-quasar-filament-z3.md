# MUSE Lyα Filament Between a $z\approx3.22$ Quasar Pair

**One-line summary:** 142 h of VLT/MUSE in the MUDF maps a glowing Lyα filament (~250 pkpc between quasars) connecting a close pair at $z\approx3.22$, with CGM→IGM break at the virial radius (~110 pkpc) and densities matching IllustrisTNG filament expectations (Tornotti et al., *Nature Astronomy* 2025).

## Key claims and results

- **Paper:** Davide Tornotti et al., *Nature Astronomy* **9**, 577 (2025), DOI 10.1038/s41550-024-02463-w.
- Depth: surface brightness ~$4.5\times10^{-20}$ erg s$^{-1}$ cm$^{-2}$ arcsec$^{-2}$ (30 Å narrowband around redshifted Lyα).
- Morphology: bright nebulae around each QSO + fainter filamentary bridge; ~250 pkpc between quasars, total structure ~700 pkpc projected.
- Broken power-law profiles: steep CGM-like decline then filament plateau; transition $R_t\sim117\pm8$ pkpc (QSO1) and $107\pm10$ pkpc (QSO2) ≈ virial radius for ~$2$–$3\times10^{12}\,M_\odot$ halos.
- Transverse filament width $\lesssim140$ pkpc; power-law index $-0.74\pm0.15$.
- IllustrisTNG pair analogs: Lyα-inferred densities within ~factor 2–3 of simulated filament gas once modest emission physics adjustments allowed.

## Physical intuition

CDM builds a highway network of filaments; galaxies grow at the interchanges. Most of that gas is too faint to see until a quasar floodlight ionizes hydrogen and Lyα paints the mist. MUSE’s ultra-deep cube turns one lucky twin-beacon system into a density laboratory: you can watch the steep city-limits (CGM) hand off to the flatter interstate (IGM filament) right where the virial radius should sit — and check that the highway traffic density matches the simulations.

## Limitations and assumptions

- Single system; cosmic variance unknown.
- Filament near 2σ noise floor; small-scale structure uncertain.
- Lyα radiative transfer approximated; full Monte Carlo would tighten simulation matches.
- Powering mix (fluorescence vs collisional excitation vs shocks) not unique.
- Analysis-based ingest; verify profile numbers against primary paper.
