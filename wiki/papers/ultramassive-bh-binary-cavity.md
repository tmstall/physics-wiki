# Ultramassive BH Binary Carves a Starless Kiloparsec Cavity

**One-line summary:** JWST + VLT data show Abell 402’s BCG central “gap” is a genuine starless ~1 kpc cavity scoured by three-body slingshots of an ~60-billion-solar-mass black-hole binary mid-hardening—the heaviest such binary candidate footprint yet.

## Key claims and results

- **Paper:** McDonald et al., *ApJL* (23 Apr 2026) (per analysis); system A402-BCG at \(z\sim 0.322\).
- Large flattened core (~2.2 kpc) plus sharper ~1 kpc stellar cavity; ~2×10⁹ \(M_\odot\) of “missing” stars in cavity scale.
- JWST mid-IR + HST: cavity is empty of stars, not dust-hidden light.
- Dual AGN signatures (mid-IR point source + opposite emission-line AGN with ~370 km/s offset) → bound pair interpretation.
- Total BH mass ~6×10¹⁰ \(M_\odot\) class—record ultramassive binary candidate.
- Core scouring by hard binary three-body ejections explains flat BCG cores vs cuspy pure-merger predictions.

## Physical intuition

Two wrecking balls chained together in a parking lot: every star that wanders close gets flung out. Over time the center is scoured flat. JWST’s IR eyes settle the old doubt—“is the gap just dust?”—and leave pure dynamical vandalism by the heaviest binary we can currently point to.

## Limitations and assumptions

- Binary bound state inferred from dual AGN geometry/kinematics, not full orbital solution.
- Mass scale model-dependent; projection and AGN duty cycle matter.
- Cavity phase is short-lived—selection favors catching rare moments.
- Dark-matter response to scouring less constrained than stars.
