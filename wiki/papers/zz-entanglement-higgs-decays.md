# ZZ Boson-Pair Entanglement in Higgs Decays

**One-line summary:** First evidence ZZ from Higgs decay are entangled as SM predicts (~4.7σ vs specific alternative; model-independent tomography still weak); one-experiment (await CMS). DOI 10.1103/y1nh-1b82.

## Key claims and results

- ATLAS H → ZZ* → 4ℓ (Run 2 140 fb⁻¹ + partial Run 3 164 fb⁻¹): first direct evidence of entanglement between two massive spin-1 bosons at the electroweak scale.
- Joint ZZ* helicity state is a coherent superposition of |+−⟩, |00⟩, |−+⟩; entanglement-sensitive SDM coefficients $C_{2,1,2,-1}$ and $C_{2,2,2,-2}$ estimated from lepton decay angles.
- Direct coefficient measurement: consistent with SM (−0.97, 0.64) but statistics-limited (~1.6σ from separable zero individually).
- Hypothesis test: SM entangled (QE) vs pure longitudinal |00⟩ (only CP-consistent separable alternative) rejects separable null at **4.7σ** (4.9σ expected); $m_{Z_2}$ split adds ~15% power.
- Not a Bell test — establishes non-separability within QM, not local-hidden-variable exclusion.

## Physical intuition

Entanglement usually means cold photons and ions. Here the “registers” are two Z helicities (three-valued qutrits) born in a Higgs decay that lasts ~10⁻²⁵ s. You never read the spins directly: chiral weak decays map helicity into lepton flight angles, and the joint angular pattern across thousands of events reveals whether the 9×9 density matrix factors. Off-diagonal coherences are the tell — classical correlation alone cannot fake them. The loud 4.7σ claim comes from asking “SM entangled or pure |00⟩?”, not from a fully model-free tomography of every separable alternative.

## Limitations and assumptions

- Headline significance assumes a CP-even scalar Higgs and restricts the separable null to |00⟩; transverse-only separable states are excluded by CP, not by this dataset alone.
- Direct SDM tomography is still weak — neither coefficient alone proves entanglement at high significance.
- Same-flavor interference correction (~10%) is simulation-derived with a 50%-of-correction systematic; no fully data-driven cross-check of that term.
- ATLAS-only; no CMS replication yet (unlike top-pair entanglement, where both experiments published).
- Deliberately not a Bell-inequality test.
