# GW-Induced Freeze-In of Fermionic Dark Matter

**One-line summary:** A stochastic gravitational-wave background breaks conformal invariance and produces massless Weyl fermions at one loop; after they acquire mass, they freeze in as dark matter whose relic density tracks the GW spectrum.

## Key claims and results

- **Paper:** Azadeh Maleknejad & Joachim Kopp, *Phys. Rev. Lett.* **136**, 13 (2026). DOI: 10.1103/lr69-45v8 (analysis; related arXiv:2405.09723).
- Smooth FLRW expansion alone cannot produce massless fermions (conformal no-go). A **stochastic GW background** adds anisotropic metric perturbations that break that protection.
- One-loop in-in calculation: cubic graviton–fermion vertex dominates for unpolarized GWB; quartic contribution cancels by symmetry.
- Phenomenological broken-power-law GW spectrum → fermion energy density scales with GW peak frequency (roughly fourth power) and GW energy density at production.
- Fermions later acquire mass via unspecified BSM mechanism → freeze-in DM, potentially lighter than superheavy gravitino/Wimpzilla gravitational production.

## Physical intuition

Massless Weyl fermions in a uniformly expanding universe are like a ship on perfectly calm, evenly rising water — no wave stress, no particle production. A stochastic GWB is chop: random anisotropic ripples that the fermion field *does* feel. Loop processes mix positive and negative frequencies; pairs pop out of the vacuum. Later, give them mass and they become the cold dark matter inventory.

## Limitations and assumptions

- Analytical estimates; spectrum model is simplified.
- Mass-generation scale and mechanism left free.
- No thermal-bath coupling or GW back-reaction.
- Chiral GWB / dark-sector asymmetry flagged, not computed.
