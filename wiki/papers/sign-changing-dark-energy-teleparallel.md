# Sign-Changing Dark Energy in Teleparallel Gravity

**One-line summary:** arXiv:2410.23068. A popular torsion-gravity model has two parameter branches with the same matter today; the neglected branch has dark energy that is negative and turns positive ~9 Gyr ago. That branch can satisfy the no-ghost condition, so "negative energy is unphysical" does not automatically apply to this geometric effective fluid. It overshoots $H_0$, misses low-$z$ BAO, and has structure-growth trouble. Not a fit to the data.

## Key claims and results

- Akarsu et al., *Phys. Rev. D* **112**, 083532 (2025), arXiv:2410.23068. Exponential infrared $f(T)$: one parameter tied to today's matter fraction by a two-valued equation. Same matter today, two histories.
- Positive branch (parameter near $0.4$): the previously studied mild phantom. CMB-anchored $H_0 \approx 72$ km/s/Mpc, within about one error bar of SH0ES. Cosmological gravity only ~12% stronger than Newton.
- Negative branch (parameter near $-3.2$, and $-3.7$ once CMB-anchored): effective dark energy is large and negative early, crosses zero at redshift $\sim 1.45$ (~9 Gyr ago), and is positive today. The no-ghost slope stays positive automatically, unlike the positive branch.
- Anchoring holds two Planck numbers fixed (distance to last scattering and the physical matter density) and solves for $H_0$. No likelihood. The Lambda-free negative branch lands at $H_0 \approx 84.5$–$85$, about eleven standard deviations above SH0ES. Radiation bookkeeping moves that by ~1 unit, not by ten.
- Adding a cosmological constant (one extra parameter) can hit SH0ES or Planck's $H_0$. Two highlighted sign-changing cases stop accelerating about a billion years ago. That is a visual resemblance to a DESI reconstruction, not a fit, and those curves still sit high above the low-redshift BAO points the paper plots.
- Recomputation from the paper's own growth equation: today's slope is ~0.11 (Lambda-free) or ~0.015 (SH0ES-matching extension), so cosmological $G$ is about 9× or ~70× Newton's. Linear growth today comes out ~1.5–2× Lambda-CDM. That worsens $S_8$; it does not help it. An independent full-data analysis (Hashim et al. 2026, seen as an abstract only) rules the sign-changing branch out.

## Physical intuition

Matching today's matter fraction is one key that opens two records. The small positive parameter adds a mild phantom boost and can lift $H_0$ toward the local measurement. The large negative parameter books a geometric fluid that starts in debt and later turns positive. In general relativity a negative-energy fluid is a reflex rejection; here the ghost test is the slope of the gravitational function, and that slope stays positive on the neglected branch. The bill is the size of the slope. A small positive slope means cosmological gravity is much stronger than Newton's, so structure grows too fast. Holding the CMB distance fixed while the middle of cosmic history expands slowly forces a fast finish, which is why $H_0$ overshoots.

## Limitations and assumptions

- No statistical fit. Two CMB-derived numbers are fixed and parameters are hand-picked; a full CMB+BAO+supernova+growth likelihood is deferred. When an independent follow-up was done, the sign-changing branch failed.
- Low-$z$ BAO miss (several standard deviations near redshift $0.4$–$0.5$, opposite the Lyman-alpha miss) uses points read off the figures, without correlations. The sign of the miss is clear; the exact misfit is approximate.
- The growth blow-up uses the paper's quasi-static equation. Whether that equation still holds in $f(T)$ when the slope is $\sim 0.01$ is unsettled; the degree-of-freedom count of $f(T)$ is itself debated.
- "Ghost-free" here means a positive slope only. Stability against general inhomogeneous perturbations is left open.
- Local safety is a qualitative "chameleon-like" argument. The authors concede the standard chameleon mechanism does not apply to $f(T)$, and there is no screening calculation for parameters of size 3–7.
- The background equations of $f(T)$ and $f(Q)$ coincide in a flat universe, so the same branch warning applies to background-equivalent $f(Q)$ studies. That is a caveat, not a new fit.
