# Eruptive Low-Mass YSO IRAS 21204+4913 (FUor-Class Outburst)

**One-line summary:** Burlak et al. (arXiv 2026) document a $\gtrsim5$–$6$ mag optical outburst of the low-mass young star IRAS 21204+4913 ($L_{\rm bol}\sim200\,L_\odot$, $\dot{M}_{\rm ac}\gtrsim3\times10^{-5}\,M_\odot\,{\rm yr}^{-1}$), with P Cygni winds, high polarization, energy-dependent line shifts, TiO emission, and a historical 1948 flare — a hybrid FUor/EXor portrait of disk instability.

## Key claims and results

- **Paper:** M. A. Burlak et al., “The eruptive young star IRAS 21204+4913,” arXiv:2602.15157 (2026).
- Target: low-mass YSO ($\lesssim0.5\,M_\odot$ class) in a dark nebula $\sim500$ pc; multiwavelength optical–NIR campaign after late-2025 brightening.
- Photometry: rise $\gtrsim5$ mag; bolometric luminosity $\sim200\,L_\odot$ during outburst.
- Accretion estimate from $L \approx GM_*\dot{M}_{\rm ac}/R_*$: $\dot{M}_{\rm ac}\gtrsim3\times10^{-5}\,M_\odot\,{\rm yr}^{-1}$.
- Spectroscopy: absorption-dominated FUor-like continuum; H$\alpha$ P Cygni with $\sim300$ km s$^{-1}$ wind; unusual **TiO emission**; forbidden Fe lines.
- Line FWHM and velocity trend with excitation energy $E_{\rm exc}$ — attributed to multiple scattering in an optically thick dusty wind (reported as a first for FUors).
- Polarimetry: polarization up to $\sim16\%$ in $I$; position angle tied to nearby HH jet geometry.
- Historical plates (1899–1975): prior outburst in **1948** → recurrence on decades-class timescales.
- SED: class I/II; low extinction in outburst view ($A_V\lesssim2$ mag in analysis, with earlier higher estimates debated).

## Physical intuition

Young stars do not sip their disks — they sometimes **gulp**. FUors are months-to-years power surges when the inner disk runs away thermally; EXors are shorter magnetic/clumpy spikes. IRAS 21204+4913 looks like a low-mass hybrid: a FUor-scale flood that still shows quirky molecular emission and a documented 1948 encore. Dust in the wind is not just fog — it is a scattering network that redshifts and broadens higher-energy lines more, like packets bouncing longer in a congested router.

## Limitations and assumptions

- Only ~two months of post-detection coverage; peak and decay incomplete → FUor vs long EXor still fuzzy.
- Extinction estimates flip ($A_V\sim8$ vs $\lesssim2$); distance/luminosity inherit that.
- $E_{\rm exc}$–scattering model is qualitative; turbulence, clumping, and magnetic geometry under-modeled.
- Gaia distance may be confused by nebular structure.
- Analysis-based ingest; verify photometry, $\dot{M}$, and historical light curve against primary paper.
