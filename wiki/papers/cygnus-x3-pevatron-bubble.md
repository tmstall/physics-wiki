# Cygnus X-3 as PeVatron Powering the Cygnus Bubble (Model)

**One-line summary:** Shi, Wang, Yang & Aharonian (*ApJL* 2026) model the >400 TeV Cygnus Bubble glow as a diffusion halo of escaped PeV protons from microquasar Cygnus X-3 (already LHAASO-variable on its 4.8 h orbit), rather than only Cygnus OB2 — continuous-injection flux scaling softens the 7× distance penalty vs OB2.

## Key claims and results

- **Paper:** Shi et al., *ApJ Letters* **1006**:L31 (2026), DOI 10.3847/2041-8213/ae878e.
- Context: LHAASO sees Cygnus X-3 UHE γ-rays modulated on the binary orbit (hadronic smoking gun) and a separate ~6° Cygnus Bubble up to ~1 PeV usually credited to OB2 (~1.4 kpc).
- Claim: Cygnus X-3 (~9.7 kpc) supplies the **>400 TeV** bubble via ~10⁵ yr proton diffusion into ISM gas; lower-energy bubble still attributed to OB2/Cocoon foreground.
- Physics lever: continuous injection + radial CR/gas profiles ≠ $1/d^2$ flash scaling — farther source can remain competitive.
- Radial intensity profile used as consistency check with point-injector diffusion.
- Acceleration efficiency ≲ few % of binary power in fiducial models; sensitive to assumed $n_{\rm gas}$ and locally suppressed diffusion.

## Physical intuition

Match the smoking gun (orbital PeV clock at Cyg X-3) to the bullet hole (extended UHE bubble). Distance looks fatal until you remember the glow is a long-built CR reservoir, not a one-shot flash. Next-gen telescopes must resolve a point core at Cyg X-3 or the model fails.

## Limitations and assumptions

- Model reinterpretation of public LHAASO data — not a new detection.
- Degenerate parameters (gas density, diffusion, injection duration/power); diffusion coeff. 1–2 orders below Galactic average (local turbulence).
- Angular resolution cannot yet separate Cyg X-3 vs OB2 along the line of sight.
- Analysis lite-mode but full text available in source analysis.
