# Interstellar Glaciers: SPHEREx Ice Maps

**One-line summary:** SPHEREx’s first wide-field near-IR spectral maps of Cygnus X and the North American Nebula show filamentary H₂O, CO₂, and CO ice — plus diffuse ice beyond dense cores — coexisting with anti-correlated PAH emission.

## Key claims and results

- **Paper:** Hora et al. 2026, *ApJ*, DOI: 10.3847/1538-4357/ae5180 (analysis; arXiv:2603.12390).
- Instrument: NASA **SPHEREx** (102 IR bands); demonstration mosaics \(\sim 10^\circ\times 5^\circ\) over GMC complexes spanning hundreds of light-years.
- Features: H₂O (~3 µm), CO₂ (4.27 µm), CO (4.67 µm) absorption; PAH emission (~3.28 µm).
- Ice tracks dense, cold, shielded filaments (agrees with ¹³CO, extinction, Herschel dust); **H₂O/CO₂ ratio varies** spatially (chemical thermometer / UV exposure).
- Surprise: ice also in **diffuse** regions outside the densest filaments.
- PAH emission anti-correlates with ice (UV that pumps PAHs also destroys ice).

## Physical intuition

At 10–20 K, gas sticks to smoke-sized dust grains and freezes — ice mantles on soot. Dense dust is sunscreen against UV from young stars; ice survives. SPHEREx turns the galaxy’s own diffuse starlight into a hyperspectral floodlight so every pixel is an ice measurement — aerial imaging of forest moisture instead of poking one tree at a time.

## Limitations and assumptions

- Tiny fraction of the full ~10 million sightline SPHEREx ice survey.
- Column densities only (no 3D structure; phase/mixing poorly constrained).
- Cygnus X is an extreme, UV-rich complex — may not generalize.
- Diffuse ice challenges standard column-density thresholds for mantle growth.
