# Resonance Breathing and the Baroreflex

**One-line summary:** Combined synthesis of 6 sources (no single DOI). Baroreflex is a delayed feedback loop; near ~0.1 Hz the correction lands in phase and heart-rate swings amplify 4–10×. Height sets rate (~4.5–6.5 breaths/min); age shrinks gain not rate. Half a breath/min off can halve the effect, but most trials do not measure rate.

## Key claims and results

- Vaschillo, Lehrer, Rishe & Konstantinov (2002; DOI 10.1023/A:1014587304314): five men, ages 24–27, made heart rate follow a sine at seven frequencies while finger blood pressure was recorded. Swings were 4–10× rest. In every participant the peak sat where heart rate and blood pressure were 180° apart, near 0.1 Hz. A 0° crossing sat at the minimum. Breathing was mostly not at the drive frequency.
- Vaschillo, Vaschillo & Lehrer (2006; DOI 10.1007/s10484-006-9009-3), 56 adults: personal resonance 4.5–6.5 breaths/min (mean 5.56). Taller people resonate slower ($r = -0.55$); the sex difference vanishes after height adjustment. Age does not move the rate. A 0.5 breath/min miss can change respiratory-band power by more than 2×. Beginners overbreathe; the height signal appears once that stops.
- Lehrer et al. (*Chest*, 2006): during biofeedback, low-frequency heart-rate power and baroreflex gain rise less over age 40 than under 40. In that asthma sample, medication need fell in every age group. That is an in-session boost, not proof the reflex stays remodeled at rest.
- The same peak appears with rhythmic muscle tension and with picture cues. A predicted second, vascular resonance near 0.03 Hz was not found with paced sighing.
- Lalanza et al. (2023) show most trials never measure the rate this physiology says matters. See [[hrv-biofeedback-methods-review]].

## Physical intuition

The baroreflex is a blood-pressure governor with a stale reading and a limited band. Stretch sensors ignore slow drifts. The inertia of blood and vessels smears fast changes. Near one cycle every ten seconds — about six breaths a minute — the correction that should damp a swing lands in phase with the next one, and heart rate amplifies. Height lengthens the effective lag, so the peak rate drops. Age turns the gain down and leaves the peak where it is. Breathing is a convenient drive, not the resonator: the peak shows up when people trace a sine with heart rate and are not breathing at that frequency. Half a breath per minute off the peak can cut the swing roughly in half, which is why an unmeasured “six per minute” protocol is a sloppy dose.

## Limitations and assumptions

- The only beat-to-beat blood-pressure frequency response here is five young men from one laboratory network. The 4–10× figure is the authors’ summary of plots, not a re-digitized transfer function.
- The quoted delay is half the period at the 180° crossing, not an independent travel time. One paper says 4.5–9 s; another, describing the same idea, says about 4–6.5 s.
- In breathing protocols, respiratory sinus arrhythmia and baroreflex resonance add. No source splits their shares.
- The age effect on gain is within-session, groups of 7–11, and one asthma sample. Clinical need for the exact personal rate is still unproven.
- Downstream claims (gas exchange, vagal signaling to mood circuits, a lasting gain increase at rest) stay hypotheses. The second vascular resonance was a failed prediction.
