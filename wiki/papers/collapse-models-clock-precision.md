# Spacetime Jitter from Collapse Models (Clock Floor)

**One-line summary:** Identifying spontaneous-collapse noise with Newtonian-potential fluctuations implies an irreducible proper-time uncertainty that grows as \(\sqrt{t}\)—yet after one year it is only \(\sim 10^{-28}\) s (CSL) or \(\sim 10^{-31}\) s (DP), far below any present optical clock.

## Key claims and results

- **Paper:** Nicola Bortolotti, Catalina Curceanu, Lajos Diósi, Simone Manti, Kristian Piscicchia, *Fundamental limits on clock precision from spacetime uncertainty in quantum collapse models*, *Phys. Rev. Research* **7**, (2025). DOI: 10.1103/p6tj-lg8l.
- CSL and Diósi–Penrose models couple mass density to classical stochastic noise; authors treat that noise as \(\phi\) jitter.
- Weak-field GR: \(\delta t \sim c^{-2}\int\phi\,d\tau\); finite clock volume averages the field.
- Time variance grows linearly with lab time \(t\); diffusion constant peaks when clock size ~ model smearing length \(r_C\).
- Standard parameters → RMS jitter after 1 yr many orders of magnitude below today’s optical-lattice / ion clocks.
- Conclusion: collapse models stay compatible with rock-solid timekeeping; the link is conceptual, not a near-term test.

## Physical intuition

If nature continuously “measures” mass density to force collapse, the bookkeeping looks like a random gravitational potential. Clocks run a hair fast or slow as that potential wiggles. The random walk of ticks is real in the model—and still tinier than any error bar we will see this century.

## Limitations and assumptions

- Hybrid heuristic: non-relativistic collapse + linearized GR + classical noise—no full relativistic collapse QFT.
- Noise is put in by hand; not derived from graviton loops or vacuum fluctuations.
- CSL rate \(\lambda\) spans many orders of magnitude; future tightening could move the floor (still tiny).
- Not practically testable with near-future clocks; cosmological timescales enlarge \(\sqrt{t}\) but remain negligible for lab work.
