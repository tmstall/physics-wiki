# Negative-Mass Objects in the Sky

**One-line summary:** Physics of the Dark Universe DOI 10.1016/j.dark.2026.102364. Two notions: a bubble (needs nuclear-density surroundings) vs genuine anti-mass (must violate energy conditions by construction). Would diverge light (dark spot not Einstein ring). Paper overstates deflection ~3×. Existence of a solution shows the modified-gravity ansatz is flexible, not that the objects are stable or real.

## Key claims and results

- Nojiri & Odintsov (*Physics of the Dark Universe* 53, 102364, 2026; arXiv:2602.15058) build two different “negative-mass objects.”
- Bubble: a star in a negative-pressure fluid, with a negative cosmological constant cancelling the background density, can leave a mass deficit. The fluid must be at least about a third as dense as a black hole of the same mass — nuclear-density surroundings — and finely tuned against $\Lambda$. With back-reaction the deficit is cosmological, not a localized object.
- Genuine anti-mass: an “anti-Hayward” metric with ADM mass $-M$, negative energy density everywhere, and an anti-de Sitter core. Scalar fields are reconstructed to order and then frozen with constraints so they do not propagate.
- Light deflects outward: a diverging lens, a dark spot rather than an Einstein ring. The paper’s special-case angle (~81°) is wrong; the corrected deflection is ~29° (~0.51 rad), about 3× smaller. The direction holds. Weak-field bending is still $\sim 4|M|/b$.
- A separate vanishing-mass metric can keep a finite shadow as its horizon shrinks to zero.

## Physical intuition

A bubble rises in water because the fluid around it falls. That is a deficit against a filled background, not a lump of negative energy. The sky does not supply a nuclear-density fluid to make the deficit outweigh the star. The object studied in the lensing sections is the other kind: matter written so the energy conditions fail. Printing that metric shows the reconstruction method can realize any spherical geometry you order. It does not show the object forms, stays stable, or survives contact with ordinary matter.

## Limitations and assumptions

- Section II needs $\rho_0 \gtrsim 10^{18}\,{\rm kg\,m^{-3}}$ for a solar-mass star. A cosmic-density fluid with a parsec-scale cutoff does not give $M_{\rm lost} \gg M$.
- Stability is asserted. A barotropic fluid with $w < 0$ is gradient-unstable. Frozen scalars are non-dynamical by fiat. No perturbation analysis.
- Negative ADM mass in flat asymptotics requires a dominant-energy-condition violation. The paper never names the positive-mass theorem. Negative inertia plus ordinary matter is the runaway the constraints were meant to avoid.
- Observational sketches give no mass scale, abundance, or link to existing negative-mass lensing limits.
- A composite bubble can have positive inertia (added mass of the fluid it displaces). Negative inertia applies cleanly only to the reconstructed anti-mass.
