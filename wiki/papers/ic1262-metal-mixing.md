# IC 1262: A Metal-Mixing Machine

**One-line summary:** Deep Chandra X-ray maps plus GMRT radio imaging catch galaxy group IC 1262 redistributing metals via three channels at once—sloshing cold fronts, a southern shock, and a north–south radio jet—with geometry that separates the mechanisms.

## Key claims and results

- System: IC 1262 at \(z\sim 0.033\) (~140 Mpc); known sloshing group with central radio-loud AGN.
- **Sloshing:** gas inside cold fronts (E and NW) ~50% more metal-rich than outside—core-enriched gas dragged outward and wrapped into fronts over Gyr.
- **Shock:** southern metallicity drop by ~factor of two across ~78 kpc projected distance; blast segregates enriched vs unenriched gas.
- **Jet:** N–S radio axis orthogonal to slosh fronts → natural experiment separating AGN uplift from merger-driven sloshing.
- Hot IGrM ~1–2 keV; metals not just passively diffused—large-scale bulk motions do the transport.

## Physical intuition

Think of a coffee cup (group core) bumped by a spoon (minor merger): the liquid sloshes, and the cream (metals) rides the wave into frothy fronts. Meanwhile a blender stick (radio jet) punches along a different axis, and a pressure blast (shock) clears a depleted wake. IC 1262 lets you watch all three kitchen tools at once because they are not collinear.

## Limitations and assumptions

- Analysis notes partial reconstruction from abstract + field knowledge—verify spectral numbers against full paper.
- Projection: 78 kpc is projected; true 3D geometry uncertain.
- Sloshing perturber not identified; timing/mass ratio open.
- Jet vs slosh metal budgets not fully separated by tailored simulations.
- Outer metallicity fits photon-starved; error bars grow at large radius.
