# One Dust Trap for Carbonaceous Chondrite Diversity

**One-line summary:** A single long-lived Jupiter-carved pressure bump at ~5–6 AU, with rigid refractories and fragile matrix filtering at different rates under photoevaporation, reproduces the ~2–4 Myr compositional timeline of carbonaceous chondrite groups from one nursery.

## Key claims and results

- Carbonaceous chondrites (CC) form a matrix-fraction vs age sequence: matrix-poor CO/CV earlier; matrix-rich CM/TL later; CR/CI last—over ~2–4 Myr after CAIs.
- Model: one pressure maximum outside Jupiter acts as a time-dependent compositional filter, not many ad-hoc traps.
- Rigid particles (CAIs + chondrules) vs fragile matrix: different fragmentation, drift, and trapping efficiencies → evolving solids mix.
- Early bump retains rigid-rich pebbles (CO/CV-like ~2.3 Myr); later outer-disk supply + gas loss favor matrix-rich and CR/CI-like compositions.
- Implication: dust traps, not the smooth disk, are the dominant planetesimal nurseries—including earlier differentiated bodies that share isotopic scatter but lack extreme CI-like mixes.

## Physical intuition

A pressure bump is a traffic jam in the solar nebula: gas pressure peaks, headwind reverses, pebbles pile up. Rigid “gravel” and fragile “dust fluff” don’t brake the same way, so the jam’s recipe drifts over millions of years as the disk evaporates. Meteorites are core samples of that evolving jam.

## Limitations and assumptions

- Fiducial run underproduces total CR/CI mass; some CR paths need extra late chondrules or tuning.
- Jupiter fixed early at ~5 AU—migration would move or weaken the bump.
- Tracks bulk matrix fraction, not full nucleosynthetic isotope maps (Ti, Cr, …).
- Simplified collision physics; 2D misses 3D vortices / secondary traps.
- Planetesimal formation efficiency is a phenomenological parameter.
