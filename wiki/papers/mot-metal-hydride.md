# Magneto-Optical Trap of CaH (Metal Hydride)

**One-line summary:** First laser cooling and magneto-optical trapping of a metal hydride molecule (CaH) — a backdoor route toward ultracold hydrogen via photodissociation that direct H laser cooling cannot provide.

## Key claims and results

- Zelevinsky group (Columbia) MOT of calcium monohydride (analysis framing).
- CaH is photochemically fragile under light; the experiment still closes a usable cycling transition.
- First metal hydride in the laser-cooled diatomic club.
- Strategic goal: trap cold hydrides, then near-threshold photodissociate to liberate cold H for precision QED / proton-radius-class metrology free of beam Doppler limits.

## Physical intuition

Hydrogen is the perfect physics atom and a terrible laser-cooling target — no convenient closed optical cycle. Cool a molecule that *carries* hydrogen, park it in a MOT, then snip the H atom free so it inherits the molecule’s cold velocity. The metal end of CaH supplies the almost-closed electronic ladder; the H rides along as cargo.

## Limitations and assumptions

- Milestone demonstration; phase-space density and H-release efficiency still open.
- Photodissociation kinematics and residual kinetic energy must be controlled for metrology use.
- Analysis-based ingest; verify numbers against the primary Letter.
