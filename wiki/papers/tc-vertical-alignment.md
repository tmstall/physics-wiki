# Tropical Cyclone Vertical Alignment: Quickly Aligning vs Persistently Tilted

**One-line summary:** Fischer et al. (*JGR Atmospheres* 2026) composite three decades of TC-RADAR aircraft Doppler analyses to show storms that quickly realign after ≥30 km initial tilt already differ a day earlier — tighter low-level spin, more favorable shear-relative tilt, stronger low-level ascent near the LLC, and a more favorable environment.

## Key claims and results

- **Paper:** Fischer, Alvey, Jariwala & Reasor, *J. Geophys. Res. Atmos.* **131**(14), e2025JD045986, DOI 10.1029/2025JD045986.
- Data: TC-RADAR — 1,510 NOAA WP-3D Doppler analyses (1997–2024); tilt = max horizontal offset of WCM centers between 2.0 km and 2.0–6.5 km altitudes.
- Sample cuts: initial tilt ≥30 km; intensity ≤75 kt; serial radar passes 6–27 h apart.
- Groups: **Quickly Aligning** vs **Persistently Tilted** (magnitude-based grouping to avoid confounding with “started more tilted”).
- Distinguishing features (Abstract + press-level reconstruction; full Results not in analysis retrieval): (1) stronger, more compact low-level circulation; (2) more cyclonic/downshear-left-favorable tilt orientation; (3) stronger low-tropospheric ascent near LLC and under mid-level vortex; (4) weaker mid-level shear, more moisture, higher MPI environment.
- Hypothesis (not proven causality): enhanced lower-tropospheric mass flux within ~35–50 km of the low-level center drives alignment via vortex stretching and precession (upward-building pathway).

## Physical intuition

A TC is a stack of loosely coupled vortices that shear knocks out of phase — like clock skew between domains. Alignment is a gateway many weak storms pass before rapid intensification. This study is the first large-sample **radar** separation of storms that straighten quickly from those that stay crooked, testing pathways previously seen mostly in sims and case studies.
