# Nodeless Gap and 70 meV Kink in Bilayer Nickelate Films (ARPES)

**One-line summary:** ARPES on oxygen-preserved (La,Pr,Sm)₃Ni₂O₇ films finds a fully nodeless ~18 meV superconducting gap across the Brillouin zone plus a sharp ~70 meV dispersion kink — ambient-pressure spectroscopic anchors for pairing symmetry and electron–boson coupling in Ruddlesden–Popper nickelates.

## Key claims and results

- **Paper (analysis framing):** Shen, Zhou, He, Xue, Chen et al., *Science* (nickelate film ARPES); ambient-pressure bilayer RP nickelate films.
- Cryogenic UHV quench-and-transfer preserves apical oxygen between growth and ARPES endstation — the materials step that unlocks clean surface spectroscopy.
- Superconducting gap ~18 meV with coherence peaks even along the zone diagonal (where pure $d_{x^2-y^2}$ must vanish).
- Full-zone maps: gap remains finite everywhere → **nodeless** (consistent with $s$ or $s_\pm$, not pure nodal $d$-wave).
- Quasiparticle dispersion kink at ~70 meV binding energy → strong coupling to a bosonic mode (optical oxygen phonon or magnetic collective mode candidates).
- Same pristine films supply both symmetry and glue-scale data that pressure-cell studies could not.

## Physical intuition

Cuprate lore taught nodal $d$-wave fortresses with open corner gates. These nickelate films look more like a closed wall: every direction on the Fermi surface still costs finite energy to break a pair. At the same time the electron speedometer shows a toll plaza at 70 meV — electrons slam into a boson they can emit or absorb. Cryo-transfer is the logistics win: without locking oxygen in place, the surface spoils and ARPES photographs a corpse, not the superconductor.

## Limitations and assumptions

- ARPES is surface-sensitive; bulk-sensitive cross-checks still needed.
- Film $T_c$ often lower than ~80 K bulk high-pressure La₃Ni₂O₇; composition/strain specific.
- 70 meV mode not chemically identified; coupling ≠ proven pairing glue.
- $s$ vs $s_\pm$ needs phase-sensitive tests.
