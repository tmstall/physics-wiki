# Microdroplet Aniline → Pyridine (Ring Rewrite)

**One-line summary:** Micrometer-scale water aerosol droplets convert aniline to pyridine in ~ms at the air–water interface without added metal catalysts or harsh bulk oxidants — a skeletal ring rewrite driven by microdroplet chemistry.

## Key claims and results

- Experimental synthetic chemistry at aerosol microdroplet interfaces (analysis framing).
- Skeletal C→N substitution in a six-membered aromatic ring (aniline → pyridine family), normally multi-step / Pd-catalyzed.
- Interface / spontaneous oxidant chemistry (microdroplet lineage of Zare-type air–water reactivity) is the enabling medium.
- Pharmaceutical relevance: pyridine scaffolds are ubiquitous in drugs (niacin, isoniazid, etc.).

## Physical intuition

Bulk water is a mild solvent. A 4 µm droplet is mostly surface. At that air–water skin, electric fields, partial solvation, and spontaneous oxidants open reaction channels that bulk kinetics lock. The droplet is not a tiny beaker — it is a different reactor topology.

## Limitations and assumptions

- Scale-up and selectivity for complex substrates open.
- Mechanism details (exact oxidant inventory) still debated in microdroplet chemistry.
