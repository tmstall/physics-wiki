# Loki: Early-Accreted Planar Very Metal-Poor Stars

**One-line summary:** Twenty solar-neighbourhood VMP stars on planar orbits share a tight chemical fingerprint and mixed prograde/retrograde paths, consistent with one early-accreted dwarf-scale progenitor the authors name Loki.

## Key claims and results

- **Paper:** MNRAS **548**, 2 (2026), DOI: 10.1093/mnras/stag563; arXiv:2409.13813.
- Sample: N = 20, \[Fe/H\] ≲ −2, \(Z_\mathrm{max} \lesssim 4\) kpc (one outlier ~10 kpc); 11 prograde / 9 retrograde; ESPaDOnS high-res spectra; 23 elements.
- Chemical scatter much smaller than general halo (MAD often >1.4× tighter); **no α-knee** → star formation ≲ 1 Gyr, little Type Ia input.
- Neutron-capture patterns resemble classical dSphs more than ultra-faint dwarfs.
- One-zone GCE: \(M_\mathrm{bary} \sim 1.4\times 10^9\,M_\odot\), low SFE, high-energy CCSN/hypernovae + NS mergers.
- NIHAO-UHD-style zooms: early single accretion can populate both orbital senses while keeping stars planar; late accretion after disc settles struggles.

## Physical intuition

Very metal-poor stars are the first hire cohort before “Series A” iron funding. Chemical ratios are foundry stamps; planar high-eccentricity orbits are truck routes that stayed on the interstate floor. Drop a dense package into a still-swirling proto-Galaxy before the main walls are up, and fragments spread prograde and retrograde along the floor — not in random 3-D drone space.

## Limitations and assumptions

- Modest N = 20; one kinematic outlier.
- Single GCE parameter set; possible overlap with GSE VMP tail still debated.
- Needs larger homogeneous samples (WEAVE, 4MOST) to confirm Loki as independent structure.
