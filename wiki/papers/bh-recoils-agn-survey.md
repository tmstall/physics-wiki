## Key claims and results

- **Bibliography:** DOI: 10.48550/arXiv.2605.04781; arXiv:2605.04781
- You can hunt for kicked black holes without ever seeing one move — just notice that, across 100,000 quasars, the faster-moving ones are statistically dustier, exactly because a recoiling hole is fastest when it's plowing
- A "weak" correlation of 0.12 is actually the headline win — the theory predicted it would be small once you account for messy velocity measurements, so recovering that exact small number is the signal, not noise.

## Physical intuition

When two supermassive black holes merge, the survivor gets a "kick" from lopsided gravitational-wave emission and goes careening through its galaxy's center. Catching one of these runaways in the act has frustrated astronomers for twenty years, because every individual candidate has an innocent alternative explanation.  This paper tries something new: instead of chasing single fugitives, it looks for a statistical fingerprint across an entire pop
