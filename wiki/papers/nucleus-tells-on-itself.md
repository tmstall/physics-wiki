# Nuclear γ Upbend: E1 vs M1 via Parity Bookkeeping ($^{70}$Zn)

**One-line summary:** FRIB/MSU-led experiment determines the multipole character of the low-energy γ-strength enhancement (“upbend”) in $^{70}$Zn by feeding the *same* nucleus two ways and using parity selection rules the nucleus enforces on itself.

## Key claims and results

- Target question: is the low-energy enhancement (LEE) in the γ-ray strength function electric dipole (E1) or magnetic dipole (M1)?
- Method: dual population pathways of $^{70}$Zn; strength that survives a parity selection filter reveals multipole character.
- Inputs matter for neutron-capture rates in r-/i-process nucleosynthesis, not only nuclear structure aesthetics.
- Complements giant/pygmy/scissors features of the γSF with a clean LEE diagnosis.

## Physical intuition

At high level density, individual γ lines blur into a strength function — an aggregate “performance profile” of photon emission. The upbend is a mysterious rise at the *bottom* of that profile. Instead of building a better spectrometer alone, the collaboration lets the nucleus audit itself: two different feeding routes, one selection rule, and the multipole that violates the rule dies. Parity bookkeeping is the internal checksum.

## Limitations and assumptions

- Species-specific ($^{70}$Zn); universal E1/M1 assignment across the chart is open.
- Analysis-based ingest; verify multipole fractions on the primary paper.
- Astrophysical rate impact requires Hauser–Feshbach folding with level densities.
