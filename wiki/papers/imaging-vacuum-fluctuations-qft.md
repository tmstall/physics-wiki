# Imaging Vacuum Fluctuations in a Cold-Atom Quantum Field

**One-line summary:** A two-component $^{39}$K BEC spin field is used as an analog relativistic quantum field; Cambridge/Hadzibabic group images mode-resolved vacuum fluctuations and finds amplitudes tracking the zero-temperature $1/\omega_k$ law rather than thermal $1/\omega_k^{2}$ scaling (arXiv:2608.20311 preprint).

## Key claims and results

- Synthetic bosonic field from relative phase $\phi$ and imbalance $Z$ of a RF-coupled two-component box-trapped BEC; dispersion dialable between Rabi and massive (sine-Gordon-like) regimes.
- Two protocols (quench amplification + complementary readout) extract structure-factor amplitudes vs wavevector.
- Measured amplitude law matches quantum vacuum ($A\sim 1/\omega_k$), not ordinary heat — mode by mode over a wide frequency-ratio range.
- Instrumental stakes: tabletop access to vacuum jitter underlying Casimir/Lamb-type physics and analog cosmology hooks; not a challenge to QFT itself.
- Unrefereed arXiv preprint (2026-08-20); single-lab; shares apparatus/systematics with a companion analog-field preprint.

## Physical intuition

Every field mode is a harmonic oscillator that cannot sit perfectly still. This experiment builds a tunable “field” out of spin degrees of freedom in a superfluid, then photographs how hard each wavelength jitters. Heat would leave a different fingerprint than true zero-point motion; the data follow the quantum fingerprint.

## Limitations and assumptions

- Preprint / adversarial posture until peer review and independent replication (different species or platform preferred).
- Correction factors and calibration are load-bearing; companion paper shares systematics.
- Analog “relativistic” = engineered dispersion shape, not literal $c$-limited spacetime.
