# Dynamical Formation of Gravastars from Dust Collapse

**One-line summary:** Within pure GR, a fine-tuned Oppenheimer–Snyder dust collapse can nucleate an expanding de Sitter core that stalls near the Schwarzschild radius—forming a horizonless gravastar instead of a black hole—but only if initial compactness stays below \(3/8\).

## Key claims and results

- **Paper:** Daniel Jampolski & Luciano Rezzolla, *Formation of gravastars*, *Phys. Rev. D* **113**, L121502 (2026). DOI: 10.1103/c6lw-nx7k; arXiv:2509.15302.
- First **dynamical** formation path for a gravastar without higher-curvature corrections.
- Setup: uniform dust collapse + small central de Sitter (dark-energy) region that expands and meets the collapsing surface.
- Junction conditions naturally brake the bubble near \(r_s\), yielding a static thin-shell equilibrium (horizonless exterior).
- **Hard wall:** initial compactness \(\gtrsim 3/8\) forces black-hole collapse—the de Sitter bubble cannot catch the surface in time.
- Proves dynamical possibility in GR, not astrophysical inevitability.

## Physical intuition

A black hole is the default “sink to a point” endpoint. A gravastar is a star that instead grows a tiny baby universe of dark energy at its center; that bubble expands until it almost becomes a horizon, then freezes into a shell of ordinary matter around a de Sitter interior. From outside it can mimic a black hole—without a singularity or a true event horizon—if the initial star is not already too compact.

## Limitations and assumptions

- Pressureless dust is unphysical; real fluids, shocks, and radiation untested.
- Fine-tuning is severe (measure-zero curve in parameter space).
- Stability under perturbation and realistic equations of state open.
- No demonstration that nature selects these initial conditions.
