# Discrete Gravity from Planck-Scale Cells

**One-line summary:** Chamseddine & Mukhanov rebuild geometry from indivisible, structureless cells linked by local rotations; continuum GR (vielbein, metric, curvature) is recovered exactly as cell size → 0 (*JHEP* 11 (2021) 013; arXiv:2109.03752).

## Key claims and results

- Postulate: below an elementary volume (motivated as Planck-scale), points are not physically distinguishable — a physical claim, not a mere UV regulator.
- Cells carry only neighbor displacement operators + spin connection; tangent, vielbein, and metric are derived.
- Continuum limit reproduces ordinary curved-spacetime geometry term by term.
- Peer-reviewed JHEP; same-lineage follow-ups exist (e.g. scalar curvature 2022).
- Contested among QG programs: discreteness postulated here vs dynamically derived (e.g. LQG) or continuum UV completion (asymptotic safety).

## Physical intuition

Smooth spacetime assumes infinite addressability — any midpoint is a real place. Near the Planck length, quantum metric jitter may erase that idea. This paper starts from “cache-line” cells with no interior addresses and shows that when cells shrink away, the GR you already know falls back out exactly.

## Limitations and assumptions

- Founding discreteness postulate is assumed, not derived from a deeper theory.
- Continuum recovery is a consistency win, not by itself a completed quantum gravity.
- Does not uniquely beat rival short-distance diagnoses.
