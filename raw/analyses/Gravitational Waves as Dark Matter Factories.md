## Gravitational Waves as Dark Matter Factories: Spacetime Ripples That Literally Conjure Fermions from the Vacuum

****Hook:**** Gravitational waves --- normally just messengers from violent cosmic collisions --- can actually produce fermionic dark matter in the early Universe, through a quantum loop process that bypasses the standard prohibition on gravitational fermion creation.

### 1. Big-Picture Context

The dark matter origin problem has a dirty secret: gravity, the most universal force in the cosmos, is actually terrible at making fermions. Scalars and vectors (spin-0, spin-1 fields) get gravitationally produced just fine when the Universe expands --- the changing spacetime curvature squeezes the quantum vacuum and pops particles out of it (gravitational particle production, or GPP). But massless fermions are special. Their minimal coupling to gravity is conformally invariant, which is a fancy way of saying the fermion field doesn\'t \"notice\" a Universe that expands uniformly. Expansion alone can\'t make them. This is one of the most elegant no-go results in cosmological QFT, and it meant light fermionic dark matter was essentially off the table for pure-gravity production.

Maleknejad and Kopp punch a very clean hole through this barrier. Their key insight: a stochastic gravitational wave background (SGWB) is not just expansion. It\'s an anisotropic, time-varying perturbation on top of the smooth Friedmann--Lemaître--Robertson--Walker (FLRW) background. And crucially, those perturbations break conformal invariance. The GW background acts like a fluctuating \"texture\" on spacetime that Weyl fermions can feel, and at one-loop order in QFT, those fermions get excited --- they get produced with a calculable energy density. If those fermions later acquire mass (through, say, a Higgs-like mechanism or a phase transition at some lower energy scale), they freeze in as the dark matter we observe today.

What makes this more than a curiosity is observational relevance. Stochastic gravitational wave backgrounds from early-Universe phase transitions, cosmic strings, primordial magnetic fields, or inflationary dynamics are being hunted right now by pulsar timing arrays (NANOGrav) and will be targets for LISA. The researchers point out that this new mechanism connects dark matter production to sources that are potentially detectable. If this mechanism is correct, dark matter relic density is literally encoded in the gravitational wave spectrum --- the same spectrum that next-generation GW detectors will read out. [ScienceDaily](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2026/04/260424233217.htm)

The researchers emphasize that stochastic gravitational waves differ from the familiar GWs born from massive colliding objects; instead, these arise from early-Universe phenomena that don\'t involve astronomical bodies at all --- they\'re a relic of the cosmos\'s violent first instants. [Tech Explorist](https://www.techexplorist.com/dark-matter-origin-lies-early-universe-gravitational-waves/102799/)

### 2. Necessary Background Crash-Course

****Conformal invariance and the fermion no-go.**** In a smoothly expanding Universe, massless fields with certain symmetries can be \"rescaled away\" --- you change coordinates and the field barely notices the expansion. Massless fermions (Weyl fermions) have exactly this property: their equation of motion in an expanding universe reduces to the flat-space version after a simple field redefinition. That means the vacuum state of a Weyl fermion in a flat Universe is identical to the vacuum in an expanding one. No particles are produced. Think of it like a ship in perfectly calm, uniformly rising water --- the hull doesn\'t feel any wave-induced stress, just the hydrostatic pressure it can absorb passively.

****Stochastic GW backgrounds.**** A stochastic GWB is not a single binary merger\'s chirp --- it\'s a superposition of incoherent gravitational waves from many sources, acting like a random noise bath on spacetime geometry. Cosmologically, it can arise from first-order phase transitions (bubbles of new vacuum colliding), cosmic strings, or primordial magnetic turbulence. These waves arise from early-Universe phenomena without massive objects being involved. Crucially, unlike smooth expansion, a GWB introduces anisotropic, time-varying metric perturbations --- it\'s like the calm water developing chaotic, random chop. That chop breaks the conformal invariance, and the hull of the fermion field starts feeling real forces. [Tech Explorist](https://www.techexplorist.com/dark-matter-origin-lies-early-universe-gravitational-waves/102799/)

****Weyl fermions.**** A Weyl fermion is a two-component, massless, chiral fermion --- it\'s what an electron would be if it had no mass, only handedness (left or right helicity). The Standard Model neutrino was long thought to be a Weyl fermion. These are the lightest possible fermion candidates and the ones most kinematically favored for freeze-in from a feeble gravitational coupling.

****Freeze-in mechanism.**** Freeze-in is dark matter production by an extremely weak (\"feeble\") interaction that never achieves thermal equilibrium with the bath. Particles are produced slowly and steadily, then freeze in as production shuts off when the Universe cools. Unlike freeze-out (WIMPs), freeze-in DM never talks to the thermal plasma --- it\'s an accumulation mechanism. Think of it as slowly dripping water into a vessel that never equilibrates with the bath\'s temperature.

****In-in formalism.**** Standard QFT computes S-matrix elements --- you prepare a state, run it forward in time to infinity, and measure what came out. But in cosmology, you don\'t have an asymptotic future --- the Universe is always \"in progress.\" The in-in (or Schwinger--Keldysh) formalism computes expectation values of operators (like the energy density) at a finite time, within a given state. It\'s the natural language for cosmological particle production. Think of it as computing the real-time power dissipation in a circuit, not just the total energy delivered to infinity.

### 3. Core Technical Explanation

****The Geometry Setup.**** The authors work in a perturbed FLRW spacetime where the metric has both the usual expansion part (described by the scale factor) and a tensor perturbation --- the gravitational wave part. That tensor perturbation encodes both the familiar \"plus\" and \"cross\" GW polarizations. Working with circular polarization states (left/right handed GWs) turns out to be algebraically cleaner for fermions, because fermions care about helicity.

****The Interaction Vertices.**** A Weyl fermion minimally coupled to gravity has two types of interaction vertices with gravitons (quantized GW modes): a cubic vertex (one graviton + two fermion legs) and a quartic vertex (two gravitons + two fermion legs). The authors show that for an unpolarized GW background, the quartic vertex\'s contribution to the fermion energy density vanishes, so only the cubic vertex matters. This is a non-trivial simplification --- it collapses what would be a complicated two-diagram problem into one dominant contribution. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.09723)

****The Loop Calculation.**** They compute the fermion energy density at one-loop order in the in-in formalism. Diagrammatically, this looks like a loop where two graviton lines (representing the stochastic GW background) attach to a fermion loop --- the GW is the \"source\" exciting fermion modes out of the vacuum. The stochastic GW background is treated classically (as a random field), so the quantum expectation value over fermions is taken first, then the stochastic average over GW realizations. The result is a convolution integral: for each fermion mode of momentum k, they integrate over contributions from all graviton modes q, where the graviton carries momentum much smaller than the fermion (long-wavelength GWs producing short-wavelength fermion pairs).

****The Long-Wavelength Trick.**** When the GW wavelength is much longer than the fermion wavelength, the fermion effectively sees the long GW mode as a coordinate transformation --- a large gauge transformation --- and after that transformation, the fermion in the presence of the long GW mode looks like a free fermion. This is an elegant UV/IR separation: the long GW modes don\'t produce fermions, only the modes with comparable wavelengths do. This is the same kind of separation you\'d recognize in renormalization group flow --- long-wavelength modes decouple from short-wavelength physics. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.09723)

****The GW Spectrum Model.**** Rather than tying their calculation to a specific early-Universe model, they introduce a simple phenomenological broken-power-law model for the GW spectrum --- one peak frequency, a slope below and above it. This is generic enough to capture phase transitions, cosmic strings, and primordial magnetic field scenarios. The fermion energy density ends up scaling as the fourth power of the GW peak frequency (from dimensional analysis) times the GW energy density at production, modulated by the spectral shape. [Tech Explorist](https://www.techexplorist.com/dark-matter-origin-lies-early-universe-gravitational-waves/102799/)

****The Relic Density Result.**** The fermion energy density scales as the fourth power of the GW peak momentum, with corrections from the spectral shape. The mass-acquisition step is crucial: initially massless Weyl fermions are produced gravitationally (overcoming the conformal no-go by the anisotropy of the GWB), and then at some later time they acquire mass (through a beyond-Standard-Model mechanism), becoming the non-relativistic dark matter we observe. The final relic density depends on: the GW energy density at production time, the peak frequency of the spectrum, the mass the fermion eventually acquires, and the temperature at which mass is generated. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.09723)

Remarkably, the authors find that this mechanism can be more efficient than conventional gravitational production of superheavy fermions --- the standard approach to gravitational dark matter production (gravitino-like heavy fields near the Planck scale), which requires masses at or near 10¹³ GeV. GW-induced freeze-in can work with much lighter dark matter candidates. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.09723)

### 4. What\'s Genuinely New or Clever

****The symmetry bypass.**** The clean identification that a stochastic GWB breaks the conformal symmetry that normally forbids fermion production is the conceptual core of the paper. Earlier work on gravitational production of fermions always relied on heavy mass --- the mass explicitly breaks conformal invariance. Maleknejad and Kopp show that time-varying metric anisotropy is a second way to break it, with the anisotropy sourced by the GWB itself. This is a genuinely new door into fermionic dark matter production.

****The quartic vertex cancellation.**** The result that the quartic graviton-fermion vertex contributes zero for an unpolarized GWB is not obvious --- it requires careful symmetry analysis. This simplification is what makes the calculation analytically tractable and the result clean.

****The GW--DM observational bridge.**** The research introduces a new way dark matter could have formed, involving stochastic gravitational waves that are potentially detectable by current and future GW observatories. This is the intellectually powerful move: dark matter relic density becomes a function of the gravitational wave spectrum, and GW detectors become dark matter abundance detectors, in principle. It connects two of the most active observational frontiers --- pulsar timing arrays and dark matter searches --- through a single theoretical mechanism. [SciTechDaily](https://scitechdaily.com/new-study-suggests-gravitational-waves-may-have-created-dark-matter/)

### 5. Limitations and Open Questions

****Only analytical estimates so far.**** The authors themselves acknowledge that the next step is to go beyond their analytical estimates and conduct numerical calculations to improve prediction accuracy. The broken-power-law GW spectrum model is a useful stand-in, but real early-Universe GW spectra from specific scenarios (e.g., a first-order electroweak phase transition, or Abelian-Higgs cosmic strings) have more complicated shapes. How sensitive is the relic density to those details? [Phys.org](https://phys.org/news/2026-04-gravitational-candidates-dark.html)

****The mass-generation mechanism is unspecified.**** The paper treats mass acquisition as a free parameter --- the fermions start massless and acquire mass at some later time through an unspecified beyond-SM mechanism. The connection between the mass-generation scale, the electroweak scale, or other BSM physics is left open. This is not a criticism so much as an invitation for model-builders, but it does mean the dark matter candidate isn\'t fully defined.

****Chirality effects.**** If the GW background is chiral (unequal power in left- and right-handed modes), a gravitational triangle anomaly contributes, analogous to the Alvarez-Gaumé--Witten gravitational anomaly. This could produce a matter-antimatter asymmetry in the dark sector --- potentially linking dark matter abundance to baryogenesis. The authors flag this but don\'t compute it. The possibility of a mechanism accounting for the well-known particle-antiparticle difference is noted as an avenue for future research. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.09723)[Phys.org](https://phys.org/news/2026-04-gravitational-candidates-dark.html)

****No thermal bath coupling.**** The paper treats the fermion as completely decoupled from the thermal plasma. In reality, any beyond-SM model that gives the fermion mass will likely give it some (feeble) coupling to SM fields. How robust is the freeze-in against small thermal corrections?

****GW backreaction.**** The GW background is treated as a fixed classical source --- they don\'t account for the energy drain as fermions are produced. For very efficient production, this could matter.

### 6. Three Crystallized Takeaways

1.  ****Gravity waves don\'t just carry information --- they can manufacture dark matter.**** A stochastic GW background from the violent early Universe acts as a particle factory, producing massless Weyl fermions through a quantum loop process that completely bypasses the usual prohibition on gravitational fermion creation.
2.  ****The conformal symmetry that protects massless fermions from smooth expansion doesn\'t protect them from metric anisotropy.**** The GWB\'s random, directional perturbations break exactly the symmetry that was blocking production --- an elegant mechanism that works even without invoking superheavy particles.
3.  ****Your future gravitational wave detector might actually be a dark matter abundance meter.**** Because the fermion relic density depends directly on the GW spectrum\'s peak frequency and amplitude, measurements by LISA, NANOGrav, or Einstein Telescope of cosmological stochastic GW backgrounds could constrain --- or confirm --- this dark matter production channel.

### 7. Detailed Summary and Explanation

****The Problem Being Solved****

Cosmological dark matter production mechanisms fall into two broad camps: thermal (WIMPs that freeze out of equilibrium) and non-thermal (particles produced by gravitational effects, never in equilibrium). The non-thermal gravitational production route is attractive because it requires no new interactions beyond gravity --- the minimal scenario. However, this minimal gravitational production has a critical limitation: it works beautifully for scalar (spin-zero) and vector (spin-one) fields, but it completely fails for massless fermions.

Why? Because massless fermions are conformally invariant. Conformal invariance means that if you stretch the entire spacetime uniformly (as cosmic expansion does), the physics of the fermion field is unchanged --- you can absorb the expansion into a redefinition of the field and time coordinate. The fermion doesn\'t \"feel\" the expansion. No particle production occurs. This is sometimes called the \"fermion production problem\" in gravitational cosmology --- the lightest, most stable candidates are the hardest to make gravitationally.

****The Key Physical Insight****

A stochastic gravitational wave background is not uniform expansion. It\'s a collection of random, anisotropic perturbations in the metric --- ripples in spacetime moving in all directions with random phases. These perturbations are not conformally equivalent to flat space. They introduce a time-varying, direction-dependent distortion of spacetime geometry.

The authors show that this anisotropic perturbation breaks the conformal invariance of Weyl fermions. In the language of QFT, the fermion vacuum in the GWB is not the same as the Minkowski vacuum --- the GW modes mix positive and negative frequency fermion modes (Bogoliubov mixing), and this mixing corresponds to particle production. The produced fermion pairs carry momenta comparable to the GW modes that produced them.

****The Calculation****

They work at one-loop order in the in-in (Schwinger--Keldysh) formalism, which computes expectation values of operators at finite time rather than the asymptotic S-matrix elements used in standard particle physics. The setup: Weyl fermions minimally coupled to gravity, living in a perturbed FLRW spacetime where the perturbation is the GWB. Two graviton-fermion vertices exist --- cubic (one graviton, two fermion legs) and quartic (two gravitons, two fermion legs). The quartic vertex contribution vanishes for unpolarized GWBs by symmetry, leaving only the cubic vertex.

The dominant process is: a graviton mode (from the GWB) \"scatters\" off the quantum vacuum and produces a fermion-antifermion pair. At one-loop, this looks like a single graviton line connecting two points of a fermion loop. The stochastic average over GW realizations replaces the classical GW amplitude squared with the GW power spectrum --- a standard technique in stochastic field theory.

The result is an integral relating the fermion energy density to the GW power spectrum. A key simplification emerges: when a long-wavelength GW mode interacts with a short-wavelength fermion mode, the long GW mode can be treated as a coordinate transformation (a large gauge transformation). After this transformation, the fermion in that sector looks free --- it decouples. This means only GW modes with wavelengths comparable to the fermion wavelength contribute to production, providing a natural UV/IR cutoff on the integral.

****The GW Spectrum Model****

Rather than picking a specific early-Universe scenario, they parameterize the GW power spectrum with a broken power law: it rises with a certain spectral index below a peak frequency, peaks at that frequency with a certain amplitude, then falls off above it. This captures the key features of GW spectra from phase transitions (which peak at a characteristic bubble-collision frequency) and cosmic strings (which have a characteristic power-law shape). The four parameters --- peak frequency, peak amplitude, and the two spectral indices --- encapsulate the relevant physics without committing to a specific BSM model.

****The Relic Density****

After plugging in this parameterized spectrum and integrating over fermion modes and GW production history, the fermion energy density today depends on: the GW energy density at the time of production (how strong the GW background was), the peak frequency (which sets the typical fermion momentum), the time of GW production (through the Hubble parameter at that epoch), and crucially, the mass the fermion eventually acquires.

The fermion energy density scales roughly as the fourth power of the peak GW momentum times the GW energy density fraction --- physically sensible because the fermion gets its energy from the GW, and the phase space for production scales with momentum cubed times the coupling.

The authors then match this to the observed dark matter density (about 23 percent of total energy density) and find that viable parameter space exists. Remarkably, the mechanism works for dark matter masses much below the Planck scale --- far lighter than superheavy gravitinos or Wimpzillas that conventional gravitational production requires.

****The Chirality Angle****

One intriguing extension: if the GWB is chiral (more right-handed than left-handed GWs, as can happen in inflation models with gauge fields), the fermion production is asymmetric --- more fermions than antifermions (or vice versa). This is related to the gravitational anomaly (the Alvarez-Gaumé--Witten anomaly). The authors flag this as a potential link between dark matter production and baryogenesis --- one mechanism might explain both the DM abundance and the matter-antimatter asymmetry of the universe.

****Why This Matters Observationally****

The stochastic GW backgrounds this mechanism requires are in principle detectable by current and near-future observatories. NANOGrav operates in the nanohertz range, sensitive to GWs from supermassive black hole mergers and potentially from cosmological phase transitions. LISA will probe the millihertz band, sensitive to electroweak-scale phase transitions and cosmic strings. Einstein Telescope and Cosmic Explorer push into the decihertz and hertz range. If the peak frequency of the GWB responsible for dark matter production falls in one of these windows, the same spectrum that sets the dark matter relic density could be directly measured. This would be extraordinary: a gravitational wave measurement that doubles as a dark matter experiment.

****Explaining the Summary****

The analysis above walks through three nested ideas. First, the conformal invariance no-go theorem --- why massless fermions resist gravitational production --- and how GW anisotropy breaks it. Second, the QFT machinery (in-in formalism, loop diagrams, stochastic averaging) used to compute the production rate. Third, the cosmological phenomenology --- how the produced fermions become dark matter if they later acquire mass, and how the relic density maps onto observable GW properties.

The paper is fundamentally a proof of concept backed by an analytic estimate. It establishes that the mechanism is viable and interesting, maps out which GW spectrum parameters give the right dark matter abundance, and identifies the key physics (conformal symmetry breaking by anisotropy, loop production via cubic vertex). The detailed calculation is deferred to a companion paper (JHEP 2025) --- this PRL letter is the high-impact announcement of the mechanism and its cosmological viability.

### 8. Shorter Summary

Massless fermions cannot be produced by a smoothly expanding universe because of conformal symmetry --- they simply don\'t \"feel\" uniform expansion. But a stochastic gravitational wave background breaks that symmetry through its anisotropy, allowing Weyl fermions to be produced at one-loop order via graviton-fermion scattering in the quantum vacuum. Maleknejad and Kopp calculate this production rate using the in-in formalism appropriate for cosmological particle production, parameterize the GW spectrum with a broken power law to stay model-independent, and show that the resulting fermion energy density can match the observed dark matter abundance if those fermions later acquire mass through some beyond-Standard-Model mechanism. The mechanism is potentially more efficient than conventional superheavy gravitational dark matter production, and --- most excitingly --- it links the dark matter relic density directly to the stochastic GW spectrum, making next-generation gravitational wave observatories (LISA, NANOGrav, Einstein Telescope) indirect dark matter detectors. The key open tasks are numerical calculations beyond the analytic estimates, specification of the mass-generation mechanism, and exploration of the chiral GWB case, which could simultaneously address the matter-antimatter asymmetry of the universe.

****Explaining****

Good timing to go deep on this --- freeze-in is one of those mechanisms that sounds simple but has a lot of careful physics underneath.

### Freeze-In: The Dark Matter That Accumulated in Silence

The Universe\'s thermal bath in the early epochs is a violent, dense plasma --- every particle species that couples strongly enough gets hammered into equilibrium. Quarks, photons, electrons, W bosons --- they\'re all constantly scattering, annihilating, re-creating each other, maintaining a shared temperature. The bath is the majority shareholder of the Universe\'s energy budget.

Freeze-in dark matter lives outside that system. Its coupling to the thermal bath --- call it some tiny dimensionless number λ, maybe 10⁻¹¹ or smaller --- is so feeble that the dark matter particle (call it χ) never achieves equilibrium. It doesn\'t have enough \"social currency\" to join the party. But it\'s not completely isolated either: every once in a while, two bath particles collide with enough energy and the right quantum numbers that they produce a χ pair. Very rarely. But the bath contains an enormous number of particles, and the early Universe runs for a long time at high temperature, so those rare events accumulate.

### The Production Rate Logic

The key quantity is the production rate of χ per unit volume per unit time. It goes roughly as:

Γprod∼λ2 T5/MPl2Γprod​∼λ2T5/MPl2​

(for a gravitationally or feebly coupled case --- the exact form depends on the interaction type)

****Symbol definitions:****

- λ : the feeble coupling constant (dimensionless, extremely small)
- T : temperature of the thermal bath
- M_Pl : the Planck mass (\~2.4 × 10¹⁸ GeV), sets the gravitational coupling scale

****What this actually means:****

The rate has λ² because you need two vertices (one to put each χ in the final state). The T⁵ dependence comes from dimensional analysis --- in a renormalizable theory, the number density of bath particles scales as T³, and phase space adds more powers of T. The critical point: this rate decreases as T falls. The Universe is most productive at the highest temperatures, right after reheating at the end of inflation.

### Freeze-In vs. Freeze-Out: The Fundamental Difference

Freeze-****out**** (the WIMP story) starts with the dark matter in equilibrium. At high temperature, χ is abundant and constantly being created and destroyed. As the Universe cools and T drops below the χ mass, creation becomes energetically disfavored, but annihilation continues until the number density is so diluted that χ particles can\'t find each other anymore. They freeze out of equilibrium --- the reaction rate falls below the Hubble expansion rate.

Freeze-****in**** is the mirror image. χ starts with essentially zero abundance. The thermal bath dribbles χ particles into existence through rare reactions, but those χ particles never accumulate fast enough to build a number density sufficient for back-reactions (χχ → bath) to matter. The dark sector is always \"undersaturated.\" Production just runs open-loop, accumulating, until it shuts off.

Production shuts off for one of two reasons:

****1. The bath cools below the mass threshold.**** If χ has mass m, then bath reactions need center-of-mass energy above 2m to produce a pair. Once T drops well below m, the Boltzmann tail of the thermal distribution is exponentially suppressed --- exponentially fewer collisions have enough energy. Production turns off like a switch.

****2. The Hubble rate wins.**** Even for massless χ (as in the Maleknejad-Kopp paper), the production rate falls as T drops. Eventually the rate Γ_prod \< H (Hubble expansion rate), and the produced particles are simply diluted away faster than they\'re created.

### The \"IR Dominated\" vs. \"UV Dominated\" Distinction

This is where freeze-in gets genuinely subtle, and it\'s directly relevant to the GW paper.

****IR-dominated freeze-in:**** Production peaks at low temperatures --- near the mass threshold or at the reheating temperature if m \< T_RH. The final abundance depends mostly on late-time physics. This is the classic FIMP (Feebly Interacting Massive Particle) scenario. The coupling λ \~ 10⁻¹¹ that gives the right relic density is sometimes called the \"FIMP miracle\" --- analogous to the WIMP miracle but at the opposite end of coupling strength.

****UV-dominated freeze-in:**** Production peaks at the highest temperatures --- right after reheating. This happens when the operator mediating production is non-renormalizable (higher-dimensional), so the rate grows faster than H at high T. The relic density then depends sensitively on the reheating temperature T_RH. Gravitational production (including the GW mechanism) is UV-dominated, because gravity is mediated by a dimension-5 or higher operator suppressed by M_Pl.

This distinction matters enormously for observational predictions. IR freeze-in is insensitive to the reheating history. UV freeze-in (and GW-induced freeze-in) depends critically on what happened right at or just after inflation ended.

### Why χ Never Equilibrates: The Condition

Equilibrium requires the interaction rate Γ to exceed the Hubble rate H. In the radiation-dominated Universe:

H∼T2/MPlH∼T2/MPl​

The interaction rate for freeze-in goes as:

Γ∼λ2TΓ∼λ2T

(schematically, for a renormalizable coupling)

Equilibrium requires Γ \> H, i.e., roughly λ² T \> T²/M_Pl, which gives λ \> √(T/M_Pl). At T \~ 100 GeV (electroweak scale), this threshold coupling is about 10⁻⁸. Any coupling below that never equilibrates at the electroweak scale. For gravitational coupling, λ \~ T/M_Pl \~ 10⁻¹⁷ at 100 GeV --- utterly feeble, never equilibrates. Freeze-in is guaranteed.

### The Intel Analogy

Think of it like a processor with a very high-impedance debug port. The main silicon --- the thermal bath --- is running at full clock speed, enormous power, billions of transactions per second. The debug port taps a tiny current off that activity, so small it doesn\'t affect the main chip\'s operation at all. But over billions of cycles, the debug register slowly accumulates counts. Eventually the chip cools (or the clock slows), the tap current falls below detectable threshold, and whatever accumulated in the debug register is frozen there permanently. That\'s your dark matter relic density --- a quiet accumulation during the hot epoch, locked in forever once the Universe cooled below the production threshold.

### Connection to the Maleknejad-Kopp Paper

In the GW-induced case, the \"feeble interaction\" is literally quantum gravity --- the graviton-fermion vertex with coupling strength \~1/M_Pl. Normally for massless fermions even this doesn\'t work (conformal protection). What the GW background does is effectively act as the \"bath\" that provides the anisotropic perturbation needed to break conformal invariance and enable the vertex to contribute. The GW background is itself a relic of the early Universe, and it mediates freeze-in production of Weyl fermions. The fermion energy density accumulates until the GW background redshifts away or the relevant modes fall below the Hubble horizon --- then it freezes in. If the fermion later acquires mass, that frozen-in energy density becomes today\'s dark matter.

The beautifully strange thing about this version of freeze-in: the \"portal\" between the bath and the dark sector isn\'t a new particle or a new coupling constant --- it\'s the gravitational wave background itself, a macroscopic coherent structure of spacetime fluctuations, acting as a transducer converting metric energy into fermionic matter.

### Gravitational Wave Polarizations: The Geometry of Stretching Spacetime

#### Why Polarization Exists for Gravitational Waves

A gravitational wave is a propagating perturbation in the metric --- a ripple in the fabric of spacetime itself. When it passes through a region, it doesn\'t push or pull things in the direction it travels (no longitudinal component --- GWs are purely transverse, like light). Instead it distorts the geometry perpendicular to its direction of travel.

The question is: in that transverse 2D plane, how many independent ways can spacetime distort?

The answer is ****two independent polarization states**** --- exactly like the two independent polarization states of light. But the geometry of GW polarization looks very different from EM polarization.

### The Plus ( + ) Polarization

Imagine a ring of test particles floating freely in space, all in a plane perpendicular to the GW\'s direction of travel. A plus-polarized GW passing through does this:

- It ****stretches**** the ring along one axis (say, horizontal) while simultaneously ****squeezing**** it along the perpendicular axis (vertical)
- Half a cycle later, it reverses: squeezes horizontally, stretches vertically
- The pattern oscillates at the GW frequency

The distortion pattern looks like a ****+**** sign --- it\'s aligned with the coordinate axes you happen to be using. The name is literal.

The key physics: the metric perturbation for plus polarization stretches one axis by a factor (1 + h/2) and compresses the other by (1 − h/2), where h is the ****strain amplitude**** --- an incredibly tiny number, around 10⁻²¹ for LIGO\'s first detection. The two axes are stretched/compressed by equal and opposite amounts because GWs are traceless --- they don\'t change the overall volume of space, only its shape.

### The Cross ( × ) Polarization

Cross polarization does exactly the same stretching/squeezing, but ****rotated 45 degrees**** relative to the plus polarization. The distortion pattern looks like a ****×**** sign.

Critically: plus and cross are not the same physical state viewed from a different angle. They\'re genuinely independent degrees of freedom. A GW can be purely plus, purely cross, or any linear combination. This is directly analogous to linear polarization of light --- horizontal and vertical are independent, and you can have any linear combination.

The 45° relationship between + and × is a consequence of the spin-2 nature of the graviton. For spin-1 particles (photons), the two linear polarizations are 90° apart. For spin-2 particles, they\'re 45° apart. This is a deep geometrical fact: the symmetry group that relates the two polarizations for a spin-s particle involves rotations by 90°/s.

### Spin-2 and the 45° Rotation: The Deep Reason

This is worth pausing on because it connects to everything downstream.

A photon (spin-1) is unchanged if you rotate it 360°. Its polarization states are related by 90° rotations.

A graviton (spin-2) is unchanged if you rotate it 180°. Its polarization states are related by 45° rotations.

Mathematically, the GW perturbation h_ij is a ****symmetric, traceless, transverse rank-2 tensor**** in the plane perpendicular to propagation. A 2×2 symmetric traceless matrix has exactly 2 independent components:

hij=h+(100−1)+h×(0110)hij​=h+​(10​0−1​)+h×​(01​10​)

****Symbol definitions:****

- h_ij : the metric perturbation tensor (dimensionless strain)
- h\_+ : amplitude of plus polarization
- h\_× : amplitude of cross polarization
- The two matrices : the polarization tensors e\^+\_ij and e\^×\_ij

****What this actually means:**** The metric perturbation is just a 2×2 matrix, and those two basis matrices are the only two independent symmetric traceless 2×2 matrices. Plus and cross are the natural Cartesian basis for the space of GW polarizations --- just like x̂ and ŷ are the natural basis for 2D vectors. Everything else is a linear combination.

### Circular Polarization: Left and Right Handed GWs

Now here\'s where it gets beautiful. Just as with light, you can form a different basis by taking complex linear combinations of the two linear polarizations:

hR=12(h+−i h×)hR​=2​1​(h+​−ih×​)hL=12(h++i h×)hL​=2​1​(h+​+ih×​)

****Symbol definitions:****

- h_R : right-handed (right circular) polarization amplitude
- h_L : left-handed (left circular) polarization amplitude
- i : imaginary unit (the 90° phase shift between the two components)

****What this actually means:**** The factor of i represents a 90° phase shift in time between the + and × components. When plus is at maximum stretch, cross is at zero; when cross is at maximum, plus is at zero. The two linear polarizations oscillate a quarter-cycle out of phase with each other.

#### What Circular Polarization Looks Like Geometrically

Go back to your ring of test particles. For right-handed circular polarization:

- The ring doesn\'t alternately stretch horizontally then vertically
- Instead the ring continuously ****rotates its elliptical distortion pattern**** --- the long axis of the ellipse sweeps around in a circle
- For right-handed: the ellipse rotates clockwise when viewed from the direction the wave is traveling toward you
- For left-handed: the ellipse rotates counterclockwise

The shape of the distortion --- always an ellipse with the same eccentricity --- continuously precesses. It\'s like watching a spinning football pass through your ring of particles, with the spin axis aligned with the direction of propagation.

### Helicity: The Quantum Language

In quantum language, plus and cross polarizations are states of definite linear polarization --- they\'re superpositions of angular momentum eigenstates. Circular polarization states are ****helicity eigenstates**** --- states of definite angular momentum along the propagation direction.

A right-handed graviton carries helicity ****+2**** (two units of angular momentum along the propagation axis). A left-handed graviton carries helicity ****−2****. This is the spin-2 analog of how right-handed photons carry helicity +1 and left-handed photons carry helicity −1.

This is why circular polarization basis is natural for Weyl fermions. Weyl fermions are also helicity eigenstates --- they\'re intrinsically chiral. When a graviton interacts with a Weyl fermion, helicity must be conserved (or accounted for by the vertex structure). Working in the circular basis for gravitons means the selection rules are transparent: you can immediately see which graviton helicity couples to which fermion helicity at each vertex.

In the plus/cross basis, you\'d be working with superpositions of helicity eigenstates throughout, which obscures the selection rules and makes the algebra messier. This is exactly why Maleknejad and Kopp switch to circular polarization --- it\'s the natural language for the fermion physics.

### Chirality of a GW Background

A monochromatic plane wave propagating in one direction can be purely right-handed, purely left-handed, or linearly polarized (equal mix of both). A ****stochastic**** GW background --- random waves coming from all directions --- is called ****unpolarized**** if, on average, it has equal power in left and right handed modes.

A ****chiral**** GWB has unequal power: more right-handed than left-handed power (or vice versa). This is characterized by the ****Stokes V parameter**** --- the GW analog of circular polarization fraction in optics. A chiral GWB can be generated by:

- Inflationary models with gauge fields (axion inflation, Chromo-Natural inflation --- Maleknejad\'s own earlier work)
- Parity-violating physics during inflation
- Helical magnetic fields in the primordial plasma

****Why chirality matters for the dark matter paper:****

For an unpolarized background, left and right contributions average out. Production of left-handed Weyl fermions from left-handed gravitons equals production of right-handed anti-fermions from right-handed gravitons --- no net asymmetry. Equal numbers of matter and antimatter.

For a chiral background, one helicity dominates. Now the production is asymmetric --- more fermions than antifermions (or vice versa). This is the gravitational anomaly channel that Maleknejad and Kopp flag but don\'t fully calculate. The same mechanism that makes dark matter could simultaneously seed a dark sector matter-antimatter asymmetry --- potentially linked to baryogenesis through some shared parity-violation at high energy. It\'s one of the most tantalizing open threads in the paper.
