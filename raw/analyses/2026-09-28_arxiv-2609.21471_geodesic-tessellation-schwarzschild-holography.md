## 1. Punchy Title & One-Sentence Hook

**The White-Furnace Test for a Black Hole: Counting Geodesics Around Schwarzschild**

A glowing sphere that emits geodesics evenly in every direction fills the space around a Schwarzschild black hole so uniformly that counting crossings measures the area of *any* surface. That result is correct, and I checked it to 0.01%. It is also, in essence, Santaló's 1952 formula. The step from there to "we realize Bekenstein–Hawking entropy and ER=EPR" is made by assigning 1/4G to each thread by hand.

## 2. Big-Picture Context

**Paper Type & Stakes:** a theoretical-construction paper in holography and integral geometry. It tries to carry the "geodesics tessellate the bulk" toolkit that underpins kinematic space and the PEE tensor-network models out of vacuum AdS into a non-homogeneous, asymptotically flat spacetime. If that worked in a way that constrained physics, it would be one concrete foothold for holography beyond AdS.

The background, briefly, since this cluster is tier 2 for you. The Ryu–Takayanagi (RT) formula says a boundary region's entanglement entropy equals the area of a minimal bulk surface divided by 4G. Czech, Lamprou, McCandlish and Sully (2015) recast RT in the language of *integral geometry*: on a hyperbolic slice of AdS you can compute a curve's length by counting how many geodesics cross it, weighted by a "kinematic measure" on the space of geodesics (the Crofton formula). In pure AdS that measure comes for free from the isometry group, because every point and direction looks like every other one. The Wen group (Southeast University; this paper's senior author) then built the **PEE-thread** program on top of this. The measure is read off the *partial entanglement entropy* of the boundary CFT vacuum, the threads are the kinematic-space geodesics, and tensor networks whose bonds run along the threads reproduce RT (refs. [15, 21, 28, 29]).

The open problem is that everything leans on AdS homogeneity. A Schwarzschild spatial slice has only SO(3) symmetry. Points at different radii are genuinely different, so no group hands you a canonical measure on its geodesics. The paper's route around this is to *source* geodesics from an outer sphere with the natural cosine-weighted boundary measure, follow them inward, and show that the resulting "geodesic gas" is still uniform and isotropic at every interior point.

**Prior Belief Check:** the **geometric result aligns with classical mathematics rather than overturning anything**. For a compact Riemannian region with boundary in which no geodesic is trapped, *Santaló's formula* (1952; a standard tool in inverse problems and boundary rigidity, e.g. Croke, Guillarmou) says exactly this: the Liouville measure on unit tangent vectors equals the boundary's cosine-weighted inward measure transported along the geodesic flow. The Crofton formula on non-homogeneous Riemannian and Finsler spaces via the symplectic structure of geodesic space is Álvarez Paiva's work, which the paper does cite [30–32] and explicitly positions itself against in §6. An integral geometer would find the geometric claims unsurprising. The genuinely specific content is the Schwarzschild bookkeeping, meaning the completeness of a one-sided source (Prop. 2.2) and the Einstein–Rosen bridge sector split. The **holographic results** (Bekenstein–Hawking entropy, RT, the ER=EPR count) are **confirmatory by construction**, not surprising. Two further points: bit threads (Freedman–Headrick) already give an RT-like min-cut/max-flow statement on any Riemannian manifold, and, as I recall, Czech et al. (2015) already discussed kinematic space on non-vacuum AdS backgrounds such as conical defects and BTZ. So "unclear whether this extends beyond vacuum AdS" slightly understates the existing literature.

**Replication & Convergence Note:** single-group work. Every PEE-thread paper it builds on shares authors with it ([15, 21, 25, 26, 28, 29]), and v1 is ten days old with no independent follow-up. My Monte Carlo in §4.6 independently checks the paper's central geometric identity (it holds), but that's not replication in the literature sense. Independent confirmation that *matters* would come from a boundary-theory side: a quantum state defined on the sphere at infinity whose partial-entanglement structure *produces* this thread measure, rather than having the measure copied from bulk geometry.

## 3. Necessary Background Crash-Course

### 3.1 The Crofton formula: area by counting line hits

Scatter lines "uniformly" through space, meaning uniform in position *and* direction. The expected number of hits on a surface is then proportional to its area. That is Buffon's needle generalized, and it's the whole Crofton formula. In three dimensions, with the normalization the paper uses, integrating the cosine-weighted crossing measure over directions gives a universal factor of π per unit area.

**Analogy:** Monte Carlo area estimation with ray casting. Fire a huge number of rays whose origins and directions are uniformly distributed, count how many intersect a mesh, and the hit count estimates the mesh's surface area. That works for any shape, because uniform rays have no preferred place or direction to over- or under-sample.

**Breaks when:** in a renderer, "uniform rays" is easy because space is flat and every straight line is equally available. In curved space the geodesics bend, bunch and spread, so "uniformly distributed geodesics" is not automatically defined. That is the whole problem the paper addresses.

### 3.2 The cosine-weighted boundary measure and why it propagates uniformly (Liouville / étendue)

This analogy has real internal structure (source measure, transport and a completeness condition), so it gets the full split.

**Concept:** Put a measure on geodesics crossing a surface that is uniform over surface area and uniform over direction, but weighted by the cosine of the angle to the surface normal (the paper's intrinsic measure, Eq. 3). Liouville's theorem, which says phase-space volume is conserved under Hamiltonian flow, implies that this cosine-weighted *flux* measure is conserved as the geodesics travel. At any later surface the crossing measure again has the same cosine-weighted, uniform form. That holds only at points and directions actually reached from the source, and only if each geodesic is counted once (the paper's "reachability" and "finite multiplicity" conditions). The mathematical identity is Santaló's formula.

**Vehicle:** In physically based rendering, a *Lambertian* emitter radiates with intensity proportional to the cosine of the angle from its normal, which makes its *radiance* (brightness per unit area per unit solid angle) the same in every direction. Radiance is conserved along a ray in a lossless medium, even a graded-index medium in which rays bend (étendue conservation). Renderer developers use the **white-furnace test**: put the camera inside a closed, uniformly glowing Lambertian enclosure, and every pixel must come out the same brightness in every direction. Any deviation means the integrator is broken. The test fails in one legitimate way: if some rays inside are trapped, for example circulating forever in a fiber loop, they never connect to the emitter and render dark.

**Analogy:** Outer sphere S₊ ↔ Lambertian emitter. Cosine-weighted intrinsic measure ↔ Lambertian emission law. Transport of the crossing measure along geodesics ↔ conservation of radiance along bent rays. Line density ρ_ℓ = 1 everywhere ↔ a uniform furnace image. The reachability condition ↔ "no trapped rays". The multiplicity quotient ↔ not double-counting a ray that leaves and re-enters the emitter surface. Crofton reconstruction ↔ "measure anything's area by counting how much radiance lands on it."

**Breaks when:** radiance is a *local* field. At a point, a ray knows its direction and brightness but not where it started or where it will end. The paper's tensor-network layer (§4.5) uses exactly the information the furnace picture throws away: *which endpoint is paired with which*. Entanglement entropy counts threads whose two endpoints sit on opposite sides of a partition, which is a global, endpoint-topology fact. A uniform local radiance field has no slot for it, and §4.6 shows this is exactly where the paper's factorized-model argument slips.

### 3.3 Geodesics on the spatial Schwarzschild slice and the Einstein–Rosen bridge

The paper works on the time-symmetric t = 0 slice. That is a 3D Riemannian space, and its geodesics are **spatial curves, not light rays**. By spherical symmetry each geodesic lies in a plane through the center and has a conserved angular momentum J (units of length). J plays the role of an impact parameter: the geodesic's minimum areal radius is r = J. So geodesics with J larger than the horizon radius r_h turn around outside the horizon, and geodesics with J below r_h go all the way in. On the maximally extended slice (the Einstein–Rosen bridge) the J < r_h ones pass through the throat into the second asymptotic region (paper Fig. 2, a Flamm-paraboloid embedding with crossing geodesics in blue and returning ones in orange). The throat is **totally geodesic**: it's the fixed set of the left–right reflection symmetry, so its extrinsic curvature vanishes.

**Analogy:** a bottleneck link between two datacenters. Only flows with a small enough "angular momentum" (steep enough approach) make it through the tunnel. Everything else bounces off the funnel wall and returns to the side it came from, and the tunnel's capacity is fixed by its cross-section.

**Breaks when:** a network tunnel's capacity is a separately engineered number. Here the "capacity" (the measure of crossing geodesics) *is* the throat area by the Crofton identity, so there's no independent quantity to compare it to. That's worth keeping in mind when §4.5 calls this count "entropy".

### 3.4 PEE tensor networks: counting cables vs. finding the minimum cut

**Concept:** Put a qudit pair on each thread and contract. In the **factorized** model every thread ends up as one EPR pair shared between its two endpoints, so a boundary region A's entropy is exactly the number of threads with one end in A and the other outside. In the **random** model, random tensors sit at the sites and entropy is set by the minimum number of thread-bonds any surface separating A from its complement must cut (Hayden et al. 2016, at large bond dimension). With each thread weighted 1/4G, the Crofton identity turns "bonds cut" into "area/4G".

**Vehicle:** A cluster interconnect. The factorized model is point-to-point cabling: the traffic A can exchange with the rest is just the number of cables with one end in A. The random model is bisection bandwidth: the capacity between A and the rest is the size of the smallest set of cables whose removal disconnects them.

**Analogy:** Thread ↔ cable. The 1/4G per thread ↔ per-cable bandwidth. Factorized entropy ↔ count of A-to-outside cables. Random-network entropy ↔ minimum bisection cut. RT surface ↔ the minimum cut plane.

**Breaks when:** in the interconnect, per-cable bandwidth is a measured spec. Here 1/4G per thread is *chosen* so the answer matches RT (paper §5.1: "to match the RT formula exactly, one assigns an entanglement 1/(4G) to each EPR pair"). Nothing in the network determines it.

**Central analogy for this paper:** *white-furnace test — Lambertian sphere lighting curved space*

## 4. Core Technical Explanation

### 4.1 The shell and its chords (§2.1)

The authors take an annular shell between an inner sphere S₋ and an outer sphere S₊, both outside the horizon, with a general radial metric that covers Schwarzschild. They cut every geodesic down to its maximal segment inside the shell (a "shell chord") and classify chords by J (Prop. 2.1):

- **J > R₊:** never enters the shell.
- **R₋ ≤ J < R₊ (outer–outer / returning):** the radial minimum r = J lies inside the shell, so the chord starts and ends on S₊.
- **J < R₋ (inner–outer):** no turning point in the shell, so the chord runs monotonically from S₊ to S₋.

Two observations do real work. First, the radius along a geodesic has only *minima*, never interior maxima (Eq. 11), so no chord can start and end on S₋. **Every chord touches S₊**, which is why a single outer source is complete (Prop. 2.2). Second, chords have finite length (non-trapping). Sourcing from S₊ with the cosine-weighted measure, the inner–outer chords are hit once and the returning chords twice, so the authors divide by the source multiplicity N₊ (Eq. 21). "Hit twice" means the source generates a returning chord once from each end.

**Worked-equation box 1 — the source measure in J-space (paper Eqs. 25–26, 30):**

$$ d\Gamma_+^{(J)} = 8\pi^2 \, J \, dJ $$

**Symbol definitions:**
$d\Gamma_+^{(J)}$ : source weight for geodesics with angular momentum between J and J + dJ, integrated over launch point and azimuth (area × steradian)
$J$ : conserved angular momentum = minimum areal radius reached (length)
$8\pi^2$ : 4π (launch positions over the unit sphere) × 2π (azimuth around the radial direction)

**What this actually means:** the Lambertian source puts weight on geodesics in proportion to J. That's the same reason a random line through a disk is more likely to pass at a large impact parameter than a small one, since there's more circumference out there. Integrating J from 0 to R₊ gives 4π²R₊², which is exactly π × (area of S₊ = 4πR₊²). That's the Crofton normalization. **Micro-example:** take R₊ = 10 r_h. The fraction of source weight carried by geodesics that reach the horizon (J < r_h) is (r_h/R₊)² = **1%**. The other 99% turn around outside. Yet that 1% crossing sector, 4π²r_h², is exactly π × horizon area (paper Eq. 38). The horizon's area is carried by a small, specific slice of the gas.

### 4.2 Why every interior surface is reconstructed (§2.2–2.3)

For an intermediate sphere S_r, a returning chord with J < r crosses S_r twice and was sourced twice, so the ratio of crossings to sourcings is 1. An inner–outer chord gives 1/1. Chords with J > r never reach S_r. So the induced crossing measure on S_r is the same cosine-weighted, uniform form (Eq. 29), and it integrates to π × 4πr². Because that holds for *every* sphere and the directions at each point are fully covered, the line density at every point in every direction must be 1 (Eq. 33). That is the furnace coming out uniform. Uniform line density then gives Crofton for *any* surface Σ (Eq. 34). Notably, none of this depends on the specific form of the radial metric function. It uses only which sectors return and which cross, which is a topological fact.

### 4.3 Through the throat (§3)

On the Einstein–Rosen bridge, the areal radius r is no longer monotone in proper distance: it hits its minimum r_h at the throat. Geodesics launched from the right-hand sphere split cleanly. For **J < r_h**, the geodesic crosses the throat once and runs out into the left universe. For **r_h < J < R₊**, it returns to the right side. So a right-side-only source reconstructs the throat exactly (every crossing geodesic hits it once, total measure π A_H), but on any sphere on the *left* side it supplies only 4π²r_h² instead of 4π²r², and Crofton fails there. The fix is a second source on the left sphere. The left and right gases **share** the crossing sector and each keeps a private returning sector. The paper frames this as "the throat is a distinguished surface in Crofton geometry", which is accurate and cleanly stated.

### 4.4 The general transport theorem (§4, Appendix A)

The Hamiltonian section proves, for any smooth Riemannian manifold, what the furnace picture assumes:

1. The symplectic form on phase space is invariant under the geodesic flow, so the Liouville volume is too (Eqs. 41–53).
2. Restricting to unit speed and contracting out the flow direction gives a 4-form, the "oriented transverse measure" β (Eq. 64), whose pullback to a crossing surface is the cosine-weighted crossing density (Eq. 65).
3. **Theorem A.1:** β is preserved even under the *variable-time* map that carries each point on one crossing section to where its geodesic hits another. The key step (Eq. 98) is that the extra term from differing travel times is proportional to the flow vector, and β annihilates the flow vector.

All three steps are correct, and the proof is clean. They are also standard: this is the argument that the transverse Liouville form descends to the space of geodesics, and the paper's own §6 is candid that "what is new here is not the local symplectic machinery but its assembly". The "assembly" (boundary source + transport + multiplicity quotient + reachability) is, in the non-trapping case, precisely the content of **Santaló's formula**. The paper doesn't cite it by name, though it does cite Santaló's integral-geometry book [8].

### 4.5 The holographic layer (§5)

The authors put the PEE tensor network on the thread gas and read off entropies:

- **Exterior (S₋ pushed to the horizon, S₊ to infinity):** the horizon is totally geodesic, and the threads joining S₊ to the horizon are the crossing sector, each crossing the horizon once. So S(S₊) = N(S₊, horizon)/4G = horizon area / 4G (Eq. 83), which they call the Bekenstein–Hawking entropy.
- **Subregions A of S₊:** S(A) = Area(γ_A)/4G (Eq. 84). For the factorized model the argument (§5.1) is that because γ_A is totally geodesic, "every geodesic joining A to Ā crosses it exactly once and no other geodesic meets it". For general regions they fall back on the random tensor network's minimal cut.
- **Einstein–Rosen bridge:** the left and right boundaries are joined by exactly the crossing-sector threads, each an EPR pair, so the left–right entropy is again area/4G (Eq. 85). They call this a concrete realization of ER=EPR.

The paper is honest in the body. It says the Schwarzschild duality "is restricted to the PEE tensor network model we have constructed, and it is not clear what the theory on the sphere S₊ at infinity actually is", and that its "interpretation as a gravitational dual remains a hypothesis". The abstract ("we can concretely realize the Bekenstein–Hawking entropy, the Ryu–Takayanagi formula and the ER=EPR proposal") reads considerably stronger than that.

The paper's Fig. 4 is worth looking at here. It shows three 2D reconstruction regions, with the dashed geodesic γ_A (homologous to A) drawn as though every red A-to-Ā thread crosses it exactly once. That picture is correct in hyperbolic space. It is not correct in the Schwarzschild geometry, as the next subsection shows.

### 4.6 Analyst numerical checks

I integrated the planar spatial-geodesic equation for the Schwarzschild slice (units r_h = 1) and ran a Monte Carlo over 2 million source samples drawn from the paper's own measure (Eq. 25, with the 1/N₊ quotient). Then I counted crossings of a test surface.

**(a) The central Crofton claim holds.** Test surface: the equatorial disk from the horizon out to R₊ = 10, plus the northern half of the horizon. That surface is γ_A for A = the northern hemisphere of S₊, and it's totally geodesic piecewise by reflection symmetry.

| Quantity | Value |
|---|---|
| π × Area(γ_A), exact quadrature | 1123.42 |
| Monte Carlo crossing count | 1123.55 (0.01% agreement) |
| Crossing-sector measure vs. π A_H = 39.48 | 39.74 (MC noise ~0.7%) |

The paper's geometry is right. The "furnace" is uniform.

**(b) The factorized-model argument fails.** The claim "a totally geodesic γ_A is crossed exactly once by every A-to-Ā thread and by no other thread" is a hyperbolic-space fact (no conjugate points). On the Schwarzschild slice, spatial geodesics bend *toward* the hole. A returning geodesic sweeps an in-plane angle Δφ larger than π:

**Worked-equation box 2 — sweep angle of a returning spatial geodesic:**

$$ \Delta\varphi(J) = 2\int_J^{\infty} \frac{J \, dr}{r^2 \sqrt{1 - r_h/r}\,\sqrt{1 - J^2/r^2}} $$

**Symbol definitions:**
$\Delta\varphi$ : total in-plane angle swept between entering and leaving (radians); flat space gives exactly π (a straight line)
$J$ : angular momentum = closest-approach areal radius
$r_h$ : horizon radius; the factor $1/\sqrt{1-r_h/r}$ is the stretched radial metric of the slice
$r$ : areal radius along the geodesic

**What this actually means (computed values, r_h = 1):**

| J / r_h | 100 | 10 | 5 | 2 | 1.5 | 1.1 | 1.01 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Δφ / π | 1.003 | 1.034 | 1.072 | 1.234 | 1.381 | 1.895 | 2.853 |

At large J the excess is about r_h/J. Near the critical value J → r_h it diverges logarithmically, and the geodesic winds around the throat. Any sweep beyond π means that for suitable orientations the geodesic crosses a plane through the center **twice**, starting and ending in the *same* hemisphere. Think of a cable that leaves partition A, crosses the cut plane, and comes back into A. It counts toward the cut plane's crossings (area) but carries no A-to-outside traffic (entropy). There's a second effect from the crossing sector: a thread from Ā can cross the disk and end on the *northern* half of the horizon, crossing γ_A twice while connecting two parts of the complement.

**Result — factorized-model entropy vs. Area(γ_A)/4G for A = hemisphere:**

| R₊ / r_h | Factorized S(A) ÷ [Area(γ_A)/4G] | Shortfall in units of horizon area | Split: returning / horizon sector |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3 | 0.747 | 0.93 A_H | 11.5% / 13.9% |
| 10 | 0.941 | 1.66 A_H | 3.8% / 2.0% |
| 100 | 0.998 | 4.19 A_H | 0.14% / 0.03% |

The relative error shrinks as the cutoff sphere moves out, but the **absolute** shortfall *grows* and is always comparable to or larger than the Bekenstein–Hawking entropy itself. That's the very quantity the model is advertised as realizing. The random-tensor-network version is unaffected, since a min cut counts every bond crossing including repeats, so the paper's fallback survives. The explicit factorized-model claim, and the statement that Eq. 84 follows "from the previous subsection" by the totally-geodesic argument, does not survive. (Caveat: I take the disk plus half-horizon to be the minimal surface by symmetry. If a smaller one existed, the factorized count would fall even further below RT, since every connecting thread must cross *any* separating surface at least once.)

**(c) What "RT on S₊" implies.** The equatorial disk's area is about πR₊², growing with the boundary region's own area rather than its perimeter. At R₊ = 100 r_h the hemisphere's RT entropy is **2,526 ×** the Bekenstein–Hawking entropy. So the putative boundary theory on S₊ has **volume-law** entanglement, as Li & Takayanagi (2011, the paper's ref. [34]) found for flat-space holography. The paper doesn't discuss this.

### Assumption Audit

> **Watch:** Reader likely assumes the "threads" are light rays, so the photon sphere at 3Gm should trap some of them. The paper actually uses **spatial geodesics of the t = 0 slice**, a purely Riemannian problem with no photon sphere. Non-trapping holds because the areal radius along a spatial geodesic has only minima (Eq. 11), apart from the measure-zero set winding onto the throat.

> **Watch:** Reader likely assumes Bekenstein–Hawking entropy is *derived*. The paper actually **assigns** 1/4G to each thread to match RT (§5.1), and the measure itself is the bulk geometric measure (§5: "the measure for the PEE threads is determined by the intrinsic measure of the boundary surface"). What's derived is that the crossing sector's measure equals π × horizon area, which is a statement about area. "Entropy = area/4G" is then an input, not an output.

> **Watch:** Reader likely assumes, as in AdS PEE work, that the thread measure encodes a boundary quantum state. In AdS the measure is read off the CFT's partial-entanglement structure, which is independent physical input. Here there is no boundary state at all: geometry fixes the measure, the measure fixes the network, and the network returns the geometry's areas. The loop closes on itself.

> **Watch:** Reader likely assumes "totally geodesic ⇒ each connecting thread crosses once" is safe, since it's how Fig. 4 is drawn. It isn't on this background. Geodesic bending toward the hole (sweep angles above π, table above) produces repeat crossings, and the factorized entropy falls short of Area/4G by roughly one to several horizon areas.

## 5. What's Genuinely New or Clever

1. **The completeness bookkeeping for a one-sided source.** Observing that radius along a spatial geodesic has only minima, so every shell chord touches the *outer* sphere, and pairing that with the returning-chord multiplicity quotient, is a neat, economical argument. It's what lets a single outer "furnace wall" suffice without an inner emitter. *New to the reader:* likely yes. *New to the field:* the specific Schwarzschild application yes. The general mechanism is Santaló's formula applied to a non-trapping region whose inner boundary never emits.

2. **The sector split on the Einstein–Rosen bridge.** Stating crisply that a one-sided source reconstructs exactly up to the throat, that the throat's area *is* the measure of the crossing sector, and that bilateral sources sharing that sector are needed to reconstruct the far side, is a clean structural result. It is the paper's most quotable contribution. Its information content is still "the throat is a minimal, totally geodesic surface that each crossing geodesic hits once", but the framing is useful.

What is *not* new, and the paper largely says so in §6, is the symplectic transport machinery of §4.

### Predictive Content Check

1. **Falsifiable handle:** **none empirical, and the paper relabels rather than predicts.** Every holographic output is "area/4G" with the 1/4G inserted by hand, so no observable or scale could come out differently. The nearest *internal* falsifiable claims are two:
   - The factorized model reproduces RT for totally-geodesic γ_A. I tested this, and **it comes out false on this background** (§4.6b).
   - The boundary theory on S₊ has RT-type entanglement. Taken at face value, this predicts a volume law (hemisphere entropy ≈ 2,500 × S_BH at R₊ = 100 r_h). That is a concrete and quite restrictive claim about any would-be dual, and the paper leaves it unexamined.

2. **Formalism load (fires, given the adversarial posture and heavy formal apparatus):** the §4 Hamiltonian/transport machinery is **load-bearing but classical**. It does real work (it proves the Crofton claim for arbitrary surfaces), yet it reproduces a known theorem. The §5 tensor-network formalism is **decorative with respect to predictions**. Delete it and every reported number stays the same (area, then divided by the 4G that was put in), because the network's entropy is defined to equal the thread count. The one place the network formalism *could* have produced a discriminating statement, the factorized model, gives a result that disagrees with the one claimed.

## 6. Limitations & Open Questions

**L1. The geometric core is essentially Santaló's formula, unnamed.** The boundary-sourced, cosine-weighted, flow-transported measure on a non-trapping region reproducing the Liouville measure, and therefore Crofton, is standard integral geometry and inverse-problems material. **(B) Contested** — the authors argue explicitly (§6) that the *assembly* and the explicit reachability/multiplicity conditions are the novelty; whether that clears the bar is a judgment reasonable people could make differently. **(broader literature + analyst inference)**

**L2. The factorized-model RT argument is wrong on Schwarzschild.** Spatial geodesics sweep more than π, so totally-geodesic surfaces are crossed twice by some same-side threads. The factorized entropy falls short of Area/4G by about 6% at R₊ = 10 r_h, and by an absolute amount of one to several horizon areas. **(C) Speculative** — my own calculation, not in the paper or literature; the geometric input (sweep angle above π) is textbook, and the Monte Carlo reproduces the paper's own Crofton identity to 0.01%, which validates the harness, but a specialist should re-check the choice of γ_A. **(analyst inference)**

**L3. Entropy = area/4G is inserted, and there is no boundary theory.** The 1/4G per thread is chosen to match RT, and the thread measure is copied from bulk geometry rather than derived from any boundary state. **(A) Consensus** — the paper itself says the gravitational-dual interpretation "remains a hypothesis" and "it is not clear what the theory on the sphere S₊ at infinity actually is." **(paper §5, §5.2)**

**L4. Static, time-symmetric slices only.** No covariant (HRT-type) version exists yet, and no Lorentzian geodesic measure. **(A) Consensus** — named as the first open direction by the authors. **(paper §6)**

**L5. The implied boundary dual has volume-law entanglement.** Minimal surfaces anchored on a large sphere in asymptotically flat space have area that scales with the boundary region's area, so any dual on S₊ would be highly non-local. **(B) Contested** — flat-space holography literature has found this before and argued over what it means; the paper cites that work [34] but doesn't engage the scaling. **(broader literature)**

**L6. The continuum random-tensor-network step is formal.** The min-cut theorem is proven for finite graphs at large bond dimension. Mapping "each thread carries 1/4G" onto "1/4G → ∞ is the large-bond-dimension limit" over a continuum of threads meeting at every point is an identification, not a derivation, and subleading corrections are unaddressed. **(B) Contested** — continuum tensor networks are an active area where rigor varies by construction. **(broader literature + analyst inference)**

**L7. The "matter need not source threads" claim (§6) isn't supported by anything computed.** Nothing in the construction uses Einstein's equations. Any non-trapping Riemannian metric, physical or not, admits the same tessellation, so the construction can't distinguish matter-supported geometries from arbitrary ones. **(C) Speculative** — this is my reading of the logical structure. **(analyst inference)**

**L8. Positioning against prior generalizations is thin.** Bit threads already give RT-type flow statements on arbitrary Riemannian manifolds, and kinematic space on non-vacuum AdS (conical defect, BTZ) was treated in the original integral-geometry paper. The introduction's "it has so far been unclear" framing undersells both. v1 also has unresolved "[?]" citations. **(B) Contested** — how much these prior results overlap with a boundary-sourced construction is arguable. My recollection of the conical-defect/BTZ treatment in Czech et al. (2015) should be verified. **(broader literature)**

## 7. Detailed Summary & Explanation

**Summary.** The paper asks whether the "geodesic tessellation" behind kinematic space and PEE tensor networks, where geodesics fill space evenly enough that counting crossings measures area, survives outside vacuum AdS. Working on the static spatial slice of a Schwarzschild black hole, the authors launch geodesics inward from an outer sphere. Each point gets equal weight and each direction is weighted by the cosine of its angle to the normal. Geodesics that return to the sphere are counted once, not twice.

They show the resulting geodesic gas is perfectly uniform and isotropic everywhere between the sphere and the horizon. So the number of crossings of *any* surface, divided by π, equals its area: the Crofton formula holds. The argument uses only which geodesics return and which pass through, not the detailed metric, and a general Hamiltonian section proves the underlying flux conservation for any smooth Riemannian manifold. Only two global conditions are needed: every point and direction must be reachable from the source, and each geodesic must hit the source finitely often.

On the full Einstein–Rosen bridge, geodesics with angular momentum below the horizon radius pass through the throat and those above it turn back. A one-sided source therefore reconstructs areas exactly up to the throat, whose area equals the measure of the crossing geodesics, and fails beyond it. Sources on both sides, sharing the crossing sector, are needed.

Finally, the authors place entangled pairs along the geodesics to form a tensor network, weight each thread by one over 4G, and obtain three results: the sphere at infinity carries entropy equal to the horizon area over 4G; boundary subregions obey an RT formula; and the two sides of the bridge share exactly the Bekenstein–Hawking entropy, which they present as a realization of ER=EPR.

**Explanation of the framing.** I split the verdict in two because the paper has two layers of very different strength.

- **Geometric layer.** It is correct and I reproduced it numerically. It is also close to a classical theorem (Santaló's formula), so its value lies in the Schwarzschild-specific bookkeeping, meaning the one-sided completeness argument and the bridge sector split, rather than in the transport theorem.
- **Holographic layer.** It produces no number the geometry didn't already contain, because the entropy per thread is set to make entropy equal area. Where the network formalism *does* make an independent claim (the factorized model on a totally geodesic surface), my calculation says it's off by an amount comparable to the black hole's own entropy. The reason is that Schwarzschild bends geodesics enough for them to recross planes, which hyperbolic space never does.
- **The takeaway.** A clean result in integral geometry has been re-described as holography. The honest caveats in the body are right, and the abstract oversells.

I didn't materially adapt the nine-section structure. The only non-standard element is §4.6, my own numerical check, labeled as analyst work throughout.

**Where I'm least confident in this analysis:** two places. The first is the §4.6b factorized-model shortfall. It rests on my choice of the equatorial disk plus the northern half-horizon as γ_A, and on my reading of what "threads connecting A and Ā ∪ γ_Σ" means in the paper's Fig. 4 convention for a region that also borders the horizon. The geodesic-sweep fact is solid, but the exact percentages depend on that interpretation. The second is the Santaló identification. I'm confident the mathematical content matches for non-trapping regions, but an integral geometer might point to a real technical difference (for example the multi-component chord bookkeeping, or the inner boundary that never emits) that makes the paper's version a modest generalization rather than a restatement.

## 8. Three Crystallized Takeaways

1. **A glowing sphere lights curved space evenly.** Emit geodesics from a sphere around a black hole with Lambertian (cosine) weighting, and the gas stays perfectly uniform inside. So counting crossings measures any surface's area. That is correct, and it's the white-furnace test of computer graphics carried out in curved space. The mathematicians' version is 70 years old.

2. **The black hole's horizon is carried by a thin slice of the gas.** With the sphere at ten horizon radii, only 1% of the emitted geodesics reach the horizon, yet that 1% exactly encodes its area. On the full wormhole geometry, a source on one side can "see" only up to the throat, and the far side needs its own source.

3. **Calling area "entropy" doesn't make it entropy.** The paper gets Bekenstein–Hawking, RT and ER=EPR by assigning one over 4G to each geodesic. Where its simplest network model makes an independent claim, the bending of geodesics around the hole makes it miss by about one horizon's worth of entropy.

## 9. Shorter Summary

Holography's best-understood toy models rest on a geometric fact about anti-de Sitter space: its geodesics can be spread so evenly that counting how many cross a surface gives that surface's area. This paper asks whether that survives around an ordinary Schwarzschild black hole, where space is no longer the same at every point.

The authors launch geodesics inward from a large sphere, spread evenly over its surface and weighted toward the inward direction by the same cosine law a matte glowing surface follows. They show the resulting "geodesic gas" is perfectly uniform in position and direction everywhere down to the horizon. So crossing counts reproduce the area of any surface, and a general proof extends this to any curved space in which no geodesic gets trapped. On the full wormhole geometry joining two universes, geodesics aimed steeply enough pass through the throat. Their count equals the throat's area, but a source on one side can't reconstruct the other side.

I reproduced the central geometric claim numerically to within 0.01%. It is sound, but it's essentially a classical 1952 result from integral geometry (Santaló's formula), which the paper doesn't name.

The paper then treats each geodesic as an entangled pair and assigns it one-quarter of an inverse Newton's constant. That makes entropy equal area divided by 4G, and it recovers the black hole's Bekenstein–Hawking entropy, the Ryu–Takayanagi formula and a picture of the wormhole as entanglement. Because that factor is chosen rather than derived, and no theory on the outer sphere is identified, these results restate the geometry rather than predict anything. The authors acknowledge this in the body but not in the abstract.

Where the simplest version of their network does make an independent claim, it fails. Geodesics bend around the black hole enough to cross a dividing plane twice, so the predicted entanglement falls short by an amount comparable to the black hole's own entropy.

The result is a clean geometric tool relabeled as holography. The genuine open step is finding a boundary quantum system that produces this geodesic measure on its own.
